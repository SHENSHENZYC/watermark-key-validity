## A. Evaluation statistics and experimental design

**C01 — Inference on winners for LLM leaderboards.** A leaderboard ranks K models on n shared items and reports the top model's score and the top-k gaps. Is the reported winner's score biased upward by the selection of the maximum (winner's curse), and do selective-inference corrections (conditional and hybrid intervals of Andrews, Kitagawa and McCloskey, "Inference on winners"; Fithian–Sun–Taylor selective inference) give valid and usefully tight intervals on public per-item leaderboard data (Open LLM Leaderboard per-sample details, HELM, arena snapshots)? Comparator: naive Wald or bootstrap intervals; Bonferroni. Failure mechanism: naive intervals ignore that the winner was chosen by the same data. Who acts differently: leaderboard maintainers (report debiased winners), model selectors.

**C02 — Two-level resampling design for paired model comparisons.** With a budget of B generations, how should an evaluator split it between more prompts and more decoding samples per prompt to minimise the variance of a paired accuracy or win-rate difference, and which resampling scheme (item bootstrap, nested bootstrap, exact two-level permutation) holds its Type I error at small n? Comparator: single-sample evaluations with a CLT interval (Miller 2024); seed-variance studies (Madaan et al. 2024); "don't use the CLT" (Bowyer et al. 2025). Failure mechanism: item difficulty and decoding noise are confounded in one-sample evaluations; item clustering breaks the CLT at small n. Who acts: anyone designing an evaluation budget.

**C03 — Anytime-valid sequential LLM evaluation.** Can confidence sequences and e-processes for paired differences and win rates (betting confidence sequences of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas; Howard et al.) let an evaluator stop an evaluation early with valid error control, and how much does that save against fixed-n evaluation on real benchmark streams under item heterogeneity and ordering effects? Comparator: fixed-n tests; naive peeking at running accuracy. Failure mechanism: peeking inflates error; fixed-n wastes budget. Data: public per-item results replayed in random order; optional small-model runs.

**C14 — Factorial designs for prompt-format sensitivity.** Do orthogonal-array or fractional-factorial designs over prompt-format factors (separators, casing, option labels, instruction phrasing, few-shot count) estimate main effects and interactions on accuracy more efficiently than one-factor-at-a-time or random template sampling, with a variance-component model giving a sensitivity decomposition? Comparator: FormatSpread (Sclar et al. 2023); multi-prompt evaluation (Mizrahi et al. 2024); PromptEval (Polo et al. 2024). Failure mechanism: ad hoc template sampling confounds factors and wastes budget.

## B. Judges, preferences and measurement

**C04 — Prediction-powered inference for LLM-judge evaluation when judge error depends on the judged model.** When a judge's error is correlated with the model being judged (self-preference, length bias), do prediction-powered estimators and rank-set methods (Boyeau et al. 2024 "AutoEval done right"; Chatzi et al. 2024 prediction-powered ranking) keep nominal coverage, how much efficiency is lost, and does a labelling allocation stratified by model do better? Comparator: judge-only scores; PPI with a random labelled subset. Failure mechanism: model-dependent judge bias with small labelled subsets. Data: MT-Bench human judgments; arena data with judge labels; JudgeBench; RewardBench.

**C05 — Bradley–Terry adequacy on public arena preference data.** Do crowd pairwise preferences satisfy the Bradley–Terry model (transitivity, homogeneity across voters and prompt categories, the tie model), and how much do violations move rankings and their stated uncertainty? Comparator: Bradley–Terry and Elo as used by arenas; style-controlled BT; mixture-BT. Failure mechanism: a misspecified model gives overconfident ranks. Data: lmarena human-preference releases (55k, 100k), Arena-Hard.

**C11 — Generalisability theory for LLM judges.** Decomposing judge scores into item, judge, prompt-template and seed variance components (G-theory), what reliability do common judge set-ups reach, and how many judges × templates × items are needed for a target reliability (a decision study)? Comparator: inter-rater agreement studies ("Judging the judges"); single-judge single-template reporting. Failure mechanism: single-judge scores reported as if reliable. Data: public judge datasets plus small local judges (1.5–3B).

## C. Conformal, detection and randomised tests

**C06 — Shift-robust conformal selective answering for LLMs.** Under topic or difficulty shift between calibration and deployment prompts, how much coverage do conformal abstention rules for LLM answers lose, does weighted conformal with embedding-based density ratios restore it, and can a cheap exchangeability diagnostic predict the loss? Comparator: split conformal (conformal language modelling; conformal factuality); weighted conformal (Tibshirani et al. 2019). Failure mechanism: exchangeability violation. Compute: scoring MCQ and short QA with 1–3B models.

**C07 — Key-conditional validity of randomised detection tests** (flag: adjacent to the previous project's lesson of 2026-09-30). For detection tests whose p-values are exact on average over a secret random key (token-level watermarks, activation watermarks, fingerprints, copyright traps) but which are deployed with one fixed key, how dispersed is the per-key false-positive rate, which property of the statistic controls the dispersion, and do key-conditional calibration or derandomisation restore per-key validity at what power cost? Comparator: marginal tests (KGW z-score; pivotal statistics of Li, Ruan, Wang, Long and Su; resampling tests). Failure mechanism: marginal validity is not conditional validity. Compute: light (synthetic plus a 1.5B model).

**C12 — Group-conditional calibration of zero-shot AI-text detectors.** Do zero-shot detectors (Binoculars with a 1.5B base–instruct pair; Fast-DetectGPT) keep their nominal false-positive rate across writer groups and domains, and does Mondrian (group-conditional) conformal calibration restore group-wise control at acceptable power? Comparator: a single global threshold; RAID-style evaluation. Failure mechanism: calibration shift across groups (non-native writers, Liang et al. 2023). Data: RAID and permissively licensed human corpora with group labels.

## D. Provenance, membership, scaling and interpretability

**C08 — A calibrated hypothesis test for model lineage.** Can "model B was fine-tuned from base A" be tested from weights or activations with a null built from independent models and a controlled false-positive rate, across a public population of small fine-tunes (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-0.5B, SmolLM2 families), and how does power fall with fine-tuning depth, pruning, quantisation and merging? Comparator: REEF (CKA fingerprints), HuRef, PhyloLM, weight-distance heuristics. Failure mechanism: fingerprints report similarity scores without calibrated error rates. Who acts: licence enforcement, provenance auditors, hub moderation.

**C09 — Shift-robust dataset inference with negative controls.** Can dataset-membership inference for LLMs be made valid under distribution shift between candidate members and reference non-members by a matched negative-control design (post-cutoff texts from the same source; multi-model differencing), and at what power? Comparator: dataset inference (Maini et al. 2024); Min-K%; MIMIR (Duan et al. 2024); blind baselines (Das et al. 2024). Failure mechanism: distribution shift between members and non-members masquerades as memorisation. Data: Pythia with Pile splits (pythia-1.4b is cached).

**C10 — Statistical inference for scaling laws at toy scale.** How uncertain are fitted scaling exponents and compute-optimal allocations from a realistic number of runs with seed noise, and do standard fitting procedures (the Chinchilla approaches) recover known exponents in a controlled tiny-model testbed (1–20M parameters)? Comparator: Hoffmann et al. 2022; Besiroglu et al. 2024 replication; Porian et al. 2024; Choshen et al. 2024. Failure mechanism: point estimates without uncertainty; fitting instability. Compute: 50–100 tiny models (tens of hours).

**C13 — Statistical reliability of activation-patching importance claims.** How many prompts, and what multiple-testing control, are needed for component-importance rankings (attention heads, MLPs) from activation patching to be stable and significant, and how often do published-style procedures declare spurious components? Comparator: hypothesis tests for circuits (Shi et al. 2024); faithfulness-metric robustness (Miller et al. 2024). Failure mechanism: no error control over hundreds of components with small prompt sets. Compute: GPT-2 small or Qwen2.5-0.5B.
