# C14 — Factorial designs for prompt-format sensitivity (search-level screen, 2026-10-08)

## Candidate (one sentence)
Do orthogonal-array or fractional-factorial designs over prompt-format factors (separators, casing, option labels, instruction phrasing, few-shot count) estimate main effects and interactions on accuracy more efficiently than one-factor-at-a-time or random template sampling, with a variance-component model giving the sensitivity decomposition, against FormatSpread (Sclar et al. 2023), multi-prompt evaluation (Mizrahi et al. 2024) and PromptEval (Polo et al. 2024)?

## Occupied (what the candidate can no longer claim)
- That prompt format drives large accuracy swings and should be reported as a distribution/spread (Sclar et al.; Mizrahi et al.; Format Sensitivity Index).
- Factorial designs over prompt factors with interaction analysis (Weber et al. 2023; Jadhav et al. 2026) and design-of-experiments constructions for prompt components and phrase order (TCARD; order-of-addition experiments; Corti & Leaver).
- Mixed-effects/variance-component attribution of evaluation variance to pipeline factors (CAFE; Messing; NIST AI 800-3).
- Efficient estimation of the template-performance distribution (PromptEval).

## Residual gap (type: applied evaluation / adequacy check with a small methods component)
Observed: no retrieved work compares orthogonal-array or resolution-IV/V fractional-factorial designs against one-factor-at-a-time and random template sampling (the FormatSpread approach) for estimating main effects and two-factor interactions of *format* factors on accuracy, nor fits a crossed variance-component model (item × format factors × model, with decoding replicates) to give a sensitivity decomposition — "search-level; absence is not proof of novelty"; the queries returning nothing were arXiv API `abs:"orthogonal array" …` (0 hits), `abs:Taguchi …` (0 hits) and the WebSearch on Taguchi/orthogonal arrays for prompts (no such study found).
Inference: the design question is standard DOE applied to a Bernoulli outcome with item random effects; its interest lies in (a) whether FormatSpread-style random sampling gives biased or inefficient spread and main-effect estimates relative to a balanced design of equal size, (b) how large format × format and format × model interactions are (Weber et al. and Jadhav et al. suggest they are material, which limits resolution-III screening), and (c) a variance share for "format" comparable across models and benchmarks. TCARD and CAFE show the DOE-for-prompts space is now active (2026), so the window is narrowing.
Proposal: a pre-registered study on 2–3 small open-weight models (0.5–3B, MPS) and 2–3 multiple-choice/short-answer benchmarks with five or six two- or three-level format factors, comparing OA/fractional designs, OFAT and random sampling at equal budget on bias and variance of main effects, interactions and the spread, with a crossed random-effects (GLMM) model for the decomposition; the methods piece is the design-efficiency comparison under item random effects and decoding noise. Budget is feasible because multiple-choice scoring uses forward passes (about 1,600 tok/s measured) rather than generation.
