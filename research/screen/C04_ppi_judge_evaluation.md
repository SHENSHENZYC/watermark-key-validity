# C04 — Prediction-powered inference for LLM-judge evaluation when judge error depends on the judged model (search-level screen, 2026-10-08)

## Candidate (one sentence)
When an LLM judge's error is correlated with the model being judged (self-preference, family bias, length bias), do prediction-powered estimators and rank-set methods keep nominal coverage with small human-labelled subsets, how much efficiency is lost, and does a labelling allocation stratified by judged model do better than a random labelled subset?

## Residual gap
Type: adequacy check / applied evaluation, with a small design component. Observed: no result I saw combines (i) judge error that depends on the judged model, (ii) coverage of PPI/PPI++ intervals and of rank-sets, (iii) a labelled subset stratified by judged model with Neyman-style allocation, and (iv) public human-labelled pairwise data — search-level; absence is not proof of novelty; the queries that returned nothing on this exact combination were the two WebSearch queries above and the two arXiv API `"prediction-powered"` queries. Inference: the pieces exist (StratPPI supplies the estimator, Fiedler's ΔJ supplies the mechanism, Chatzi supplies the ranking target), so the contribution would be the stress test and the specific stratification variable, not a new estimator. Proposal: fit judge-error-by-model on MT-Bench (GPT-4 judging GPT-4's own answers against five other models) and on locally generated open-weight judge labels, then compare random versus model-stratified labelled subsets for coverage and interval width of per-model win rates and rank-sets, with Fiedler's J and ΔJ reported as covariates of failure.
