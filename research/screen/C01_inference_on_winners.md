# C01 — Inference on winners for LLM leaderboards (search-level screen, 2026-10-08)

## Candidate (one sentence)
Is the reported top score on a K-model, n-item LLM leaderboard biased upward by selecting the maximum (winner's curse), and do selective-inference corrections (Andrews–Kitagawa–McCloskey conditional and hybrid intervals; Fithian–Sun–Taylor-style conditioning; Zrnic–Fithian zoom) give valid and usefully tight intervals for the winner's score and the top-k gaps on public per-item leaderboard data, against naive Wald/bootstrap and Bonferroni?

## Occupied (what the candidate can no longer claim)
- That LLM leaderboard winners suffer a winner's curse / post-selection coverage failure is established (Leaderboard Lottery: 0–58% coverage of the "winner is best" claim; Yang & Chen: 391/394 adjacent claims unsupported; Zrnic & Fithian's Arena illustration).
- Confidence sets for the *identity* of the best model exist (paired-bootstrap MCB; Zrnic & Fithian's extension; and, for the anytime setting, rank confidence sequences 2609.32211 — see C03).
- Winner's-curse corrections for *prompt/pipeline* selection exist (SIREN; the preprints.org paper).
- Two 2024–2026 methods papers already name model leaderboards as an application of winner-conditional inference (Zrnic & Fithian; Bakshi et al.).

## Residual gap (type: adequacy check / applied evaluation, with a small methods component)
Observed: no retrieved work evaluates conditional (AKM), hybrid, zoom and randomised-conditional intervals for the *winner's score and the top-k gaps* on real per-sample leaderboard records (Open LLM Leaderboard v2 `samples_*.jsonl`, 136k Arena votes), with K in the tens to hundreds, scores that are means of Bernoulli item outcomes dependent across models through shared items, and near-ties at the top — "search-level; absence is not proof of novelty"; the queries that returned nothing on this were the arXiv API `all:"winner's curse" AND …` (5 hits, none doing this), the OpenAlex `"post-selection inference" benchmark "model selection"` sweep, and the WebSearch on AKM applications.
Inference: the Leaderboard Lottery occupies the "which model is best" set question and Zrnic–Fithian the illustration; what is left is the quantitative adequacy question — coverage under item resampling, width relative to naive and Bonferroni intervals, behaviour when the top margin is within one standard error, and the effect of composite (multi-benchmark) scores — plus whether the bias is practically large on today's leaderboards (Yang & Chen's audit suggests margins are mostly within noise, which would make debiased winner intervals very wide).
Proposal: frame as an adequacy study of four correction families on two public per-item sources, with a pre-registered estimand (winner's population score; top-1 vs top-k gaps), and a robustness arm for hidden selection (Yang & Chen's sensitivity curve) and for Bradley–Terry winners (Arena). The methods component would be the finite-sample behaviour of these intervals for dependent Bernoulli means, which none of the retrieved abstracts addresses.

## Novelty score (1–5) and justification
**2.** Close predecessors exist on both axes: the problem is quantified on Open LLM Leaderboard v2 (Leaderboard Lottery, 2026) and audited on the Open LLM Leaderboard (Yang & Chen, 2026); a winner's-curse correction has been illustrated on Chatbot Arena (Zrnic & Fithian, 2024) and model leaderboards are named as a use case by a 2026 stat.ME methods paper. What remains is an adequacy comparison of correction families on raw per-item records, which is a legitimate but narrower contribution than the candidate as written.
