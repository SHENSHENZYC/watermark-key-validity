# C07 — Key-conditional validity of randomised detection tests (search-level screen, 2026-10-08)

Flag: adjacent to the previous project (activation-steering watermarks), where a key-resampling test was exact on average over keys while a fixed key's false-positive rate sat near 0% or 100%.

## Candidate (one sentence)
For detection tests whose p-values are exact on average over a secret random key (token-level watermarks such as KGW, Aaronson/Gumbel and Kuditipudi et al.; activation watermarks; model fingerprints; copyright traps) but which are deployed with one fixed key, how dispersed is the per-key false-positive rate, which property of the statistic controls the dispersion, and do key-conditional calibration on null texts or derandomisation restore per-key validity, at what power cost (comparators: marginal tests such as the KGW z-score, the pivotal statistics of Li, Ruan, Wang, Long and Su, and the Three Bricks tests of Fernandez et al.)?

## Residual gap (type: new method / adequacy check / applied evaluation / theory)
Type: theory plus adequacy check, with a small method component. Observed: no retrieved paper takes the per-key (conditional) false-positive rate as its object, quantifies its dispersion across keys for a fixed human-text distribution, or identifies the statistic property that governs it; the nearest statements are an appendix remark in 2512.16904 and the within-text collision analysis of Cai et al. Inference: the mechanism is a conditional-versus-marginal validity gap whose size should be governed by how many effectively independent key draws a human text exposes (context width h, with h = 0 Unigram-style lists as the worst case; vocabulary frequency concentration; repeated n-grams), so KGW with large h and Gumbel/inverse-transform statistics should concentrate while Unigram and activation-direction tests should disperse. Proposal: (i) a decomposition FPR(key) = α + Δ(key) with a variance formula in terms of the key-reuse structure, checked on synthetic text; (ii) empirical dispersion over several hundred keys on fixed public human corpora for KGW (h = 0…4), Gumbel/Aaronson, Kuditipudi EXP/ITS and the pivotal statistics — detection of these schemes needs only the tokenizer and the key, so this part is CPU-only; (iii) remedies with power cost: key-conditional calibration on null texts (empirical null per key) and derandomisation by a multi-key design with e-value aggregation. Search-level statement: queries on "false positive" × key, "p-value"/"type I" × key, "null distribution"/"empirical false positive", variance/dispersion/"across keys", the randomised-test conditional-validity query, and three WebSearches returned no paper with this framing. Absence is not proof of novelty; the Su group (2510.22007, 2507.23113, 2602.14286) works on the adjacent mechanism and could occupy this quickly.
