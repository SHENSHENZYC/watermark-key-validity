# C02 — Two-level resampling design for paired model comparisons (search-level screen, 2026-10-08)

## Candidate (one sentence)
Given a budget of B generations, how should an evaluator split it between more prompts and more decoding samples per prompt to minimise the variance of a paired accuracy or win-rate difference, and which resampling scheme (item bootstrap, nested bootstrap, exact two-level permutation) holds its Type I error at small n, against Miller 2024, Madaan et al. 2024 and Bowyer et al. 2025?

## Occupied (what the candidate can no longer claim)
- The decomposition of evaluation noise into item (data) and generation (prediction) components, and the message that repeated generations and pairing raise power (Wang; Zhang, Cai & Chen).
- CLT failure at small n and the poor behaviour of the plain bootstrap (Bowyer et al.).
- Hierarchical/cluster bootstrap for nested evaluation data and their coverage properties (Anglin; D'Oro et al.).
- Paired power and resolution diagnostics for leaderboard gaps, including clustering (Kotawala; Miller).

## Residual gap (type: adequacy check + applied design; small methods component)
Observed: no retrieved work (i) states and tests a cost-aware allocation rule for the number of prompts n and decoding samples k per prompt that minimises the variance of a *paired* difference, or (ii) compares item bootstrap, nested (two-stage) bootstrap and an exact two-level permutation (prompt-level sign flips with within-prompt resampling) for Type I error at small n with Bernoulli outcomes — "search-level; absence is not proof of novelty"; the queries returning nothing relevant were arXiv API `abs:"nested bootstrap" …`, `abs:"optimal allocation" …`, `abs:"permutation test" …`, and OpenAlex `"nested bootstrap" "language model" evaluation`.
Inference: under the two-level model Var(difference) ≈ σ_d²/n + σ_p²/(nk) with B = nk and no per-prompt overhead, k = 1 minimises variance for a population target, so repeats pay only when prompts are scarce (a fixed benchmark), when per-prompt overhead (prefill, judge calls) is large, or when the target is the fixed-set difference; Wang's finding that paired prediction noise usually exceeds paired data noise is consistent with the fixed-set regime being the practically relevant one. This narrows the design question to a defensible but modest one.
Proposal: a pre-registered simulation plus local-generation study (0.5–3B open-weight models, 2–4 benchmarks) estimating the two variance components for paired differences, deriving the allocation under measured prefill/decode costs (the M5's 1,600 tok/s forward vs 15–110 tok/s generation makes the cost ratio concrete), and auditing Type I error and power of the three resampling schemes at n ∈ {20, 50, 100, 300}. The exact two-level permutation for paired Bernoulli outcomes is the candidate methods piece.

## Novelty score (1–5) and justification
**3.** The variance decomposition and the "use repeats and pairing" message are occupied by 2025–2026 work (Wang; Zhang, Cai & Chen), and small-n interval failure is occupied by Bowyer et al. No retrieved paper poses the budget split for a paired difference with a cost model or compares nested bootstrap with an exact two-level permutation for Type I error at small n, so the residual is a real but incremental design-and-adequacy question whose answer may be partly a known textbook result (k = 1 under pure generation cost).
