## 1. Question and type
**An applied, paired evaluation of the power cost of each remedy at the thresholds Study 3 committed, per key, on one open-weight model's watermarked generations; and the realised false-positive rate of the same thresholds on the same model's unwatermarked generations (the null-law mismatch a deployer who calibrated on human text faces), with Study 3's key-free predictor tested out of sample on that model-generated null law.** It closes the research question's last clause ("which remedies restore per-key validity at what power cost?"). It measures; it proves nothing, and it adds no new scheme.

## 2. The input: Study 3's committed thresholds record, and how Study 4 binds to it
- **What exists.** `outputs/study3_v0.1/cells/cal__<tokenizer>__<cell>__h<h>__thresholds.npz`, 60 units (two tokenizers × ten cells × h ∈ {0, 1, 2}), each with 120 per-key arrays `<family>|<rule>|<method>@<N_cal>|<α>` of 1,000 thresholds on the p-value scale (families kgw, gumbel; rules raw, distinct; methods conformal, tolerance; N_cal 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000; α 0.01, 0.05, 0.001) and 24 scheme-level arrays `<family>|<rule>|empirical|<α>` and `…|pooled|<α>` of one threshold each; the fixed methods are nominal (t = α) and multiplier20 (t = α/20) and need no array. Each unit's JSON record carries the array file's SHA-256 (`thresholds_sha256`), and `RUN_MANIFEST.json` carries every file's digest and the inventory digest `c270ab81…` (the lock `0e288928…`, commit `887a2c7` for the record).
- **The binding Study 4 retains (R07/R08 carried forward).** Index i of every per-key array is evaluation key 23,000 + i (the grid's `key_eval_start` 23000, K 1,000, `s3_keys.STUDY3_EVAL`; the runner wrote the arrays in that key order). Study 4's loader reads the unit JSON, re-hashes the array file against `thresholds_sha256` and against the run manifest's digest, checks the inventory digest, binds (tokenizer, family, rule, h, calibration cell, N_cal, α, method, key index → key) and refuses any mismatch. A threshold is never recomputed or repaired.
- **Abstentions (never repaired; reported).** A threshold of 0 never rejects. Structural zeros (order index j = 0): tolerance at α = 0.001 for N_cal ≤ 2,000 and conformal@500 at α = 0.001, in every unit, family and rule; plus six positive-index zeros, all under `gumbel|raw|conformal@1000|0.001` on the Gutenberg calibration units (Llama h = 0: 1 key, h = 1: 1, h = 2: 3; Qwen h = 1: 1) — re-derived from the record in §6 below. Study 4 treats every zero as abstaining (power 0, level 0) and lists them separately, as R08 F6 requires.
- **The null side is already measured for these keys.** The evaluation units `eval__<tokenizer>__<cell>__h<h>.npz` hold, for every key, the count of rejected evaluation texts under every threshold (nominal and multiplier20; every same-tokenizer calibration source; every method, N_cal and α). So each key's realised null level ℓ_k(m) on the corpus cell is a stored input, and Study 4 can pair it with the same key's power without any new null computation on the corpora.

## 4. Estimands and the paired design
Per key k, prompt cell, (family, h, δ), rule, length L, method m, calibration source and α:
- **Realised power** π_k(m) = n⁻¹ Σ_i 1{p_k(x_ki) < t_k^m}, over the key's n generated texts; the rejection strict as in Study 3.
- **Power cost** Δ_k(m) = π_k(nominal) − π_k(m), paired on the same texts. Because the texts are shared, π_k is monotone in the threshold by construction: the *sign* of a cost is not an empirical question, its *magnitude*, its *across-key distribution* and its relation to the key's null level are.
- **The deployer's operating point** per key: (ℓ_k(m), π_k(m)), the stored null level on the corpus cell beside the power; and (ℓ̃_k(m), π_k(m)) with ℓ̃_k the level on the model's unwatermarked generations.
- **Across keys:** the key-mean, SD and quantiles of π_k and Δ_k; the ratio R_pow(m) = SD_k(π_k(m))/SD_k(π_k(nominal)) with a paired key bootstrap.
- **The model-null block:** on n₀ unwatermarked generations per prompt cell, the realised level ℓ̃_k(m) of every committed threshold for every evaluation key (all 1,000; cheap on the CPU), its key-mean, SD, shares above α, and Study 3's predictor for it from the generated null texts' own second moments (ρ̄ and σ² of the generated set, ρ_CE against each calibration prefix; the exact identities; the common-factor structure), with T1's statistic and band.

What is key-free and computed before the lock: the prompt plan and digests, the generated-null predictor's inputs *cannot* be (the null generations are produced by the run), so the model-null predictor's values are computed by the locked analyzer from the run's own null texts, from identities that involve no key; the protocol says so.

## 5. Candidate pre-registered rules (fixed in the protocol after D12–D14 and the pilot)
- **W1 (the magnitude of the per-key calibration cost; a prediction from the record and the pilot).** At the primary setting (KGW, distinct, h = 1, α = 0.01, L = 256, the diagonal, conformal@5000 against nominal), each key's predicted cost is Δ̂_k = F̄(z(t_k)) − F̄(z_α), with t_k the key's committed threshold and F̄ the pooled empirical law of the watermarked z over the pilot's texts and validation keys at the chosen strength (a key-homogeneous watermark effect, the simplest model). The rule compares the key-median of the realised Δ_k with the key-median of Δ̂_k: pass when the difference lies within c pp with its paired key-bootstrap interval (c fixed from the pilot's precision; the working figure 3 pp). A failure says the key's own watermark strength, not only its threshold shift, sets the cost. The same statistic is reported descriptively for every method and setting.
- **W2 (does per-key calibration change the across-key dispersion of power, and in which direction?).** The paired ratio R_pow(m) = SD_k(π_k(m))/SD_k(π_k(nominal)) at the primary setting under both rules, with the noise-corrected between-key SDs (the observed variance minus the mean binomial variance, Study 1's known-noise form) and a paired key bootstrap. Two sources move the power across keys: the key's null shift D_k, which a per-key threshold removes (the common-factor model predicts R_pow < 1 where the null shift is large: the raw rule at h = 1, nominal null SD 1.36 pp), and the key's own watermark strength (the green share its list reaches under the soft rule), which no null calibration touches and which a per-key threshold's own Beta variability adds to. The pilot's first two validation keys differ by 28 pp of power at δ = 0.5 and 256 tokens (green shares 0.333 against 0.315), so the second source is not small at a weak strength. The protocol therefore states the prediction as a model-derived side per setting (R_pow < 1 under the raw rule; no material change, R_pow inside [2/3, 3/2], under the distinct rule) and decides agreement or disagreement on the interval, with a mechanism diagnostic beside it: the across-key correlation between the key's stored null level (or its null shift in z) and its watermarked green share, and the share of the power variance the null shift explains in a one-factor regression. No ranking of remedies on power is claimed from W2.
- **W3 (the model-null law: T1 out of sample).** For the eligible (calibration source, method, N_cal, α) rows on the model's unwatermarked generations (eligibility and the band as Study 3 §6: theory share ≥ 0.75, no flag; [2/3, 3/2]), at least 80% of the resolved rows consistent; the row counts never the share alone.
- **Descriptive (no rule):** the length curves (64, 128, 256); every other method, α and calibration source (the transfer thresholds' power and model-null level); the Gumbel family; the quality diagnostics (the generated texts' mean log-probability under the unshifted model against the human continuation's, rep-4, EOS mass) with a stated loop bar (a text with rep-4 above 0.5 is reported as degenerate; the share of such texts per setting is a reported integrity figure, never excluded from the power).
- **Integrity checks:** the record's digests and inventory; every token array's digest in the run manifest; the hook-against-detector equality re-checked on the run's own texts (the stored green flags against the detector's colouring of the committed tokens); the locked analyzer re-computes every power from the committed tokens.

**Reading (design facts, not results).**
- **The strength axis works as intended.** At δ = 0.5 the nominal power at the calibrated length is intermediate (distinct@256: 0.66 and 0.47; raw@256: 0.73 and 0.45) at no visible quality cost (PPL 20.2–20.0 against 21.2 unwatermarked); at δ = 1 the power at 256 tokens is 1.00 and 1.00 and intermediate only at 64 tokens (0.69, 0.62); at the published default δ = 2 it is 0.97 or more at every length from 64 tokens, with PPL 26.4–31.9. So a two-strength design, δ = 0.5 (the costs visible at the calibrated operating point) and δ = 2 (the default; the costs at saturation), is the recommendation for D13; δ = 1 would show the costs only at lengths that are not calibrated operating points.
- **The key matters at a weak strength.** The two validation keys differ by 28 points of power at δ = 0.5 and 256 tokens under the raw rule (19 under the distinct rule), with green shares 0.333 against 0.315: the across-key dispersion of power is a quantity to measure with many keys (W2), not to assume small.
- **Gumbel-max with the record's context widths has no usable generation side.** Loop shares 0.97–1.00 at h = 0, 0.97–1.00 at h = 1 and 0.42–0.52 at h = 2 (PPL 1.3–6.9: the texts repeat themselves), where every KGW setting stays at a loop share of 0.02 or below. Its detector power on such text would be a property of the loops. Recommendation: no Gumbel generation arm (a documented limitation: the record's Gumbel thresholds get a model-null level but no power side); a longer context (h ≥ 4) or position-indexed keys would be a different detector than the one calibrated.
- **Chinese Wikipedia prompts.** The model's own Chinese text has PPL 33.8; at δ = 2 the power is 0.95 or more from 64 tokens with PPL 54.0 (a larger quality cost than in English); Gumbel loops in 0.78 of texts. A Chinese prompt cell is feasible for KGW (an option in D14).
- **The nominal level of the unwatermarked texts** on two validation keys and 64 texts (0.03, 0.03 at 256 tokens) is a two-key, 64-text figure and says nothing about the model-null block; that block scores all 1,000 evaluation keys on thousands of texts after the lock.

## 8. Decisions for Yichen
**D12 — scope** (recommendation first):
- **A (recommended): the power side and the model-null side, KGW primary.** Watermarked generations at KGW h = 1 (primary) and h = 2 (the design remedy, paired on the same prompts and keys), scored at every committed threshold of the key (all methods, α and same-tokenizer calibration sources: free); unwatermarked generations from the same prompts scored at every committed threshold for all 1,000 keys (the deployer's actual false-positive rate on machine text that is not watermarked; W3's out-of-sample test of the predictor); no Gumbel-max generation arm (the pilot: loops in 42–100% of texts at h ≤ 2; the record's Gumbel thresholds get a model-null level from the unwatermarked generations but no power side, stated as a limitation); three English prompt cells (news, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange) with Chinese Wikipedia as an optional fourth if the pilot's Chinese generations are sound. Trade-off: the fullest closing of the question at the cost of the unwatermarked runs (one per prompt cell) and a second width.
- **B: the power side only.** As A without the unwatermarked generations. Cheaper by one run per cell; loses the deployer's false-positive rate on the model's own null law and the only out-of-sample test of the predictor on a non-corpus law.
- **C: minimal.** KGW h = 1 only, one prompt cell (news), the diagonal and the two transfer sources with the largest predicted v. The quickest closing of the question; thin material for the paper's power figure and no model-null block.

## 9. What is not claimed; risks
Not claimed: any theorem; any power guarantee or a power ranking of the remedies beyond the measured settings; any statement about other models, sampling settings (top-p, temperature) or attacks (no paraphrase or edit robustness); any statement about the held-out partition (`final: false`); priority on per-key calibration, its pricing or the multiplier (constraints 13–14), or on power measurements of KGW and Gumbel-max as such (the published record's). Risks: (i) the Gumbel-max scheme with a short context hash loops (the probe), so its power may be a property of degenerate text — reported with the quality diagnostics and the loop share, never excluded; (ii) the generated null law differs from every corpus cell, so the model-null block's predictor is a transfer prediction from the corpus calibration sets, and its eligibility may be narrow; (iii) MPS bfloat16 generation is not bit-reproducible, so the record is the committed token arrays, and the protocol says what is reproducible from them (everything downstream) and what is not (the generation itself, given the seeds).
