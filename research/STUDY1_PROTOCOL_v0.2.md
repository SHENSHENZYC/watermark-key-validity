## 1. Question and type
**Adequacy check of the first-order theory, by simulation.** For the red–green (KGW-style) z-test deployed with one fixed key, the gate gives (i) **exact identities** for the across-key variance of any weighted sum of colours under the fixed-size and the independent colouring models and (ii) a **first-order approximation** to the per-key false-positive rate, α_P(k) ≈ Φ̄((z_α − δ_k)/σ), with δ_k the key's mean shift of the z-score. Study 1 asks, on fully specified synthetic text laws where every quantity is computable: Q1, do the exact identities hold in simulation (a check of the simulator and of the finite-population correction, R2–R3); Q2, is the first-order predictor of the across-key dispersion of the level adequate, and where does it fail (R1); Q3, how does the dispersion behave with text length under each repeated-context rule, including whether it saturates (H-length, T1; R4 as a rule for the raw rule, R4b for the support control, curves otherwise); Q4, how does it behave with context width (H-width; R5). No real text is used; no real-corpus false-positive rate is measured (Study 2).

## 2. Information timing

The only "deployed" quantity is the per-key level over a fixed key; the key law is the uniform draw over a seed range. ## 3. Target, sign, units
Per cell (law, width h, length L, retention rule, colouring model, γ), over the N_eff texts with M_i > 0 retained positions and K keys:
- **Primary target (conditional on the realised texts):** the per-key level α̂_k = N_eff⁻¹ Σ_i 1{z_ik > z_α}, a deterministic function of the fixed texts and the key, and its across-key SD, SD_K(α̂_k), with a key-bootstrap interval (keys are independent draws). No text-noise correction is applied: the texts are fixed, and the across-key variation that the finite texts induce is part of the target.
- **The predictor (the first-order theory, from the identities alone, no outcome enters):** across keys δ_k ~ N(μ_δ, ρ̄); within a key the texts' scores are independent N(δ_k, σ²); hence α_k = Φ̄((z_α − δ_k)/σ) and **Var_K(α̂_k) = Var(α_k) + E[α_k(1 − α_k)]/N_eff**, both terms by Gauss–Hermite quadrature over δ. Inputs: μ_δ = (g − γ) Σ_u ŵ_z(u) (the exact across-key mean of the z-shift; zero when γV is an integer); ρ̄ = (N_eff/(N_eff − 1))·[Var_K(z̄) − N_eff⁻¹ mean_i Var_K(z_i)], the average pairwise across-key covariance of two texts' scores, computed exactly from the identity at the realised document-averaged z-weights ŵ_z(u) = N_eff⁻¹ Σ_i n_iu/√(M_i γ(1−γ)) and at each text's own weights, under the cell's colouring (an unbiased estimate of the population mean-shift variance Var_K E_X[z | K] under i.i.d. texts; it removes the finite-text floor that the realised-weight identity carries); σ² = mean_i(Var_K(z_i) + μ_i²) − ρ̄ − μ_δ² (the key-averaged within-key variance, exact under the identities). Diagnostics: the unit-variance variant (σ = 1) and the delta-method limit φ((z_α − μ_δ)/σ)·√ρ̄/σ; the observed within-key variance σ_k² against σ²; the ratio Var(α_k)/(Var(α_k) + floor), called the theory share.
- **Secondary population target:** Var_K(α_P(k)) estimated by s_K²(α̂) − MS_E/N_eff from the two-way rejection table (E[s_K²] = v_K + v_E/N), with a two-way bootstrap SE; compared with Var(α_k) under the mixture (descriptive).
- **Also per cell:** q̂_k (the per-key mean green fraction, exactly Σ_u ŵ_q(u) G_k(u)) and its across-key variance with the exact identity at ŵ_q under both models (R3); z̄_k and its across-key variance against the identity at ŵ_z; the M distribution (mean, min, max, quantiles); the coverage (distinct retained tuples; the share of V at h = 0); the between-text collision Ã = (N_eff/(N_eff − 1))·[A(ŵ_q) − N_eff⁻¹ mean_i A_i^q] with the i.i.d. laws' analytic C_h = C_0^(h+1); the key quantiles (1, 5, 50, 95, 99%), the worst and best sampled keys, the shares of keys above 2α and 5α; the exact binomial key-mean level under independent colours and distinct tuples; the structural-zero flag.
Units: keys (K = 500 for the core laws under the independent colouring; 200 for the fixed colouring on the core subset, a reported comparison; 2,000 for the illustration laws with V ∈ {2, 10}; 500 for V = 100 and for the support control) and texts (N = 4,000 per law, shared across keys and across widths, truncated for each L). Sign convention: "larger dispersion" means a larger SD_K(α̂_k); the predictor is "adequate" in a cell when the observed-to-predicted ratio lies in [2/3, 3/2].

## 4. Data, split and holdout
None (synthetic). Text laws: (a) i.i.d. Zipf over V tokens with exponent s: (V, s) ∈ {(1,000, 1.0), (10,000, 1.0), (50,000, 1.0), (10,000, 1.2)}; (b) first-order Markov–Zipf chains with per-context supports of S = 50 tokens and global-escape probability ε = 0.1, V ∈ {1,000, 10,000}, started from the global law with 64 burn-in steps dropped (close to, not exactly at, stationarity); (c) illustration laws, reported separately and excluded from R1, R4, R5: Zipf V = 10, Zipf V = 100, the reviewer's two-token chain (p_stay = 0.9); (d) the T1 control law, uniform on the first 50 tokens of a 1,000-token vocabulary (illustration; R4b). Texts of length L_max = 2,048 (core; 1,024 illustration) are generated once per law from PCG64 seeded by `text_seed_base + sha256(law name)`, the same texts for every width h of that law (the chain for each h re-derives the tuples from the same texts, so the width comparison is paired); each L ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024, 2,048} (core) or {64, 256, 1,024} (illustration) is a prefix, and the retention masks are prefix-stable. Keys as in §2. The colourings of a chain are computed once per (colouring, γ) over the whole tuple dictionary and reused for every length and rule (paired curves within a chain); under the fixed-size model the hypergeometric count per context class is drawn from PCG64 seeded by (key, 20261010) in the chain's context traversal order, so a key's draw for a context is paired across lengths and rules within a chain but not across chains (the model is exchangeable, so no statement depends on cross-chain pairing). A hash supplies computational, not statistical, independence (the ideal random-set identities are what the simulation tests; R3 is the empirical check). Degenerate (γ, V) combinations (m = ⌊γV⌋ ∈ {0, V}: the two-token chain at γ = 0.25) are written as marker cells with the reason and enter no rule. The corpora's sealed holdout is untouched (`final: false`).

## 5. Competitors and baselines
The detector under study is the green-fraction z-test with centre γ and denominator √(Mγ(1−γ)), threshold z_α, γ ∈ {0.5; 0.25 on the Zipf V = 10,000 and Markov V = 10,000 laws and on the illustration laws}, with three repeated-context rules: **raw** (every position scored), **distinct** (each distinct (h+1)-tuple once per text; Three Bricks), **first** (the first token after each distinct context once per text; textGrain's rule), and two colouring models at the common green fraction g = ⌊γV⌋/V: **independent** (green iff U(key, tuple) < g; the implementation surrogate for Study 2) and **fixed** (per context class a uniformly random m-subset, realised exactly on the queried tokens by a hypergeometric count and a uniform choice; KGW's seeded permutation), the latter on the illustration laws and on the core subset h ∈ {0, 1}, L ∈ {256, 2,048}. The comparator statement is the first-order predictor of §3.

## 6. Primary metric and contrast
**R1 (adequacy, primary):** over the eligible core cells — independent colouring, γ = 0.5, rules raw and distinct, α = 0.01, mean M ≥ 30, non-degenerate, theory share ≥ 0.75 (so that a cell whose theory term were absent would show a ratio ≤ 1/2, below the band; the floor alone cannot pass) — the ratio SD_K(α̂_k)/SD_pred with its key-bootstrap 95% interval is **consistent** when the interval lies inside [2/3, 3/2], **inconsistent** when it lies outside, **unresolved** otherwise; R1 passes when at least 30 cells are resolved and at least 80% of the resolved cells are consistent. Inconsistent and unresolved cells are reported by (law, rule, h, L) with σ_δ = √ρ̄, M, the theory share, σ² (model and observed) and the key-mean level (observed and predicted). Secondary blocks (descriptive, same statistic): α = 0.05 and 0.001; the first rule; the γ = 0.25 cells; the unit-σ predictor; the illustration laws; the fixed colouring.

## 7. Secondary diagnostics (descriptive)
The population diagnostic (F3): s_K² − MS_E/N_eff with its SE against Var(α_k) under the mixture, on the R1 cells. The curves against L under each rule (T1): ρ̄(L) with its text-bootstrap SE, the mean M, SD_K(α̂_k) observed and predicted; saturation and non-monotonicity allowed under the distinct and first rules. The support control's table (R4b): ρ̄ and the q-identity at the realised weights at each L against the analytic limits (fixed g(1−g)(V−s)/(s(V−1)); independent g(1−g)/s; z-shift limits s·var_q/(γ(1−γ))). The width curves (R5): Ã(h), the realised A(ŵ_q), the analytic C_h for the i.i.d. laws, ρ̄(h), SD_K(α̂_k)(h). The key quantiles and the worst sampled key per cell; the shares of keys above 2α and 5α; the fraction of texts with M = 0 (none expected at L ≥ 64 > h; the convention is stated in §9); the collision statistics A, B, C_cond at the realised weights; the "first" rule at h = 0 (one token per text: structural zeros by construction); the exact ratio fixed/independent on the core subset with its key-bootstrap error; the two-token chain's realised-weight fixed-size variances at h = 0 and 1 beside the reviewer's population values 0 and 0.08.

## 8. Uncertainty
Keys are independent draws given the texts: every across-key statistic (SD_K(α̂_k), Var_K(q̂_k), Var_K(z̄_k)) carries a key-bootstrap interval (200 resamples of the K keys; relative Monte Carlo error of an SD ≈ 1/√(2K): 3.2% at K = 500, 1.6% at 2,000, 5% at 200). The R1 ratio's interval is the key bootstrap of the numerator; the predictor is fixed given the texts. Text sampling enters ρ̄, Ã and the identities at the realised weights: a text bootstrap (30 resamples of the texts, the weights and identities recomputed) gives their SEs, used as the tolerances of R4 and R5 (two SEs of each adjacent member). The two-way population estimate's SE is from a two-way bootstrap (100 resamples of texts and keys; the percentile interval is biased for a variance component, so a normal interval and the bootstrap mean are reported). R2's SE is SD_K(α̂_k)/√K conditional on the texts; the validation calibrates the normal 3-SE check by subsampling 500 keys from 20,000 (chance failure rates recorded in `VALIDATION.json`; the per-key level is skewed, up to skewness ≈ 10 at α = 0.001, which is why α = 0.001 is reported and not decided). Seeds: text_seed_base 20261010, validation_seed_base 99261010, bootstrap streams per cell from sha256 of the cell name with 20261011, colouring hash splitmix64 with the key mixed in, hypergeometric draws seeded by (key, 20261010); all fixed in `grid_v0.2.json` and `s1_core.py`.

## 9. Pre-registered rules and materiality thresholds
- **R1 adequacy (pass / fail):** as in §6.
- **R2 simulator validity (pass / fail; a failure is an audit flag):** under the independent colouring and the distinct rule, the key-mean level mean_k α̂_k against the exact binomial key-mean given the texts (for each text P(Bin(M_i, g) > γM_i + z_α√(M_iγ(1−γ))), averaged over texts), with SE = SD_K(α̂_k)/√K: decided at α ∈ {0.05, 0.01} on cells with K ≥ 500 and N_eff ≥ 1,000 (every production cell); pass if ≥ 97% of the checks lie within 3 SE **and** no check exceeds 5 SE; α = 0.001 reported; structural zeros (exact 0 and observed 0) counted separately. A failure invalidates no theory: it calls for an implementation and uncertainty audit, and no outcome is reported until it is resolved in a new version (the failed version is kept).
- **R3 the exact identities (pass / fail):** for every non-degenerate cell of every law and both colourings, the observed across-key variance of q̂_k with its key-bootstrap 95% interval against the exact identity at ŵ_q under the cell's own colouring; pass if the interval contains the exact value in ≥ 95% of the checks. The core fixed subset (K = 200) is reported beside the decision, not in it. The statement "the relative reduction of the fixed-size model is below 1% for V ≥ 10,000" is confirmed or refuted from the exact ratio (V − 1/C_cond)/(V − 1) at the realised weights of the core cells with V ≥ 10,000.
- **R4 H-length under the raw rule (pass / fail):** in every core chain (law, h, γ) with the independent colouring, ρ̄ is non-decreasing in L within the tolerance (ρ̄_{L′} ≥ ρ̄_L − 2(se_L + se_{L′}) for adjacent lengths); pass if every chain holds. **R4b saturation (reproduced / not):** on the support control under the distinct rule at h = 0, at the longest illustration length, ρ̄ lies within 3 text-bootstrap SEs of the analytic limit for both colourings and both γ. Under the distinct and first rules the core curves are reported with no monotonicity claim (T1).
- **R5 H-width under the raw rule (pass / fail):** in every core (law, L, γ) chain with the independent colouring, Ã is non-increasing in h within the tolerance (Ã_{h+1} ≤ Ã_h + 2(se_h + se_{h+1})); pass if every chain holds; ρ̄(h) and SD_K(α̂_k)(h) are reported (M = L − h changes with h, and the occupancy weights of different widths are not a projective family, so no rule is stated on them).
- **Integrity checks:** the runner verifies the lock schema (every digest, `approved_by`, `study`, `date`, `before_outcomes: true`, `final: false`, the reviewer's resolution memo digest, the key record) before any study cell and refuses otherwise with the reason; every cell carries its provenance and a cell with foreign provenance stops the run; the analyzer refuses an incomplete or padded inventory, mixed provenances, unlocked cells and incomplete chains; the illustration laws are excluded from R1, R4, R5; a text with M = 0 is a non-rejection (the unconditional rate is the key-mean level times N_eff/N; a cell with N_eff = 0 is flagged "no detection possible" and enters no rule); degenerate marker cells enter no rule.

## 10. Stop and pivot rules, and what a null result means
If the validation projects more than the 6-hour budget, the grid is reduced in the pre-registered order (K 500 → 300; N 4,000 → 2,000; drop γ = 0.25; drop L = 2,048) before the lock, never after it; a reduction changes the precision (K = 300: 4.1% relative error of an SD; N = 2,000: the finite-text term doubles, so fewer cells reach the theory share of 0.75) and the eligibility counts are recomputed before the lock. If R2 raises the audit flag, the run stops and the implementation is audited in a new version (the failed version is kept). If R1 fails, the paper's theory section reports the predictor's range of validity from the inconsistent cells (by σ_δ, M, rule and law), and Study 2 uses the empirically calibrated form; the research question stays answerable by measurement. R4 or R5 failing refutes the corresponding hypothesis for the simulated laws, which is itself a result (the gate states them as hypotheses). There is no pivot of the project on Study 1's outcome.
