## Q1 Null and what is random
Observed. The pseudorandom variable is a hash of the context window and a secret key (p.2): "generated by a cryptographic hash function A that takes a private Key and the recent context window" / "w(t−m):(t−1) as input."

Observed. The i.i.d. assumption of prior work and its justification (p.2): "are theoretically grounded in a fundamental assumption that the pseudorandom variables ζt =" / "A(w(t−m):(t−1) , Key) are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) for t = m + 1, . . . , n. This" / "assumption is justified when the context window w(t−m):(t−1) is unique for every position t, since" / "the cryptographic design of the hash function ensures that its outputs behave as independent" / "uniform draws."

Observed. Footnote 2 (p.2) makes explicit that the randomness that justifies independence is the key: "The hash function is sensitive to its inputs, that is, A(w(t−m):(t−1) , Key) is independent of A(w(t′ −m):(t′ −1) , Key)" / "whenever the text windows differ, w(t−m):(t−1) ̸= w(t′ −m):(t′ −1) , for t ̸= t′ , as Key is randomly selected."

Observed. Null hypothesis and pivot (p.6): "Under the null hypothesis H0 (human-written text), wt and ζt are independent, by the pivotal" / "property, Yt follows a fixed null distribution denoted by µ0 , regardless of the distribution of wt ." The classical formulation (p.6): "H0 : Yt ∼ µ0 i.i.d.," — and the paper's own formulation conditions on the observed repetition pattern (p.9, eq. (3)): "H0 : Yt | (Πζ , ΠY ) ∼ µ0 ," with "The notation Yt | (Πζ , ΠY ) indicates that the joint distribution of (Yt )nt=1 follows the observed" / "repetition structure: indices within the same block of Πζ share the same pseudorandom variable," / "and those within the same sub-block of ΠY take on the same pivotal statistic."

Observed. Independence assumption across blocks (p.8): "Assumption 3.1 (Independence across blocks). For k ̸= k ′ and any i ∈ Ikζ and j ∈ Ikζ′ , ζi is" / "statistically independent of ζj , denoted as ζi ⊥ ζj ." Its justification (p.8): "This independence follows" / "from the input sensitivity of cryptographic hash functions: when the input contexts differ, the" / "resulting pseudorandom outputs—A(w(t−m):(t−1) , Key) and A(w(t′ −m):(t′ −1) , Key)—are statistically" / "independent [50]." Supplementary p.45: "The independence structure in Assumption 3.3 is" / "already justified by the sensitivity of hash functions and therefore does not require further verification."

Observed. Marginal uniformity under the null is retained (p.9, Remark 3.1): "although each Yt still marginally follows µ0 under H0 or µ1,Pt under" / "H1 when conditioning on (Πζ , ΠY ), their joint distribution no longer factorizes across t"; and for Gumbel-max (p.12): "Under the null H0 , this statistic still follows" / "Unif(0, 1), since repetition does not alter its marginal distribution."

Observed. Type I error statement (p.10, eq. (4)): "and γn,α is the (1 − α) quantile of Sn under H0 , ensuring Type I error control: P0 (Sn ≥ γn,α ) = α." and "In practice, γn,α can be estimated via simulation, since the dependence structure of Y1:n is fully" / "characterized by the partitions (Πζ , ΠY ), and each Yt marginally follows µ0 under the null."

Observed. Footnote 3 (p.9): "For theoretical analysis, we assume that P1:n is fixed but unknown."

Reader's inference. What is random under H0 is the pseudorandom variables ζ (through the key: "as Key is randomly selected"), treated as exactly independent uniform draws across distinct context windows; the text is conditioned on (the NTP distributions are "fixed but unknown" and the repetition pattern (Πζ, ΠY) is conditioned on). The Type I guarantee P0(Sn ≥ γn,α) = α is therefore a statement about P0 = the distribution induced by the (random) key for the given text pattern, i.e. marginal over the key. No statement conditions on a fixed realised key.

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure
Observed. Motivating Figure 1, right panel (p.3): "Right: Inflation of type I error when the null" / "human-written data contains repetition, evaluated at significance level α = 0.05." Setup (p.80): "To verify this, we generate 1000 sequences of 1000 tokens each using the OPT-1.3B model with" / "temperature 0.1, without applying any watermarking." Observed: the plotted Type I error for raw statistics rises to roughly 0.055-0.060 at length 1000 against the 0.05 nominal (axis labels p.3: "0.0600", "0.0575", "0.0550", "0.0525", "0.0500").

Observed. Synthetic study (p.20): "fail to control Type I error: at α = 0.01, their empirical errors (light curves) hover around 0.03, well" / "above the nominal level. This inflation arises because repeated pivotal statistics are double-counted" (p.21) "as independent evidence, inflating the effective sample size and understating variance. In our setup," / "about 15%–20% of the data is repeated on average." After deduplication (p.21): "these methods" / "(darker curves) control Type I error well, with only natural random fluctuation."

Observed. Real data (p.22): "We evaluate Type I" / "errors using 2000 human-written samples from the C4 news-like dataset [42]." Result (p.22): "Across most scenarios, all detection methods" / "maintain Type I errors between 0.006 and 0.014, closely aligning with the nominal 0.01 level. This" / "result is consistent with expectations, as the deduplicated pivotal statistics can be regarded as i.i.d.," / "allowing conventional detection methods to remain effective — a phenomenon also observed in" / "[14, 50]." Details (p.79): "This procedure is repeated until" / "we collect 2000 sequences, each containing 500 pivotal statistics."

Observed. Hash and seed in the simulation (p.78): "We use a context window of size m = 7, so the pseudorandom" / "variable ζt = A(s(t−m):(t−1) , Key) depends on the preceding m tokens." and the PRNG: "This seed is then passed to a pseudorandom" / "number generator, for which we use the PCG-64 generator [38], the default in Python’s NumPy" / "package [17]."

Observed. Critical values (p.78): "For the Gumbel-max watermark under score functions hars and" / "hlog , the sum of score values follows a gamma distribution. In this case, the critical values can be" / "obtained directly from the gamma (1 − α) quantile." and "This procedure is repeated 4000 times, and the empirical" / "(1 − α) quantile of these values serves as an estimate."

Observed. Number of keys used: not found (searched: key, Key, seed, keys). The text always refers to "the key Key" in the singular.

Reader's inference. The empirical FPR is computed over null texts (2000 C4 documents; 1000 OPT-generated unwatermarked sequences; synthetic replicates), apparently under one key; the comparison with the nominal level is made at the level of this text-averaged rate; the only discrepancy explained is repetition-induced double counting.

## Q4 Remedies
Observed. Minimal units / discarding repeats (p.4): "the optimally derived rule reduces to discarding all repeated pivotal statistics in Y1:n , a form" / "that resonates with empirical heuristics proposed in [14, 50, 9]. Our optimal rule rigorously" / "controls Type I error and achieves detection power comparable to, and in some cases exceeding," / "existing methods, as shown in numerical experiments."

Observed. Justification (p.15): "A key implication" / "is that practical heuristics [14, 50, 9] that discard repeated pivotal statistics incur little information" / "loss, as the optimal rule itself follows this principle. Observed. Calibration of the threshold by simulation given the observed partition (p.10, quoted in Q1). ## Reader's inference
1. The Type I guarantee is marginal over the key: independence of ζ across distinct windows is asserted "as Key is randomly selected" (p.2) and "does not require further verification" (p.45); nothing is conditioned on a realised key.
2. The paper's dependence model is within-text (collisions of repeated windows under a single key); it strengthens the case that the i.i.d. pivot assumption fails for a given text, but says nothing about how P(reject | Key = k) varies with k.
3. The empirical FPR appears to be computed with a single key over many null texts (2000 C4 samples; 1000 OPT sequences), and the reported 0.006-0.014 band at α = 0.01 is a text-averaged, single-key figure.
4. The decisive quotation for the source gate is footnote 2, p.2: "whenever the text windows differ, w(t−m):(t−1) ̸= w(t′ −m):(t′ −1) , for t ̸= t′ , as Key is randomly selected."
