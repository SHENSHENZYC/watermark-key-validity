## Q2 Models and ties
- Observed: the comparison model is a logistic Bradley–Terry–Luce model on a low-rank task-by-model score matrix. p.7: "Conditional on Xi , the preference follows a BTL model (Bradley and Terry," [citation continues]. The matrix Θ⋆ ∈ R^{dt×dm} is assumed to have rank r, be µ-incoherent, bounded in sup-norm, and row-centred (pp.6–7); comparisons are sampled with a near-uniform task and pair sampling design (p.7).
- Observed: for the Arena study the rank is fixed by a spectral heuristic, p.30: "For the low-rank method, we set r = 3 based on the empirical singular value spectrum of this reference matrix, whose leading three singular values account for more than 94% of the total energy."
- Observed: the baseline is per-task BTL. p.26: "We compare against per-task Bradley-Terry (BTL), which fits each task row independently and uses the analogous Wald-type plug-in influence functions for its multiplier bootstrap."
- Observed, ties: the only tie treatment concerns ties between latent scores, not tied votes. p.8: "To ensure that each model has a unique rank even when two latent scores are exactly equal, we fix an arbitrary deterministic ordering ≺0 of the model labels and use it only to break such ties." No tie model for vote outcomes (searched: tie, Rao, Davidson — no hits beyond the latent-score tie-breaking rule).
- Reader's inference: the BT model is treated as the data-generating truth throughout; ties in the votes are outside the model.

## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation
- Observed: uncertainty is model-based and asymptotic. Score gaps are estimated by cross-fitted one-step estimators whose covariance is the semiparametric bound, p.16: "The variance Veff (Γ) is the resulting semiparametric efficiency bound: it is the smallest achievable asymptotic variance for regular estimators of ψΓ , accounting for the sampling design, the BTL Fisher information, and the low-rank constraint."
- Observed: joint dependence across gaps is handled by a Gaussian multiplier bootstrap of the estimated influence matrix (Algorithm 3, p.23), motivated on p.19: "Capturing this dependence is essential for multiple testing: treating correlated score-gap tests as independent can miscalibrate leaderboard-level uncertainty." Simultaneous bands are inverted into rank intervals and three-way top-K certificates, p.25: "In LLM evaluation, this three-way output: {certified top-K, certified non-top-K, unresolved} separates reliable leaderboard claims from comparisons that remain too noisy to certify."
- Observed: the inference theory assumes a randomized, known sampling design, p.17: "For the inference theory below, we consider a randomized comparison design in which the task and pair sampling law (ν, π) is specified by the randomization protocol and hence known by design"; the observational case is deferred: "An unknown observational design can be accommodated by estimating the design law from an auxiliary sample and plugging it into the Fisher operator, with an additional design-estimation error term in the analysis" (p.17).
- Observed: no term for BT misspecification, non-transitivity, voter or prompt heterogeneity enters any variance, band or certificate. The efficiency bound explicitly conditions on "the BTL Fisher information" (p.16).
- Reader's inference: misspecification is not propagated; the intervals are valid only if the logistic low-rank BTL model and the sampling assumptions hold. Arena's observational, non-uniform matchmaking is outside the stated theory except via the unimplemented design-estimation extension.

## Reader's inference
- This paper does not test whether BT fits the arena votes; it assumes the (low-rank) BTL model and builds efficient, dependence-aware rank certificates under it. The Arena experiment benchmarks the estimator against a per-task BT fit on all data, so it cannot detect BT lack of fit by construction.
- Its contribution relevant to the research question is methodological: a worked pipeline (influence functions, multiplier bootstrap, rank intervals, three-way top-K certificates) into which a misspecification-robust variance (e.g. sandwich or cluster-by-prompt) could be substituted. It leaves the lack-of-fit question open and does not mention it.
- The unspecified "preprocessing" of the 140K release (ties, both-bad, 81,150 of ~140K rows retained) is a reproducibility gap that a BT-adequacy study on the same data would need to resolve explicitly.
