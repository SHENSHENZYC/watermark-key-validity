## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

- Observed: no goodness-of-fit, lack-of-fit, likelihood-ratio, saturated-model, transitivity, cycle/triad, calibration or heterogeneity test (searched: goodness, "lack of fit", "likelihood ratio", saturated, transitiv, cycl, triad, Rao, Davidson, heterogene, misspec, "model fit", adequa, diagnos, calibrat, residual, chi-square, p-value). "diagnos" hits are reference titles (Belsley et al.; Pregibon). "homogene" hits (p.26) concern the composition of dropped matchups.
- Observed, what is tested instead: worst-case data-dropping sensitivity via the Approximate Maximum Influence Perturbation (AMIP, influence functions of the logistic-regression BT), with exact refit verification. Headline (p.1): "for instance, dropping just 0.003% of human preferences can change the top-ranked model on Chatbot Arena." Detail (p.6): "particular, we find that dropping just two (0.003% of) evaluations is enough to change the topranked model on Chatbot Arena from GPT-4-0125-preview to GPT-4-1106-preview;". Table 1 (p.8): human "2 out of 57477" (0.00348%), LLM-judge "9 out of 49938" (0.0180%); MT-bench human needs 92/3,355 (2.74%). Verification (p.6): "As a result, all non-robustness reported in this paper is definitive:"; caveat "However, the algorithm may not catch all cases of non-robustness (i.e., false negatives are possible)."
- Observed, contrast with random dropping (p.18): "Even Chatbot Arena (human-judge), which is the least stable under uniform subsampling, maintains its top-ranked model in 77% of random 1% deletions,".
- Observed, interpretation offered (p.10): "Sensitivity to worst-case data-dropping is often indicative of low signal-to-noise in the underlying data (Broderick et al., 2020);" and "time, our findings may suggest that apparent leaderboard differences may be artifacts of noise in the evaluation process rather than genuine performance gaps". (p.29): "One possible explanation for this fragility is that top competitors are often closely matched, making it difficult for annotators to reliably separate their performance on the prompts submitted to the" arena. The two influential votes are losses of GPT-4-1106-preview to much lower-ranked models (p.9): "In both cases, GPT-4-1106-preview was judged to have lost against substantially lowerranked models: Vicuna-13b (ranked 43rd) and Stripedhyena-nous-7b (ranked 45th)." and (p.29) "Thus, one might interpret the influential subsets the method identifies as “outlier” preferences".
- Observed, explicit note that prompt content is not modelled (p.27): "Additionally, we note that the BT model’s estimation procedure does not use any information about prompt content; it depends only on the BT scores of the two models involved in each match, which means it is possible for dropped subsets to be non-unique."
- Reader's inference: AMIP is a finite-sample influence/robustness diagnostic of the fitted BT ordering, not a test of whether the BT likelihood fits the pairwise win frequencies; it would flag fragility even under a perfectly specified BT with small true gaps, and it would not detect, for example, intransitive aggregate win-rates if the top gap were large.

## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation

- Observed: LMArena's 95% bootstrap confidence intervals and the CI-based set-valued rank R_m = 1 + Σ 1{inf C_m′ > sup C_m} (Eq. 9, p.14) are reproduced: "Thus, a bootstrap-based “rank” corresponds often to a set of statistically indistinguishable models, rather than a single model." Figure 3 (p.15): "95% Bootstrap-confidence-interval-based rankings on Chatbot Arena (Human Judge)."
- Observed, result (p.7): "Even with the bootstrap-based rankings, we still find arenas to be surprisingly sensitive to worst-case data-dropping." Table 2 (p.14): Chatbot Arena human "29 out of 57477" (0.0510%) changes the bootstrap top-1 set. Conclusion (p.15): "This result shows that AMIP-based non-robustness is not an artifact of ignoring statistical uncertainty captured by confidence intervals. Rather, even after incorporating bootstrap variability, the arenas continue to be AMIP sensitive."
- Observed, conceptual separation (p.16–17): "the confidence intervals measure sampling uncertainty, while worst-case data-dropping robustness examines whether the conclusion is driven by a very small fraction of the observations in the sample."
- Observed, assumption critique (p.17): "Bootstrapbased confidence intervals rely on the data being i.i.d. draws from a target population. Real-world preference datasets often depart from this regime due to differences in annotators (e.g., the same, or similar types of, annotators may annotate several prompts on LMArena), resulting prompt-selection biases, and various other potential context-based factors." and "AMIP, by contrast, does not require an i.i.d. assumption and therefore remains valid in settings where classical resampling tools do not apply reliably."
- Reader's inference: the paper asserts, without testing, that annotator and prompt-selection dependence may invalidate the i.i.d. bootstrap; misspecification is not quantified or propagated into any interval, and no alternative interval is proposed.

## Reader's inference

- This paper tests robustness of the Arena-style weighted BT ordering to worst-case deletion of a handful of votes, on five public LMArena releases, and shows the LMArena bootstrap-CI ranking is also fragile. It does not test whether BT fits the votes: no saturated-model comparison, tie model, transitivity check or homogeneity test. It is complementary to, not a pre-emption of, the specification-test question.
- Two of its remarks are direct motivation for our question and are stated without evidence: that bootstrap intervals assume i.i.d. votes while annotators and prompt selection induce dependence (p.17), and that apparent leaderboard gaps may be noise artifacts (p.10). A formal lack-of-fit/heterogeneity test would adjudicate exactly these claims.
- Its dataset list (arena-human-preference-55k, chatbot-arena-llm-judges, search-arena-24k, webdev-arena-preference-10k, VisionArena-Battle) and the wWL = 2, wT = 1 tie convention are a ready-made battery and baseline for a test paper; its finding that influential votes are "outlier" losses to far-weaker models is a candidate alternative to BT (heavy-tailed or contaminated link) that a specification test could formalise.
