## Q1 Null and what is random
Observed. The key is introduced once, as the device that reconstructs the pseudo-random sequence (p.2): "be reconstructed using the generated text together with shared secret information, referred to as the watermark key." The one-token model (p.6) treats the pseudo-random vector as a random variable: "generality assume that ζ is uniformly distributed on Z = [0, 1]K . Here K is" the vocabulary size; hypotheses (p.6): "H0 : (W, ζ) is independent;" / "H1 : W = S(P, ζ)." The pivot Y = U_W is (p.6) "which is uniformly distributed on [0, 1] under the null hypothesis. Therefore," / "the distribution of Y under UP does not depend on P, although the distribution" of (W, ζ) does. The validity assumption (Assumption 1, p.9): "(i) under the null hypothesis, Yt is uniformly distributed on [0, 1] and independent of Ft−1 ;" and "Condition (ii) does not matter for the validity (type-I error control) of our" / "method, but it affects the power of the method." In the simplest case (p.9) "(i) means that Yt , t ∈ T are iid uniformly distributed on [0, 1]. The special case" is the Gumbel-max watermark. Theorem 1 (p.10): the product of predictable calibrators "is an unbiased e-process (also a martingale), where ft ∈ Φt for each t. In" / "particular, under the null hypothesis," / "P sup Mt ≥ 1/α ≤ α for all α ∈ (0, 1)." The proof (p.21) rests on exact uniformity: "where we have used that Yt is independent of Ft−1 and that ft is determined by" / "Y1 , . . . , Yt−1 and it integrates to 1. Moreover, it is clear that Et is nonnegative."

Reader's inference. Under H0 the random objects are the token W_t and the pseudo-random vector ζ_t jointly, with ζ_t modelled as a fresh exact Unif[0,1]^K draw at every step; the key enters only as the mechanism that regenerates ζ_t. The Type I bound in Theorem 1 is therefore a statement marginal over the pseudo-randomness (equivalently, over a uniformly drawn key), not a statement conditional on one realised key. Nothing in Assumption 1 or Theorem 1 conditions on a key, and the paper does not discuss what happens when ζ_t is a deterministic hash of a fixed key and the context (repeated contexts giving identical ζ_t, or key-specific departures of Y_t from uniformity).

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure
Observed. Simulation (p.17): "vocabulary W of size 1,000 and assess Type II, Type I and sequential Type I" / "errors throughout 1,000 repeated experiments. Here, sequential Type I errors" are computed by sequential monitoring. NTP model (p.17): "In our simulation, we model LLM’s NTP distributions as spike distributions: we set its largest probability as 1 − ∆t , where ∆t is iid sampled from" U(0.001, δ), with δ = 0.2 and 0.5 (Fig. 1, p.18). Null data (p.17): "(sequential) Type I error rates on unwatermarked text, and Type II error rates" / "on text generated from the same model with the Gumbel-max watermark. The" curves are in Fig. 1. LLM experiment (p.17): "methods for text sequences generated by the LLM OPT-1.3B, following the experimental setup in prior works (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025b)." with "II error curves are evaluated based on 500 independent repeated experiments." at temperatures 0.5 and 1 (Fig. 2, p.19). Headline finding (p.17): "1. Only the e-process methods demonstrate robust Type I error control in" / "both fixed-sample and sequential testing setups. In contrast, the sumbased methods can only provide (asymptotic) Type I error control in the" non-sequential setup.
- Nominal level: the text does not state α for the experiments; the Fig. 2 axis labels (p.19) include the tick "0.05".
- Number of keys: not stated. Prompt corpus for OPT-1.3B: not stated beyond "following the experimental setup in prior works". Nature of the LLM null texts (human vs unwatermarked model): not stated.

Reader's inference. In the simulation each of the 1,000 repetitions draws fresh iid uniform ζ_t, so the reported Type I error is an average over the pseudo-randomness (over keys), not the error rate for one fixed key. For the OPT-1.3B experiment the paper does not say how ζ_t was produced or whether one key was used, so no per-key false-positive rate can be read off.

## Reader's inference
The paper's validity statement is exact and anytime-valid, but it is exact only on average over the pseudo-random vector ζ_t, which it models as iid uniform; the key is never a conditioning variable. Empirical Type I error is averaged over 1,000 (simulation) or 500 (OPT-1.3B) repetitions with no statement about how many keys were used. The question of the false-positive rate for one deployed key, or its dispersion across keys, is neither posed nor answered here.
