## Q2 Models and ties

- Observed, model (p.1): "Under a heterogeneous Bradley–Terry model with fixed inverse temperature"; (p.2) "and task, comparisons follow a BT model with fixed known parameter β > 0, and we assume that repeated comparisons of two models within a context are conditionally independent BT draws"; (p.2) "we do not place any assumptions on the relationship between the individual utility functions across different users as well as the utilities across tasks or models for the same user." Experiments (p.48): "We use β = 1 throughout." and "We don’t distinguish between users and tasks, simply modeling them as contexts c."
- Observed, ties (p.3): "For simplicity, we assume no ties between models, i.e., ∆ > 0." Reader's inference: this is a tie in population-average utility, not a tied vote. Tied votes are excluded from the semi-synthetic fit ("votes with a decisive winner", p.49). No tie model is fitted.
- Observed, per-cluster estimator (p.49): "regularized Bradley–Terry model" (regularisation form and strength not stated).
- Not found: Rao–Kupper, Davidson, any explicit tie-probability model.

## Reader's inference

- Not a competitor on the adequacy-testing question. The arena release is used as a seed for a simulation, and no fitted model is checked against the votes.
- Its theoretical message is nonetheless supportive of our motivation: under heterogeneous contexts, the sign of E[σ(βL)] − 1/2 (what a pooled BT fit estimates) can differ from the sign of E[L] (the average-utility ranking), so pooled BT win probabilities can be systematically misleading even with unlimited votes. That is a reason to test homogeneity on real arena data, which this paper does not do.
- Screening leads surfaced by its related-work section: Pukdee, Balcan and Ravikumar (2026, "What does preference learning recover from pairwise comparison data?"), Gölz, Haghtalab and Yang (2025, "Distortion of AI alignment"), Haghtalab et al. (2026, "Pluralistic leaderboards"), Aouad et al. (2026, "The sign estimator"), Frick et al. (2025, "Prompt-to-leaderboard") (reference list, p.13). None is described as running a specification test on arena votes.
