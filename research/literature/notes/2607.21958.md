## Q1 Null and what is random
Observed (p.8): "Let K denote the watermark key, and let ζt be the pseudo-random variable" / "associated with step t, generated from the key and the preceding context. An authorized" / "detector can reconstruct ζt from the observed text and the shared key. The watermarking" LLM samples W_t ∼ S(P_t, ζ_t). Hypotheses (p.8): "H0 : Wt ⊥⊥ ζt | Ft−1 ," / "H1 : Wt ∼ S(Pt , ζt )." Interpretation (p.8): "Under H0 , the pseudo-random variable does not influence token generation; equivalently," / "conditional on the past, the token is generated independently of ζt ." and (p.9) "Under H0 , the token generation satisfies PH0 (Wt = w | Ft−1 , ζt ) = Pt (w), w ∈" V. Gumbel-max instantiation (p.11): "Given the watermark key K and the current generation context," / "we generate a pseudo-random vector ζt = (Ut,w )w∈V , where Ut,w ∼ Unif(0, 1) for w ∈ V." The pivot Y_t = U_{t,W_t} and Z_t = −log(1 − Y_t) satisfy "Zt | Gt−1 ∼ Exp(1) under H0 ." (p.11), with G_t the reduced filtration σ(Z_1,…,Z_t). Validity theorem (p.12): "Theorem 3.1. Suppose that under H0 , we have Zt | Gt−1 ∼ Exp(1). Then, for every fixed" / "λ ∈ (0, 1), the process {Mt (λ)}t≥0 defined in (2) is an e-process with respect to {Gt }t≥0" under H0.

Reader's inference. The key K is named, but the null then models the key-derived vector ζ_t as fresh iid Unif(0,1) draws at every step, and Theorem 3.1 takes the exact Exp(1) null of Z_t as its hypothesis. The guarantee is thus marginal over the pseudo-randomness (over the key), not conditional on a realised key; the exact-null hypothesis is assumed rather than derived for a deterministic hash of (key, context).
