## Q2 Models and ties

Observed: BT in strength (BT-S) and utility (BT-U) parametrisations (p.3); the general pairwise-comparison framework of Han et al. (2023) with outcome set A: "Common choices of A include A = {−1, 1} for binary outcomes, A = {−1, 0, 1} to include ties, and A = R for cardinal outcomes." (p.3) Tie models are named explicitly: the framework covers "the Rao–Kupper (Rao and Kupper, 1967) and Davidson (Davidson, 1970) models for ties, the more general ordinal models (Tutz, 1986; Agresti, 1992) and the paired cardinal model (Shah et al., 2016)." (p.4) SST is stated for these models with p_ij defined as "the probability that object i defeats object j with ties split equally." (p.4) Also covered: Plackett–Luce (p.4), BT mixtures (p.5), covariate-assisted/PlusDC models (pp.5–6), the BT reward model (BT-RM) for RLHF (p.6), and BT for LLM evaluation: "BT models also arise in LLM evaluation, but with a different choice of objects. Here, the objects are the LLMs themselves, and each comparison records a preference between responses generated by two models to the same prompt (Zheng et al., 2023; Chiang et al., 2024)." (p.6) "The standard BT model therefore provides a baseline for ranking LLMs, while extensions can incorporate richer comparison outcomes and covariate information (Ameli et al., 2025; Frick et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026)." (p.6)

Observed (Elo link, p.17): "For example, the classical Elo update can be interpreted as an online stochastic gradient step for maximizing the BT log-likelihood (Király and Qian, 2017; Olesker-Taylor and Zanetti, 2024)." (p.17)

## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

Observed: the survey contains no goodness-of-fit or lack-of-fit section and reports no test results (0 hits for goodness, lack of fit, saturated, triad). What it says about adequacy:

- SST testing: "In practice, the validity of the SST assumption can be assessed via hypothesis testing (Makur and Singh, 2025a,b). However, the universal applicability of the SST assumption has been questioned." (p.4)
- Limitations (Section 2.5, p.7): "This assumption is restrictive and fails in settings such as Rock–Paper–Scissors, where cyclic preferences are intrinsic. In such cases, no latent score vector can reproduce the underlying pairwise preference probabilities, and the BT model is therefore misspecified." (p.7) Degrees-of-freedom framing: "A pairwise comparison probability matrix on n objects has n(n − 1)/2 free parameters." (p.7), with BT "reducing the number of free parameters to n − 1." (p.7); remedies cited are matrix-completion-type models (Massimino and Davenport, 2013; Levy et al., 2021; Chen and Li, 2024; Zhang and Chen, 2025; Lee and Chen, 2025) and counting methods (Borda) (p.7). For LLM reward models it points to "Munos et al. (2024); Chakraborty et al. (2024); Liu et al. (2026); Pukdee et al. (2026)" for "representational limitations of BT-based reward models for LLMs" (p.7).
- Likelihood-ratio inference: "For example, hypothesis testing for high-dimensional utility parameters can be addressed through the likelihood-ratio framework of Yan et al. (2025)." (p.15) Reader's inference: this is a test on utility parameters, not a model-adequacy test.
- Dependence/heterogeneity caveats: "In real-world networks, however, the comparison structure and observed outcomes may be coupled." (p.7); "In real-world applications, comparison data often exhibit heterogeneity across different contexts, populations, or time periods." (p.5); "While (PlusDC) focuses on object and context features, it does not account for heterogeneous subject effects (Schauberger and Tutz, 2019)." (p.6) followed by "However, we omit this additional complexity in subsequent discussion, as many of the corresponding statistical foundations remain largely open." (p.6) Rank-breaking is described as fitting "independent observations arising from a (misspecified) BT model." (p.11)

## Q5 Limitations and future work

- "A fundamental open problem in the statistical theory of the BT model is the development of a unified asymptotic theory (e.g., uniform consistency of the MLE) that applies to heterogeneous comparison graphs." (p.21)
- "Furthermore, the methodology for conducting statistical inference in covariate-assisted BT models, such as the PlusDC model, is largely undeveloped." (p.21)
- Mixtures: efficient algorithms with convergence guarantees and statistical theory still needed (p.21).
- LLMs: "A natural next step is to systematically investigate alternative pairwise comparison models and assess whether their greater flexibility can improve training efficiency, model performance, and the representation of human preferences." (p.21)
- Subject heterogeneity: "many of the corresponding statistical foundations remain largely open." (p.6)
- Not found: any statement that goodness-of-fit testing of BT on arena data (or any data) is an open problem (searched: goodness, fit, test, future, open).

## Reader's inference

Reader's inference: the survey is a map, not a test. It confirms that (i) tie models (Rao–Kupper, Davidson) and heterogeneity extensions (mixtures, covariates) exist within the BT family, (ii) SST adequacy can in principle be tested (Makur & Singh 2025a,b), (iii) BT is known to be misspecified under cyclic preferences and its reward-model variant is under scrutiny in the LLM-alignment literature, and (iv) Chatbot Arena is a connected-graph BT application with a public 140k-vote release. It reports no lack-of-fit test on any dataset, none on arena votes, and treats all rank uncertainty as model-based. Its citation list (Makur & Singh; Yan et al.; Lee & Chen; Zhang & Chen; Liu, Lu & Sun; Pukdee et al.; Ameli et al.; Frick et al.; Xu et al.) is the list to screen next for a direct competitor.
