## Q2 Models and ties

Observed:
- Core model (p.3): "We model the vote conditionally on the prompt and model pair as following a Bradley-Terry (BT) model [6]:" P(Y = 1 | X = x, Z = z) = σ(xᵀθ*(z)). p.3: "P2L is a strict generalization of marginal BT regression." Motivation (p.3): "P2L can be substantially more powerful than marginal BT regression due to heterogeneity in the prompt-conditional performance of different language models."
- Fitting: an instruction-tuned LLM (SmolLM2 135M/360M, Qwen2.5 0.5B–7B) with its LM head replaced by a coefficient head, trained by SGD on the negative log-likelihood; p.8: "We always train for 1 epoch."
- Ties (p.7): "As one example, we will consider incorporating ties via a Rao-Kupper [32] model." with a prompt-dependent threshold: "The larger this prompt-dependent tie coefficient, the more likely the two models are to tie."
- Both-bad votes (p.7): "Finally, we consider how to handle the “Tie (both bad)” category. For this, we developed a non-standard statistical model which we call the grounded Rao-Kupper model." p.7: "we imagine the existence of a fictitious “bad” model with a coefficient of zero, and use this as a grounding point for the model coefficients." p.7: "This model allows us to make efficient use of all data collected on Chatbot Arena by incorporating all votes." (eq. 8). Deployed routers used it (p.9): "We deployed the grounded Rao-Kupper versions of P2L-0.5B, P2L-1.5B, P2L-3B, and P2L-7B onto Chatbot Arena".
- Aggregation over a prompt distribution refits a logistic model to simulated win rates (p.4): "The win rate above no longer follows a simple logistic model, but we can fit another logistic model to match it:".
- Statistical lineage (p.14): "Our work builds on classic log-linear models and GLMs, like those of Bradley and Terry [6], Rao and Kupper [32]; see [26] for a review, and [3] for further extensions that enrich this model class for better LLM ranking." ([3] = Ameli, Zhuang, Stoica, Mahoney, arXiv:2412.18407.) p.14: "The closest piece of work to ours is Hastie and Tibshirani [16], which proposes varying-coefficient models. P2L can be seen as a subclass of varying-coefficient models."

## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

Observed:
- Formal goodness-of-fit, lack-of-fit, likelihood-ratio, saturated-model, transitivity or cycle test: not found.
- The evidence offered against the marginal BT is predictive, on held-out votes (p.9): "We then measure the negative log-likelihood (validation loss) on this dataset; a lower validation loss indicates better preference prediction performance." Result (p.9): "Notably, the gap between the best P2L leaderboard and the marginal model is several times the gap between the marginal leaderboard and the constant predictor." Interpretation (p.9): "This indicates that by capturing the prompt-dependent differences in model performance, P2L is able to produce much better predictions of human preference." Figure 2 (p.8): "The P2L models all substantially outperform the baselines, and performance scales with dataset and model size." Figure 2's axes show validation loss roughly between 1.15 and 1.35 and MSE roughly between 0.42 and 0.55; the exact values are not tabulated, and no test statistic or p-value is reported.
- Heterogeneity is illustrated through P2L predictions, not tabulated votes (Figure 6, p.12): "The results show strong category-specific variability in performance; for example, o1-mini is substantially better than GPT-4o-2024-05-13 in “Arithmetic Operations and Calculations” but substantially worse when asked to write a “Suspenseful Horror Story”."
- Aggregation check against per-category marginal BT fits (Figure 7, p.13): "The L1 distance plateaus at the optimal performance, which is around 0.025/0.015." p.13: "In contrast to marginal regression, which requires thousands of prompts for a stable leaderboard, P2L converges near this optimal value within 100-250 prompts (Figure 7)."
- Assumptions are stated, not tested (p.6): "It is important to note that deviations from the Bradley-Terry model—for example, any non-transitivity—will break this relationship." Theorem 1 (p.5): "Assume that for every prompt z, the BradleyTerry model holds with coefficients θ∗ (z)." p.4: "If θ∗ exists, this technique is perfect, in that it recovers the exact same BT coefficients that we would have obtained by observing an infinite population of prompts from Q."
- Out-of-distribution check uses LiveBench ground truth, not votes (Section 3.6, p.13; Table 1, p.24).

## Reader's inference

- Reader's inference: P2L is itself a very flexible alternative to the marginal BT (a varying-coefficient BT with prompt-dependent strengths and a prompt-dependent Rao–Kupper tie threshold), and the validation-loss gap is informal evidence that the marginal BT is misspecified by prompt heterogeneity; but it is not framed as, nor accompanied by, a lack-of-fit or likelihood-ratio test, and no statistic, degrees of freedom or p-value is reported.
- Reader's inference: the tie treatment here (Rao–Kupper plus the "grounded" both-bad extension) is the most developed among the four papers, yet its adequacy relative to plain BT, half-win coding or Davidson-type models is not examined.
- Reader's inference: all uncertainty is inherited from the arena's leaderboard pipeline; the paper does not quantify how prompt heterogeneity should widen the marginal leaderboard's rank intervals.
- Reader's inference on relevance: strong prior evidence that heterogeneity across prompts matters on arena votes, but the formal specification-testing question remains open.
