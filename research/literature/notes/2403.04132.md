## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

Observed:
- Goodness-of-fit, lack-of-fit, likelihood-ratio or saturated-model test of BT on the votes: not found.
- Transitivity / cycles: treated only theoretically in Appendix B. p.15: "We next consider a nonparametric extension of the Bradley-Terry (BT) model (Bradley & Terry, 1952) to the case where the ranking is not necessarily transitive." p.16: "Thus, if the parametric BT model is well-specified, the nonparametric version will exactly recover the Bradley-Terry coefficients." p.16: "it will retain statistical validity even if Ht is not binary, if the win rate is non-transitive, and if the logistic model assumed by the BT model is misspecified." Appendix B contains no computation on arena data and reports no comparison between the nonparametric score and the parametric BT fit.
- Heterogeneity across prompt topics (Table 2, p.6): GPT-4-0613's win rate against Llama-2-70b-chat on 30 prompts per cluster ranges from 96.7% (Python Game Programming Challenge) to 53.3% (Movie Recommendations & Ratings), but p.6: "To control variables, we factor out user votes and consider LLM-as-judge (Zheng et al., 2023b) to evaluate model response." Conclusion drawn (p.6): "This result shows models may exhibit varying strengths in different areas". No homogeneity test of BT coefficients across categories, languages or users is reported.
- Interval coverage check (p.7): "A natural followup question is whether or not the intervals are doing their job correctly: whether they cover the true BT coefficients with probability at least (and almost exactly) 1 − α." p.8: "this cannot be evaluated on real data, so we run a simulation." The simulation assumes BT is true (p.8): "A vector of BT coefficients is drawn, with each coordinate sampled i.i.d. from a distribution beta(1/γ, 1/γ)"; result (p.8): "The coverage of the intervals behaves as expected, centering around 1 − α, regardless of the number of models."
- Vote-quality check, not a model test (p.7): "we observe high agreement rates (72% to 83%) between Arena crowd-user and experts in both setup." (160 battles relabelled by two experts and GPT-4; Tables 3–4.)
- Anomalous-user test, a test of voter behaviour against the historical vote distribution, not of the BT fit (p.5): "Under the null hypothesis that HA′i is exchangeable with Hi′ , pi is a valid p-value (see Appendix C for a proof)." Combined by Fisher's method with a Bonferroni-type correction; Table 5 (p.8), 25 anomalous vs 25 normal users: "We find the detection method effective (e.g., reaching 90% true positive and 60-70% true negative rate)."
- Active sampling evaluated by simulation from the fitted BT on the 213,576-vote holdout (p.8): "To estimate θ∗ to a precision of 0.2, random needs 6,800 samples and adaptive needs 4,400 samples".

## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation

Observed:
- p.5: "To compute confidence intervals on the BT coefficients, we employ two strategies: (1) the pivot bootstrap (DiCiccio & Efron, 1996), and (2) the “sandwich” robust standard errors outlined in Huber et al. (1967)"; p.5: "we choose to deploy the sandwich intervals due to their smaller size in large samples."
- Rank intervals (p.5): "To get the uniform confidence set, we construct the chi-squared interval implied by the central limit theorem using the sandwich estimate of the variance." Guarantee (p.5): "with high probability, no model’s performance is understated." Figure 5 (p.7): "The multiplicity correction, in this case a chi-square CLT interval, is technically required for the purpose of calculating the ranking, because it ensures all scores are simultaneously contained in their intervals" (both corrected and uncorrected intervals are reported).
- Replay comparison (p.15, Figure 13): "The sandwich intervals, though larger in small samples, are more stable, and in large samples, they actually become smaller."
- Propagation of misspecification: the sandwich covariance is adopted with the Huber/White justification (p.4), but no misspecification is detected or measured; coverage is validated only under a correctly specified BT generating process (p.8). No adjustment of rank intervals for lack of fit appears.

## Reader's inference

- Reader's inference: this paper fits a binary BT model by inverse-probability-weighted logistic MLE and never tests its fit; the only adequacy-related material is Appendix B's nonparametric path-sum score, which is argued to remain valid under non-transitivity but is never computed on data, so no cycle, triad or stochastic-transitivity statistic exists here.
- Reader's inference: the sandwich (Huber–White) covariance is the paper's implicit hedge against misspecification, but since coverage is checked only in a BT-true simulation, the paper gives no evidence on interval behaviour when BT is wrong; the stated rank uncertainty therefore does not incorporate any measured lack of fit.
- Reader's inference: Table 2 is suggestive of prompt heterogeneity, but because it uses GPT-4-turbo as judge rather than the crowd votes, it is not evidence about BT homogeneity on the vote data.
- Reader's inference: tie / both-bad handling in the deployed fit is unspecified in this version; the FastChat rating code (cited by 2504.20879 p.23) would need to be read to pin it down.
- Reader's inference on relevance: the arena's own methods paper reports no goodness-of-fit, transitivity or heterogeneity test on the votes, so it leaves the research question open rather than answering it.
