## Q1 Null and what is random

Observed.
- H0/H1 (p.2): "The detection tests the hypothesis H0 : “the text is natural” (human written or written without watermark), against H1 : “the text has been generated with watermark”."
- Z-test null moments (p.2): "where µ0 and σ0 are the expectation and standard deviation per token under the null hypothesis H0 , i.e. when the analyzed text is not watermarked." And: "The Z-test is typically used for large sample sizes assuming a normal distribution under the null hypothesis thanks to the central limit theorem."
- Decision rule (p.2): "At detection time, we fix a false positive rate (FPR) and flag the text as watermarked if p-value(z) < FPR."
- Binomial test for the green-list scheme (p.3): "Under H0 , we assume that the event x(t) ∈ Gk(t) occurs with probability γ, and that these events are i.i.d. Therefore, ST (2) is distributed as a binomial of parameters T and γ."
- Gamma test for the Gumbel scheme (p.3): "Under H0 , we assume that the text under scrutiny and the secret vector are independent, so" [i.i.d.] "that rx(t) ∼ U(0, 1). Therefore, ST (3) follows a Γ(T, 1)" distribution. Appendix (p.7): "Proof. Under H0 , the assumption is s.t. Rt ∼ U[0,1] . Then, − ln(1 − Rt ) follows an exponential distribution E(1)." Variance proof (p.8): "The results comes because the sampled tokens are independent."
- Key randomness is explicit in the distortion statements, which average over keys (p.2): "On the other hand, E[F (p, K)] = p so on expectation over the set of cryptographic keys, watermarking does not bias the global distribution of words (K being the random variable representing the key)." (p.2): "It also presents the interesting property that ∀v ∈ V, P(X (0) = v) = pv over the randomness of the secret vector, when distributed uniformly over [0, 1]|V| (demonstration in App. A)." (p.7): "Yet, on expectation over the secret keys, ie. over r.v. R = (R1 , . . . , RV ), the distribution of the chosen token follows the distribution given by the LLM."
- Key construction (p.2): "A common practice ensuring self-synchronization - illustrated in Fig. 1 - makes the key dependent of the window of h previous tokens" ... "where H is a cryptographic hash function and k the master key. This secret is the seed that initializes a random number generator (RNG) at time t."

Reader's inference. The null is worded about the text ("the text is natural"), but the probability model under H0 makes the per-token secret (green-list membership; the uniform draw r_{x(t)}) the random object, i.i.d. across tokens and independent of the text. The binomial and gamma p-values are therefore marginal guarantees: the randomness is supplied by the key/hash and is implicitly averaged over keys. No statement conditions on one fixed master key, and no key-conditional FPR is defined. The independence assumption the paper repairs is within-text (repeated contexts), not between-key.

## Q2 Fixed key / per-key / key screening

Observed.
- "Key Management" (p.2): "The secret key k giving birth to the greenlist Gk in [17] or to the sampling of r in [16] must have a wide diversity. A fixed key causes security issues and biases the text generation." Also (p.2): "In the specific case where h = 0, the key is the same for all tokens (k (t) = k), which makes the watermarking particularly robust to text editing [25]." and "The width of this window defines a trade-off between diversity of the key and robustness of the watermarking."
- Several master keys are used, but pooled (p.3): "We repeat this with 10 different master keys, which makes 1M detection results under H0 for each method and h value." Fig. 2 caption (p.3): "Results are computed over 10 master keys × 100k sequences of 256 tokens sampled from Wikipedia."
- Residual miscalibration for the key-constant case even after de-duplication (p.4): "They now match perfectly, except for h = 0 where the FPR is still slightly underestimated."
- Per-key dispersion of the FPR, a deployed fixed key, per-key calibration, key screening or key choice: not found (searched: key, seed, fixed, screening, Kolmogorov, uniform, "false positive", FPR, repeat, n-gram/ngram, tuple, calibrat, deployed; see "Not found" below).

Reader's inference. "A fixed key" on p.2 means a key that does not change from token to token (the h = 0 case), and the stated concern is security and generation bias, not false positives. The 10 master keys are pooled into one empirical FPR curve, so Fig. 2 estimates the key-averaged (marginal) FPR; the authors had the data for per-key curves but did not report them. The h = 0 residual is the closest thing in the paper to a fixed-key effect: with h = 0 there is literally one green list for every position, and after removing repeated tokens the FPR is still "slightly underestimated", which is consistent with a key-specific shift of the null green fraction that de-duplication cannot remove.

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure

Observed.
- Scale, corpus, tokenizer, length (p.3): "So far, the FPR has been checked on only around 500 negative samples [17], [18], [20]. We scale this further and select 100k texts from multilingual Wikipedia to cover the distribution of natural text." "We tokenize with LLaMA’s tokenizer, and take T = 256 tokens/text. We run detection tests with varying window length h when seeding the RNG." "For the detection of the greenlist watermark, we use γ = 0.25."
- Keys and total null tests (p.3): 10 master keys × 100k texts = "1M detection results under H0 for each method and h value" (quoted in Q2). Context widths in the Fig. 2 legend: 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 (read from the rendered PDF page 3; the numbers are scattered in the txt).
- Z-test result (p.3): "Fig. 2a compares empirical and theoretical FPRs. Theoretical guarantees do not hold in practice: the empirical FPRs are much higher than the theoretical ones. We also observed that distributions of p-values were not uniform (which should be the case under H0 )." "Besides, the larger the watermarking context window h, the closer we get to theoretical guarantees. In pratice, one would need h >> 8 to get reliable p-values, but this makes the watermarking method less robust to attacks on generated text because it hurts synchronization."
- Fig. 2 caption (p.3): "Theoretical values do not hold in practice for Z-tests, even for high values of h, and empirical FPRs do not match theoretical ones. This is solved by basing detection on grounded statistical tests and analytic p-values, as well as by revising the scoring strategy."
- First explanation, the Gaussian approximation (p.3): "The Gaussian assumption of Z-tests breaks down for short or repetitive texts." Remedy: "Here are non-asymptotical tests for both methods that reduce the gap between empirical and theoretical FPR, especially at low FPR values as shown in Fig. 2."
- Second explanation, repeated contexts (p.3): "Even with grounded statistical tests, empirical FPRs are still higher than theoretical ones. In fact, Kirchenbauer et al. [17] mention that random variables are only pseudo-random since repeated windows generate the same secret." "This can happen even in a short text and especially in formatted data. For instance in a bullet list, the sequence of tokens \n\n*_ repeats a lot as shown in Fig. 3. Repetition pulls down the assumption of independence necessary for computing the p-values."
- Fig. 3 (p.3): the txt is garbled (fragments "text with low p-value because", "of repeated", "tokens."). Caption as read from the rendered PDF page 3 (not grep-verifiable): a typical vanilla text with p-value 10^-21 under the greenlist watermark with γ = 1/4 and h = 2 on 256 tokens.
- Proposed fix (p.3–4): "We experimented with two simple heuristics mitigating this issue at the detection stage. The first one takes into account a token only if the watermark context window has not already been seen during the detection." "The second scores the tokens for which the h + 1-tuple formed by {watermark context + current token} has not already been seen. Note, that the latter is present in [17], although without ablation and without being" (p.3) "used in further experiments. Of the two, the second one is better since it counts more ngrams, and thus has better TPR. It can also deal with the specific case of h = 0." (p.4)
- Reported effect (p.4): "Figure 2c reports empirical and theoretical FPRs when choosing not to score already seen h + 1-tuples. They now match perfectly, except for h = 0 where the FPR is still slightly underestimated." "In short, we guarantee FPR thanks to new statistical tests and by scoring only tokens for which {watermark context + current token} has not been scored."
- Cost of de-duplication in scored tokens (p.4): "For instance in our case, the average number of scored tokens is around 100 for h = 0, and 150 for h = 1 and h = 4."
- Operating point for the TPR experiments (p.4): "We flag a text as watermarked if its p-value is lower than 10−5 ensuring an FPR=10−5 ."

Reader's inference. Fig. 2 is a pooled empirical-vs-theoretical FPR plot reaching about 10^-6 (1M null tests), the largest null check among the four papers, but it reports only the key-averaged curve. "match perfectly" is a visual judgement on log-log axes without confidence bands; at the 10^-6 end a pooled count of about one event per key cannot resolve between-key differences.

## Q4 Remedies

Observed.
1. Exact non-asymptotic tests replacing the Z-test: binomial (p.3, eq. 6) and gamma (p.3, eq. 7), quoted in Q1.
2. De-duplicated scoring of unseen (h+1)-tuples (p.3–4), with the effect quoted in Q3.
3. Larger context width h improves calibration but costs robustness (p.3, "h >> 8" sentence in Q3).
4. Detection with LLM access (p.5): a Neyman-Pearson score with "There are two drawbacks: (1) detection needs the LLM to compute px(t) , (2) there is no close-form formula for the p-value." "This last point may be fixed by resorting to a Chernoff bound, yet without guarantee on its tightness:" and "Experiments show that this detection yields extremely low p-values for watermarked text, but they are fragile: any attack increases them to the level of the original detection scheme (3), or even higher because generated logits are sensitive to the overall LLM context."
5. Multi-key (multi-bit) detection with a multiplicity correction (p.5): "The global p-value becomes 1 − (1 − p)M , where M is the number of possible messages." (p.6): "A text is deemed watermarked if the score is above a threshold set for a given global FPR (see III). Then, the source is identified as the user with the lowest p-value."

Reader's inference. None of the remedies is a per-key calibration; they are distributional corrections (exact tests), dependence removal (de-duplication), a trade-off knob (h), or multiplicity corrections (multi-key). The only guarantee claimed is the matched empirical/theoretical FPR in Fig. 2c, pooled over keys.

## Reader's inference

1. Guarantee type: marginal over the key (the per-token secret is the random object, i.i.d. and independent of the text); no key-conditional statement anywhere.
2. Per-key dispersion: not studied; 10 master keys are pooled, which is exactly the averaging that hides a fixed-key FPR.
3. The h = 0 residual after de-duplication (p.4) is the paper's only observation consistent with a key-specific shift of the null green fraction, since h = 0 is the "same key for every token" case and repeated tokens were already removed.
4. The de-duplication fix is a within-text independence repair; it cannot address a bias that is common to all distinct (h+1)-grams under one key, which is the mechanism later named by 2512.16904 (A.3).
5. Measurement limits: 100k × 10 null tests at T = 256 give one expected event per key at 10^-6, so even a per-key re-analysis of these data could only resolve between-key dispersion down to about 10^-4.
