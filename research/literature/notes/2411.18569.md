## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation

- Observed, method (p.2): "To briefly sketch the method, it first forms a simultaneous confidence region for all m candidates and then simply projects this set along the winning coordinate ı̂ to obtain a confidence interval for the winner." Level (p.12): "We set the error level to be α = 0.1 throughout." Selection is exact (p.3): "We focus on exact, non-randomized selection of the winner."
- Observed, Arena results (p.15): "The zoom correction yields smaller intervals than both baselines, achieving a major improvement over the full union bound in all four examples." "Locally simultaneous inference leads to noticeable improvements over the union bound in the TorchVision evaluations and Bradley-Terry score estimation, but does not see much gain for win rate estimation."
- Observed, identity of the population winner (Section 5.2 extension, p.14): "Additionally, we apply the extension from Section 5.2 to form a confidence set for the identity of the population winner." Result (p.15): "it is able to identify the single best model on Chatbot Arena in terms of the Bradley-Terry score." Table 2 (p.16), Bradley-Terry score: zoom "{chatgpt-4o-latest}" versus simultaneous "{chatgpt-4o-latest, gemini-1.5-pro-api-0409-preview}"; the win-rate sets are larger (7 vs 10 models).
- Observed, what is propagated: selection bias (the winner's curse) and arbitrary dependence (union bound). Misspecification of BT is not mentioned or propagated.
- Reader's inference: the interval for the BT winner inherits any error in the normal approximation and in the BT model itself; if BT lacks fit, the marginal tail bound S(·) would be wrong and the coverage guarantee would not hold, a point the paper does not raise.

## Reader's inference

- This paper does not test BT adequacy on arena votes; it takes the fitted BT scores as logistic-regression coefficients with a normal error approximation and corrects the top model's interval for selection (winner's curse) while allowing arbitrary dependence through a union bound. The Chatbot Arena example is one of four illustrations; its data release is unspecified in the text.
- It is a downstream tool rather than a competitor: once a specification test has either validated BT or identified a better-fitting model, the zoom correction (or its variance-adaptive variant, Section 5.4) could be applied to the resulting winner, and a misspecification-robust (sandwich) variance would be the natural replacement for the model-based normal approximation they use.
- Relevant observed fact for our framing: even without selection correction they note that BT scores of different models are strongly dependent through shared battles (p.15); any adequacy test on arena data must likewise respect that dependence (e.g. pair-level cell counts rather than vote-level i.i.d. assumptions).
