## Q1 Null and what is random

Observed.
- Claim (p.1): "Detection of the watermark in a text is then performed through a theoretically grounded statistical test that provides rigorous guarantees over the false positive rates."
- Main-text null (p.4): "Under the null hypothesis H0 “not watermarked with this specific scheme”, the statistic should follow a known distribution, yielding a p-value for the probability of observing such a score or a more extreme score by chance." "We flag text as watermarked if the p-value is below a detection threshold α, which controls the false positive rate (FPR)." Binomial example (p.4): "this statistic follows under H0 a Binomial distribution with parameters N and γ (expected green ratio, typically 0.5), then p-value = P (X ≥ K | X ∼ Binomial(N, γ))."
- Appendix nulls are stated for the specific private key (p.17): "Under the null hypothesis H0 “the text is not watermarked with that scheme and private key s”, this score follows a binomial distribution." (p.18): "Under H0 “the text is not watermarked with that scheme and private key s”, S follows a Γ(N, 1) distribution." SynthID (p.18): "Under the null hypothesis H0 (unwatermarked text), the g-values are independent and identically distributed (e.g., uniform or Bernoulli)."
- Determinism given key and context (p.18): "Consequently, for a fixed context, the noise vector is deterministic." Hash design (p.17): "This construction ensures that small changes in any input (window, token, or key) produce uncorrelated outputs, satisfying the pseudo-randomness requirements for watermark security."
- The decisive passage, Appendix A.3 (p.19): "How to choose the secret key? As explained in section 3, valid detection requires p-values to be uniformly distributed under H0 ." "While this holds in expectation over all possible keys, fixing a specific key s creates preferences for certain (k + 1)-grams." "If these patterns align with natural language statistics, the expected green fraction under H0 shifts slightly (e.g., from 0.5 to 0.505), causing p-values to collapse toward zero on long texts and inflating false positive rates."

Reader's inference. This is the only one of the four papers that (i) writes the null for the specific private key s, i.e. conditionally, and (ii) states that uniformity of null p-values, hence the FPR guarantee, holds "in expectation over all possible keys" and fails for a fixed key through a shifted null green fraction whose effect grows with text length. The p-value is nevertheless still computed with the nominal γ under Binomial(N, γ); the per-key null is not re-calibrated, the key is instead chosen so that nominal and actual approximately coincide.

## Q2 Fixed key / per-key / key screening

Observed.
- Main text (p.4): "But deduplication alone may not guarantee valid statistical tests because natural language inherently favors certain n-grams over others. This bias can cause p-values to be artificially low or high even for unwatermarked text." "To address this, we ensure that under H0 , p-values are approximately uniform on U (0, 1). In practice, we test many candidate secret keys and select one for which the empirical p-value distribution on unwatermarked text is close to uniform." "We provide more details in subsection A.1." - Experimental setup, paragraph "Choice of private key" (p.6): "We address the private key issue described in section 3 by testing 50 candidate keys. For each combination of tokenizer, watermarking method (and depth for SynthID), we evaluate the keys on non-watermarked texts to estimate the p-value distribution under H0 ." "We then select the key that minimizes the deviation from U (0, 1), as measured by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic." - Window-size-zero excluded (p.17): "Note that the case where k = 0 corresponds to the work of Zhao et al. (2023), but we found that it empirically breaks theoretical assumptions and often leads to degenerate text; therefore, we use k ≥ 1 in our experiments."

Reader's inference.
1. This is explicit key screening: 50 candidate keys per (tokenizer, scheme, depth), a KS uniformity test of null p-values on 100 short texts, selection of the best key, then a large-scale FPR check (1.5M Wikipedia documents) that is asserted in the text but not tabulated or plotted anywhere in the PDF.
2. σkeys is the standard deviation across keys of the mean p-value over 100 texts. For 100 i.i.d. U(0,1) p-values the sampling standard deviation of the mean is sqrt(1/12)/10 ≈ 0.029, so the observed 0.029–0.044 sits at or modestly above pure sampling noise; Table 6 therefore does not isolate the between-key component of the null, and a KS test on 100 texts of about 200 tokens has little power against a 0.5 → 0.505 shift that, by the authors' own account, matters "on long texts".
3. Selecting the best of 50 KS p-values is a winner's-curse procedure: the reported KS p (0.93–1.00) is optimistic, and the selected key's mean p-value can be further from 0.5 than the all-key average (e.g. Gemma 3 Green-red: 0.488 selected versus 0.501 pooled), because KS distance and mean shift are different criteria.
4. Identical DipMark / Green-red / MorphMark rows are consistent with those schemes sharing one green/red PRF partition for a given key, so the null behaviour depends only on (tokenizer, hash, key), not on the sampling rule; the screening is a property of the hash and key.
5. The "private key issue" is framed as a nuisance to remove so that H1 results reflect the scheme; the paper does not quantify how often a random key fails, nor the FPR of an unscreened key.

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure

Observed.
- De-duplication (p.4): "An important practical consideration is deduplication: because the pseudorandom function depends on the preceding k tokens, repeated n-grams generate identical hashes. This violates the statistical independence of the scores used when assuming the distribution of the test statistic under H0 ." "Aggregating scores only over unique watermark windows within the text (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a; Fernandez et al., 2023) is a good way to mitigate this issue and to ensure valid p-values." Radioactivity test (p.20): "We filter the indices t to form a set U such that each watermark window yt−k:t−1 is only scored once, which fully removes this issue (Sander et al., 2024)."
- Null texts and keys: 100 non-watermarked texts of 800 characters per candidate key, 50 candidate keys per configuration (p.19); one selected key per configuration; verification "on 1.5M Wikipedia documents (they contain up to 100k tokens, with an average of 715 tokens)" (p.6). Window size for all schemes (p.5): "We evaluate multiple watermarking methods (detailed in subsection A.2), all with window size 2, varying their hyperparameters to explore their detection-quality trade-offs."
- Comparison with the theoretical level: asserted at p.6 ("we verify that the theoretical FPRs for these keys match the empirical FPRs"), but no table or figure of empirical versus theoretical FPR appears in the PDF; Table 6 reports only mean p-values and KS p-values on 100 texts.
- Detection metric (p.6): "Detection: We compute the p-value of the corresponding detection test, which can be read as the probability that a watermark score at least as high would happen if the text were not watermarked." Code experiments (p.10): "Second, we measure the detection power : the true positive rate (TPR) at a fixed false positive rate (FPR), and/or the distribution of log10 p-values." Artefact warning (p.10): "In practice, the latter is a better indicator of the watermark power, since codes that do not pass tests often exhibit degenerative patterns (repeated code or text at the end) which artificially increase the TPR."

Reader's inference. TPR at FPR = 10^-3 (Figures 6–7, Table 3) uses the nominal analytic level, trusted after key screening; the empirical validation on 1.5M documents is reported to exist but its numbers are not shown, so the reader cannot see the achieved FPR of the selected keys at any α.

## Q4 Remedies

Observed.
- De-duplication over unique windows (p.4, p.20; quoted in Q3).
- Key selection by KS uniformity on null texts (p.4, p.6, p.19; quoted in Q2).
- Exclusion of window size 0 (p.17; quoted in Q2).
- For the radioactivity test, the same binomial null with de-duplication (p.20): "Under the null hypothesis H0 (i.e., the suspect model is unaware of s), the count of green predictions should now follow a binomial distribution, as the suspect model should exhibit no preference toward green or red tokens:"
- A documented null failure from selection bias, WaterMax (p.20): "However, WaterMax selects the final sequence x∗ from a set of candidates C by maximizing the number of green tokens globally by chance." An innocent model "will find that its optimal next token" ... "is green significantly more often than γ, purely due to this selection bias." Evidence: "As expected, the radioactivity test yields a p-value of 0.93 for Green-list/Red-list random sampling and 0.76 for Green-list/Red-list beam search with biased scoring, but 1.0 × 10−6 for WaterMax." (on about 100k watermarked tokens, p.20)
- Entropy filtering at detection is not a calibration remedy and is judged marginal (p.13): "Third, entropy filtering at detection time provided only marginal gains while introducing additional complexity, making it a less practical option in typical deployment scenarios."

Reader's inference. The WaterMax radioactivity example is an independent demonstration that a nominal binomial null can be badly wrong for an innocent party (p = 10^-6 on ~100k tokens) through a mechanism (candidate selection) unrelated to the key; it shows the authors treat null validity empirically, case by case, rather than by a general per-key guarantee.

## Reader's inference

1. Guarantee type: the null is stated conditionally on the private key s, but the authors say the uniformity/FPR guarantee holds only in expectation over keys; for a fixed key the null green fraction can shift (their example 0.5 → 0.505) and the FPR inflates with text length.
2. Per-key dispersion: Table 6 is the only quantitative evidence in the four papers, but it measures the across-key spread of mean null p-values on 100 short texts (σkeys 0.029–0.044), which is indistinguishable from sampling noise; the between-key component of the FPR at small α and long texts is not isolated.
3. Remedy: key screening (50 keys, KS test, best-of-50 selection) followed by an untabulated 1.5M-document FPR verification; this is a pragmatic sound-in-expectation-to-sound-for-this-key repair with no stated guarantee, and its selection step is optimistic by construction.
4. The main-text cross-reference to "subsection A.1" points to the wrong appendix; the relevant text is A.3 on p.19.
5. The identical DipMark / Green-red / MorphMark rows indicate that key quality is a property of the (tokenizer, hash, key) triple rather than of the sampling scheme; screening results transfer across schemes that share a partition.
