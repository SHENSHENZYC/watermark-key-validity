## Q2 Models and ties

Observed:
- The object of criticism (p.1): "That is, it posits that each LLM has a single latent “quality” score, where the probability that one model beats another on a prompt depends only on the difference between their qualities, independently of the identity of the user." Section 2 (p.4) restates Chiang et al.'s reweighted maximum-likelihood BT estimator with active pair sampling.
- Current arena practice noted (p.4, footnote 3): "The current mechanism by LMArena extends this by calculating the confidence intervals for each model’s estimated BT score, and displaying ties in the ranking when intervals overlap [LMArena Team, 2025b]."
- The paper's own user model has no ties and no noise (p.3): "Each user i is associated with a strict preference order over the models in C"; (p.4): "We make the key simplifying assumption that users are consistent: for any user i and any pair of models x, y ∈ C, i prefers x over y in a battle if and only if x appears before y in the" associated ranking σ_i.
- BT baseline in the experiments (p.24): "Compute the Bradley-Terry ranking over a sample of 100, 000 users and all pairwise comparisons for each user." p.24: "This is equivalent to ranking by Borda count over all sampled users, and is also equivalent to ranking by Elo-score over all possible pairwise comparisons from all sampled users."

## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

Observed:
- The statistic is a social-choice stability ratio, not a fit statistic. For a committee W_k, γ̂ = k · max over a ∉ W_k of the sampled fraction of users preferring a to all of W_k (p.11); it "captures the proportion of users that are unsatisfied with the committee Wk" relative to the 1/k allowance. Figure 2 caption (p.11): "Values of γ̂ ≤ 1 indicate that the top-k prefix committee was stable for each k. The plotted points and error regions show the mean and standard error, respectively, after 5 repeats."
- Result on the LMArena-derived semi-synthetic population (p.12): "The Bradley-Terry ranking actually violates stability for top-k prefix committees with k = 4, 5 when ϕ = 0.1, and k = 5 when ϕ = 0.5. In contrast, both Algorithms 2 and 3 maintain stability for all k and all ϕ." p.12: "Intuitively, stability is “easiest” to satisfy when ϕ = 0.9, since this corresponds to high enough user diversity that there are no large clusters of users to activate the stability constraint." Summary (p.11): "We show that the currently adopted Bradley-Terry ranking indeed violates stability for some top-k prefix committees, whereas our ranking is able to preserve stability for all top-k prefix committees."
- Fully synthetic Mallows result (p.11): "In contrast, the top-k prefix committee of the Bradley-Terry ranking actually violates stability for many k when ϕ = 0.1 and ϕ = 0.5."
- Headline claims (p.1): "Using data from LMArena, we show that standard Bradley–Terry aggregation can violate local stability in practice, whereas our method provides substantially stronger stability guarantees." (p.3): "Our experiments demonstrate that the currently-used Bradley–Terry ranking can violate stability, whereas our methods achieve stability to a degree that is even stronger than our theoretical bounds in practice."
- The misspecification argument is conceptual, via non-identifiability (p.2): "Statistically speaking, a single BT model is a misspecified model of the exhibited comparisons, and as such, the maximum-likelihood BT scores need not represent what people want in any meaningful way". Worked example (p.2): "The two profiles induce the same pairwise comparisons and are therefore indistinguishable from the perspective of the BT model, which outputs the ranking a ≻ b ≻ c ≻ d ≻ e ≻ f in both cases." (p.2): "By contrast, the same outcome is far less satisfactory for Profile 1. Here, 40% of the population is only exposed to models that they dislike".
- Goodness-of-fit, likelihood-ratio, transitivity/cycle or homogeneity test on the raw 140k votes: not found. The dispersion ϕ of the Mallows mixture is a free parameter rather than estimated from the data (p.12): "In essence, we model a population of users whose preferences are generally clustered around prompt categories, and higher dispersions ϕ correspond with higher user diversity within these clusters."

## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation

Observed:
- Error bars are standard errors over repeats (5 for Figure 2, 10 for Figure 1). p.24: "With the sample size of 100, 000 users, there was no variation in the Bradley-Terry ranking between repeats." p.24: "Compute the stability approximation factors γ̂ for all top-k prefix committees of all rankings using a draw of n = 100, 000 users."
- Theoretical guarantee (p.6): "Algorithm 1 returns a committee Ŝ of size at most k, such that with probability at least 1 − δ, Ŝ is (16 + O(ε))-approximately stable for D." (p.8): "For example, setting ε = 0.01 and 0.05 gives us approximation factors of 18.97 and 39.2, respectively." (p.12): "While Algorithm 2 has stronger theoretical guarantees, we find that Algorithm 3 performs slightly better empirically."
- No rank intervals are computed for the BT baseline, and BT misspecification is not propagated into BT uncertainty; the paper's response is to replace the BT ranking by a committee-selection mechanism.

## Q5 Limitations and future work

Observed (Section 6, p.12):
- "First, our framework assumes that a user’s type deterministically yields their preference ranking over models. In practice, user preferences may depend on the specific prompt as well as stochasticity in model responses."

- "In many real-world settings, however, one must work with pre-collected preference datasets in which users already contribute varying numbers of comparisons." and "It would be interesting to study how to achieve fair representation in this offline setting."

## Reader's inference

- Reader's inference: the 'local stability violation shown on LMArena data' is a semi-synthetic result: real votes enter only through twenty per-category BT rankings and category vote shares; within-category dispersion ϕ is assumed, and the violation disappears at ϕ = 0.9. Local stability is a fairness criterion from committee elections, not a statistical lack-of-fit test, so the paper does not say whether BT fits the observed vote frequencies.
- Reader's inference: the claim that BT is "misspecified" rests on a non-identifiability example (two profiles with identical pairwise margins) — exactly the heterogeneity that a saturated-pairwise or voter-homogeneity test would probe — but no such test is run, and the paper discards tie votes, voter/session identifiers and language.
- Reader's inference: BT uncertainty is not treated beyond observing that 100,000 synthetic users yield a deterministic BT ranking; nothing is propagated into rank intervals.
- Reader's inference on relevance: as of June 2026 the social-choice critique of arena BT had not produced a formal goodness-of-fit test on public vote data, so the research question remains open; the paper is a useful 'misspecification-by-heterogeneity' motivation and a source for the 140k dataset pointer.
