## Q3 Adequacy tests and results
- Observed: non-transitivity is measured, not tested. Two descriptive metrics: PNT, the percentage of instructions whose triplet of judge verdicts violates transitivity (Eq. 2, p.3; the 14 violating patterns listed in Appendix B.1, pp.12–13), and SNTD, p.3: "where the Jensen–Shannon divergence (J SD) quantifies the discrepancy between observed win rates ϕ and estimated win rates ϕ̂ under transitivity assumptions, as defined below." The transitivity-implied win rate is the BT prediction from the other two pairs (Eq. 5, p.3; derivation Appendix B.4, p.16).
- Observed: motivation, p.3: "However, an implicit assumption in these frameworks is that transitivity holds in preference judgments, which has not been empirically verified."
- Observed results (Table 1, p.5; four scenarios LL/LM/ML/MM): GPT-4-Turbo PNT 3.98/5.96/3.98/8.45 and SNTD 0.1121/0.1336/0.1215/0.1431; GPT-3.5-Turbo PNT 21.37/22.48/22.86/20.87 and SNTD 0.2654/0.2586/0.2625/0.2629. p.5: "Both PNT and SNTD generally increase as the performance gap between model pairs (mA , mB ) or (mB , mC ) narrows." Figure 4 (p.6) heatmap over all 6,840 triplet permutations peaks near zero win-rate gap. GPT-4o-mini (Table 3, p.14) and Arena-Hard-Auto (Table 4, p.14) replicate the pattern.
- Observed, model level: p.5 "Surprisingly, we detect no instances of hard non-transitivity (e.g., ma ≻ mb , mb ≻ mc , and ma ≺ mc ) at the model level, which we partially attribute to the effectiveness of calibration and randomness mitigation techniques." Soft violation example, p.6: "transitivity would predict a win rate of 0.68 for GPT-4-Turbo against Claude-3-Opus. However, the observed rate of 0.72 reveals a subtle violation of transitivity at the model level." Under random position assignment with one API call, a hard cycle appears (Qwen1.5-72B-Chat over Yi-34B-Chat over claude-3-opus-20240229 over Qwen1.5-72B-Chat; Figure 11, pp.19–20) and model-level SNTD is 4.00 × 10−4 (position switching) versus (5.38 ± 0.04) × 10−4 over 50 random-assignment trials (Table 6, p.20): "As shown in Table 6, the degree of non-transitivity in the ablated experiment is significantly higher than in the original experiment."
- Observed, baseline sensitivity: p.6 "We find that only 20% of models maintain consistent rank positions across all rankings. Moreover, when comparing any pair of ranking lists, only 61% of models preserve their rank positions on average."
- Observed, causes: p.7 "Interestingly, we find non-transitivity still occurs within consistent instructions, with GPT-4-Turbo serving as the judge, indicating that position bias is not the sole cause of non-transitivity."
- Observed: no formal hypothesis test, p-value, likelihood-ratio test, saturated model, goodness-of-fit statistic or heterogeneity test (searched: p-value, "p <", hypothesis test, bootstrap, goodness, "lack of fit", "likelihood ratio", saturated, heterogene, homogene, misspecif — 0 hits; "significantly" occurs only descriptively). The word "triad" does not occur; the unit is a "triplet".
- Reader's inference: this paper establishes non-transitivity of LLM-judge preferences descriptively and shows that fixed-baseline rankings are fragile, but it offers no inferential test of BT adequacy and applies nothing to human votes.

## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation
- Observed: the only uncertainty statement is a Monte Carlo band on rank correlations over random model subsets, p.8: "For each model count, we randomly sample models and conduct 2000 trials, reporting the mean correlation with a 95% confidence interval." (Figure 6 right), plus the standard deviation over 50 trials in Table 6 (p.20).
- Observed: no confidence intervals on BT coefficients or Elo ratings (Table 9, p.23 reports point Elo values only), no rank intervals, and no propagation of non-transitivity into rating uncertainty.
- Observed: headline results, p.8: "As shown in Table 2, our method achieves higher correlations, with a 4% increase in Spearman correlation and a 5.2% increase in Kendall correlation." (Table 2, p.8: Spearman 81.4% → 85.4% without length control, 95.0% → 96.4% with; Kendall 63.2% → 68.4% and 82.1% → 86.3%.)
- Reader's inference: non-transitivity is treated as something to mitigate by design (round-robin, SWIM) rather than as misspecification to be quantified in the uncertainty of the resulting ranking.

## Q5 Limitations and future work
- p.8: "Furthermore, while our benchmark relies on human rankings from Chatbot Arena, inherent human biases (Chen et al., 2024) may introduce non-transitivity in human preferences, fundamentally limiting the achievable alignment between automated and human evaluations."
- p.8: "Finally, our analysis relies on the Bradley-Terry model, which assumes transitive model-level preferences by assigning each model a global scalar score." and "While we do observe instance-level non-transitivity in our pairwise comparisons, these cases are relatively rare, and hard non-transitivity in the aggregated model-level preferences is mild. Therefore, we find the Bradley-Terry model sufficient for our ranking purposes." Future work named: multi-dimensional capability models (Duan et al., 2017), judges beyond GPT-4-Turbo and GPT-3.5-Turbo, pointwise-to-pairwise conversions (p.8).
- Observed: the first quotation is the closest the paper comes to the research question: it conjectures non-transitivity in human arena preferences and does not test it.

## Reader's inference
- The paper is about LLM judges, not human voters: it documents instance-level non-transitivity in GPT-4-Turbo and GPT-3.5-Turbo preferences with two descriptive metrics (PNT, SNTD), finds hard model-level cycles only when position-debiasing is weakened, and still adopts BT as "sufficient" for ranking.
- For the research question it supplies (i) a reusable metric (SNTD: JSD between observed and BT-implied triplet win rates) that could be turned into a test on arena votes, and (ii) an explicit, untested conjecture that human arena preferences may themselves be non-transitive. It performs no specification test, reports no uncertainty on ratings, and does not touch public vote data, so it leaves the question open.
- The reading brief attributed a "noise-augmented BT model" to this paper; that model is in 2609.22170. This paper's modelling variation is soft- versus hard- versus rounded-BT labels (Appendix D).
