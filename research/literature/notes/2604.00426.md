## Q2 Models and ties

Observed (model and null): cardinal paired-comparison model Y_ijk = ν_ij + ε_ijk with iid zero-mean errors (eq. (1), p.2), Saari's orthogonal decomposition ν = ν_linear + ν_cyclic (eq. (2), p.2), and the null of linear stochastic transitivity ν_ij = μ_i − μ_j (eq. (3), pp.2–3). "In this paper, we focus on cardinal PCD; the applicability of the proposed methodology to binary PCD, and in particular to Bradley–Terry models (Bradley and Terry, 1952; Hunter, 2004; Cattelan, 2012), is discussed in Section 6." (p.2) Scores are estimated by least squares with the Laplacian pseudo-inverse (p.4). Scope statement: "where lack of fit refers to violations of this constraint rather than to general model misspecification." (p.3)

Observed (BT analogue, Section 6, p.21): "In fact, the framework proposed here can be directly applied to the BT model." (p.21) The BT version of statistic (4) is a weighted sum over edges of squared differences between the empirical win fraction S_ij/n_ij and the fitted logistic probability, motivated by Grizzle et al. (1969) and Beaver (1977) (p.21).

Observed (ties): not discussed anywhere (0 hits for tie, draw, Rao, Kupper, Davidson). The Kendall–Smith benchmark dichotomises at the win fraction, Z_ij = I(W_ij ≥ n_ij/2) (p.13). Reader's inference: a 50% win fraction is counted as a "win" in that benchmark, but ties as outcomes are simply outside the paper's cardinal model.

## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

Observed (tests proposed, Sections 2–3): three weighted-residual statistics R_n^(1), R_n^(2), R_n^(3) (eq. (4) and modifications summing only over edges with n_ij → ∞ or n_ij/n → c > 0), with limiting weighted chi-square distributions under the null and local alternatives (Theorems 2.1–2.2, pp.5–7); detectability: "Theorem 2.3 shows that the tests Rn , s ∈ {1, 2, 3}, can detect a departure from the null if and only if Es contains a triad (i, j, k) satisfying (5)." (p.7), i.e., a cyclical triad inside the tested edge set. For K → ∞: the statistic R_{m,K} (eq. (8)) with asymptotic normality (Theorem 3.1, pp.8–9), detectability only if K·ψ²_K does not vanish (Proposition 3.1, p.10), and localized tests (12) and (15) that compare residual variance on a suspected cyclic subset U against the rest, including sparse Erdős–Rényi graphs (Theorems 3.2–3.3, pp.10–12). "Theorem 3.3 shows that LOF can be detected even on very sparse graphs, a somewhat unexpected phenomenon, provided the subset U on which departures from (3) may occur is known." (p.12)

Observed (positioning): "To the best of our knowledge, LOF testing for PCD has not been studied at this level of generality." (p.3); "We note that Brunelli (2018) surveyed many other measures of fit for PCD; none, to the best of our knowledge, has been analyzed from a statistical perspective." (p.4); "Despite these well–documented violations, the methodological framework for formally detecting LOF has remained underdeveloped, and the test introduced by Kendall and Smith (1940) has been the predominant approach." (p.20)

Observed (simulation results): Table 1 (p.13), K = 30, m = 10, two cyclic triads, normal errors — empirical power T_KS 0.078, cardinal Kendall–Smith 0.496, R_n^(1) 0.818; "Table 1 shows that the Kendall–Smith test has limited power for detecting LOF." (p.13) Table 2 (p.14): F-test vs R_n^(1) power 0.709 vs 0.366, 0.972 vs 0.456, 0.998 vs 0.996 for γ = 1/2, 3/4, 1; a misspecified F-test has power 0.348 at γ = 1. Table 4 (p.15): on highly unbalanced graphs R_n^(1) power 0.107/0.278 versus R_n^(2) 0.678/0.972 and R_n^(3) 0.689/0.957. Table 5 (p.17): empirical levels 0.041–0.076 and powers 0.306–0.962 on ER graphs; "The proposed tests are somewhat liberal when the sample size is small, but attain the nominal level of 0.05 once K exceeds approximately 70." (p.17)

Observed (NBA results): Table 6 (p.18), caption "Testing LOF: p-values for the seasons for teams violating LOF. Critical values are obtained using 105 Monte Carlo smaples." (p.18) [10^5; typo in source]; p-values by season 2015-16: 0; 2016-17: 0.00017; 2017-18: 0; 2018-19: 0.00008; 2020-21: 0; 2021-22: 0; 2022-23: 0.00001; 2023-24: 0.04466; 2024-25: 0. "Table 6 shows that the null hypothesis of LST is rejected, with highly significant pvalues." (p.17) A Kendall–Smith-style triad-removal search identifies 12 cyclical teams in 2023–24: "This represents a substantial fraction of the league, indicating that intransitivity is not a marginal phenomenon but a pervasive feature of the data." (p.18) Season-to-season Jaccard overlap of cyclic-team sets 0.13–0.62 (Table 8, p.19); a two-state Markov chain gives transition matrix rows (0.61, 0.39) and (0.49, 0.51): "These estimates indicate that a cyclic team has approximately a 50% probability of remaining cyclic in the subsequent season, suggesting moderate persistence of LOF violations over time." Not found: likelihood-ratio tests against a saturated model (the LR test appears only as the benchmark F-test, p.14), tie models, heterogeneity/homogeneity tests across sub-populations (the only "homogeneity" is the Markov-chain time-homogeneity assumption, p.19), or any test on binary/BT data beyond the "Preliminary numerical experiments" remark (p.21).

Reader's inference: the paper is a lack-of-fit theory for cardinal LST models whose detectable alternatives are exactly cyclic triads; its binary/BT extension is sketched, not demonstrated, and no LLM or arena data are touched.

## Q4 Uncertainty and propagation

Observed: the abstract motivates the work by "Empirical violations, however, are common and can substantially affect inference and ranking." (p.1), but no rank or score confidence intervals are computed for the NBA data; inference is confined to test p-values (critical values by Monte Carlo simulation of the null, p.8 and Table 6) and bootstrap/resampling estimates of the test-statistic variance (pp.9, 16). Not found: confidence intervals or standard errors for team scores or ranks, or any propagation of detected cyclicity into ranking uncertainty (searched: confidence, uncertaint, bootstrap, interval). The cited Gao, Shen and Zhang (2023) paper on uncertainty quantification in the Bradley–Terry–Luce model appears only in the reference list (p.23).

Reader's inference: the paper detects and localises lack of fit but stops short of saying what it does to the ranking's uncertainty; the follow-up modelling of ν_cyclic is delegated to Singh and Davidov (2026, arXiv:2406.11584) (p.22).

## Q5 Limitations and future work

- Binary/BT data: "Nevertheless, the resulting procedures do not account for the binary nature of the observations and are therefore not fully satisfactory." (p.21) and "Thus, the development of more powerful, principled tests for the BT and other binary PCD models that leverage the ideas introduced in this paper remains an open and important problem for future work." (p.21) Preceded by: "Preliminary numerical experiments indicate that these tests are effective in practice." (p.21)
- Sparse graphs: "A limitation of the current approach to large sparse PCGs is the assumption that the set of potentially cyclic items, U, is known a priori, whereas in practice it is typically unknown." (p.21) Also: when all n_ij ≤ 1 "additional information is required, either in the form of the localization of the set of cyclic items or knowledge of σ 2" (p.21).
- Non-localized cyclicity: Condition 3.2 requires J = o(K); when J = O(K) "the family of statistics proposed here does not appear to be well suited for this setting." (p.21)
- Not found: any explicit mention of LLM arenas or crowd-vote leaderboards as a target (searched: arena, LLM, leaderboard, crowd — only "crowdsourced environments", p.11).

## Reader's inference

Reader's inference: Singh & Davidov supply the most directly relevant statistical machinery of the four papers — formal lack-of-fit tests whose alternatives are cyclic triads, with asymptotics for fixed-K unbalanced graphs and for large sparse random graphs — but they analyse cardinal NBA data only, state in their own words that the BT/binary version remains "an open and important problem for future work" (p.21), handle no ties, and compute no rank uncertainty. They therefore leave the arena question (BT fit of binary crowd votes with ties, and its effect on rank CIs) open rather than closing it, while showing that a mature cardinal-data toolkit exists to adapt.
