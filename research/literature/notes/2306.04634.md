## Q1 Null and what is random

Observed.
- Scheme (p.3): "A random number generator is seeded with a context window of h preceding tokens, based on a pseudo-random function (PRF) f : Nh → N. With this random seed, a subset of tokens of size γ|V | are “colored” green and denoted Gt ." "The watermark can be described by four parameters. The “hash” used to generate the greenlists f with context width h, greenlist fraction γ, and the logit bias δ."
- Test (p.3): "The statistical significance of a sequence of tokens of length T can be established by deriving the z-score" z = (|s| − γT)/sqrt(γ(1−γ)T) (eq. 2). "When this z-score is large, one can be confident that the text is watermarked."
- The null is written out explicitly only for the run-length detector (p.32): "H0 : The text sequence is generated with no knowledge of the watermarking rule and therefore the “green” run lengths are geometrically distributed."
- The secret is a salt (p.21): "Each depends on a secret salt value s ∈ N and a standard integer PRF P : N → N."
- Independence assumption acknowledged (p.19): "In their original work, Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) discussed the impact that repeated n-grams in a given piece of text would have on the validity of the independence assumption of the z-test."
- Nominal level used (p.40): "(a z-score of 4 implies a FPR of 3.2e−5)".
- Sample-complexity heuristic under H1 (p.4): "Suppose each token is only ε more likely to be green than a random baseline, i.e. |s| = γT (1 + ε). Then for any z-score threshold, we expect to detect the watermark after seeing T = (z 2 − γz 2 )/(ε2 γ) tokens."

Reader's inference. The paper inherits the z-test of 2301.10226 without re-deriving it; green membership is treated as Bernoulli(γ) i.i.d. with the randomness supplied by the PRF/salt, which makes the stated level a marginal (hash-averaged) guarantee. Nothing is said about the FPR for a fixed salt. The paper also says it does not use p-values (p.19, quoted in Q2); its headline error rates are empirical quantiles on human text, so for a given run they are measurements under whatever single salt was used.

## Q2 Fixed key / per-key / key screening

Observed.
- Black-box setting and key secrecy (p.17): "In this work we have focused on the black-box setting, where text is modified in plausible use cases for machine-generated text and the secret key of the watermarking scheme is unknown to the party modifying the text through paraphrasing or other editing." "the watermark context width h has to be chosen sufficiently large so that in an adversarial scenario, the watermark key could not be easily discovered (or alternatively, sufficiently many keys must be employed simultaneously, as discussed in Kirchenbauer et al. (2023))."
- Discoverability of a one-token hash (p.4): "Because the greenlist depends only on one single token, a third-party observer could learn the greenlist associated with the token at position t − 1 by searching subsequent words at position t that are less likely to appear than expected under a non-watermarked distribution."
- One fixed configuration throughout (p.5): "We use a single set of language model sampling parameters across all experiments, multinomial sampling at temperature 0.7," "for all experiments, unless explicitly stated, we use the LeftHash watermark scheme based on an additive PRF with context window h = 1 and (γ, δ) = (0.25, 2.0)."
- Empirical calibration of the WinMax detector (p.31): "As this test involves multiple hypothesis testing, we calibrate to a fixed false-positive rate based on comparisons with non-watermarked text."
- Stance on p-values (p.19): "While we do not utilize p-values in our analyses, and instead consider empirical FPR and TPR rates, we run some experiments to evaluate the significance of the impact that counting or not counting repeated n-grams at detection time can have ."
- Variation of the FPR across keys, a deployed fixed key, per-key calibration, key screening or key choice: not found (searched: key, seed, fixed, screening, Kolmogorov, uniform, "false positive", FPR, repeat, n-gram, tuple, calibrat, deployed, salt, "master key"; see "Not found").

Reader's inference. The number of salts/keys used is never reported. Read together, "a single set ... across all experiments" and the fixed LeftHash scheme imply one PRF/salt throughout, so every empirical FPR in the paper is a key-conditional measurement for one unspecified key, with no between-key dispersion available. The WinMax calibration (p.31) is an empirical quantile on non-watermarked text, which in practice is calibration under the deployed key, although the paper does not frame it that way.

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure

Observed.
- Sample sizes (p.5): "Unless otherwise stated, in all figures, ROC space plots and measurements are backed by > 500 positive and > 500 negative samples, and other types of point estimates are also based on > 500 samples."
- Negatives (p.36): "the “negative” samples at test time that should be classified as “not watermarked” or “not machine-generated” respectively, are human-written text i.e. the gold completions/suffixes extracted from the input data as prompts are created."
- Corpora (p.4): "Following Kirchenbauer et al. (2023), we use the Colossal Common Crawl Cleaned corpus (C4) dataset as a source of prompts for open-ended generation." Appendix A.7 (p.26) adds Pile subsets (github, free law, pubmed, uspto) and Wikitext103.
- Empirical FPR versus nominal p-value, Appendix A.3 (p.19): "We find that while the p-values produced by the uncorrected version of the detection test suggest a lower FPR than we actually measure at a given z-score threshold, the correction for n-gram repeats improves the correspondence between the empirical FPR and the p-value significantly."
- Fig. 7 caption (p.19): "The other curves show that the basic LeftHash scheme (blue) produces a slightly higher FPR than the corresponding p-value, but that the correction based on not counting repeated n-grams during detection (green) fixes the error underestimation." "For small values of T, the normal assumption is slightly violated, as the z-score required is closer to 4 whilst the z-score that should produce a p-value of 0.1% = 0.001 is actually 3.09 (black)." "the threshold required by the uncorrected version of the LeftHash (blue) scheme remains above the analytical value, but that correcting by not counting duplicate n-grams at detection time (green) causes the z-score threshold to correctly settle around the expected value of 3.09"
- Fig. 8 (p.19): "versus N ∗ P for N = 1000 samples from Wikitext103 (T=200)"; caption: "While the uncorrected version of LeftHash incurs a false positive rate (y axis) slightly larger than N ∗ P given a threshold P , the correction of not counting repeats fixes this issue."
- Credit to concurrent work (p.19): "Concurrently, the work of Fernandez et al. (2023) presents a more in depth analysis of this effect on the watermark of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) as well as other watermarking techniques with detection algorithms based on hypothesis tests."
- Mechanistic view of null scores (p.38): "we see that for all T , the z-score of human text remains very low (Left) whilst the z-scores for the attacked watermarked texts continue to grow steadily (Right)"
- Correlated negatives caveat (p.36): "If a specific sequence of human-written text happens to produce an abnormally high z-score, which could artificially inflate the z-score of the copy-paste attacked example making it an easier detection, then it will simultaneously increase the chance of a false positive on that sequence"

Reader's inference. The only empirical-versus-nominal FPR check is N = 1000 Wikitext103 texts at T = 200 with one scheme (one key), which resolves the FPR only down to about 10^-3; the 0.1% threshold curve is an empirical quantile of those 1000 texts. The main-text results (>500 negatives) cannot resolve the 1e-5 level quoted in the abstract; that level comes from the Gaussian p-value at z = 4, not from empirical nulls. The direction of the uncorrected error (empirical FPR above nominal) and its repair by de-duplication agree with 2308.00113.

## Q4 Remedies

Observed.
- Not counting repeated n-grams at detection (Appendix A.3, quoted in Q3).
- WinMax, calibrated empirically (p.4): "To be able to accurately detect watermarked sub-regions even in long documents, we design a windowed test, called WinMax that searches for the continuous span of tokens that generates that highest z-score (see Appendix A.9 for a formal definition)." Cost (p.38): "we see that the likelihood of a False Positive at smaller values of T is higher under WinMax than the basic z-score detection test as the separation between the scores for human text (Left) and attacked watermarked text (Right) is not as large."
- Wider context with Min/SelfHash schemes for secrecy (p.21): "This shows that we can use the additional strength provided by Min and SelfHash to run longer context widths, which in turn secure the watermark."
- Run-length detector found no gain (p.31): "Yet, we found no gains from such a detector over z-test and WinMax within the range of settings we consider in this work."
- Multiple keys as a secrecy measure (p.17, citing the earlier paper; quoted in Q2).

Reader's inference. Empirical calibration on non-watermarked text (p.31) is the paper's only calibration procedure and is a template for per-key empirical calibration; it carries no analytic guarantee, only an empirical quantile from at most 1000 human texts.

## Reader's inference

1. Guarantee type: marginal by inheritance from 2301.10226; the paper itself relies on empirical FPR/TPR rather than p-values.
2. Per-key dispersion: not studied; the number of keys is not stated, and the single fixed LeftHash configuration implies one key, so the reported FPRs are single-key measurements of unknown representativeness.
3. The de-duplication result (Appendix A.3) confirms, on N = 1000 texts at the 10^-3 level, that uncorrected empirical FPR exceeds the nominal p-value and that skipping repeated n-grams repairs it for that key.
4. The WinMax calibration "based on comparisons with non-watermarked text" is the paper's implicit answer to miscalibration: empirical quantiles under the deployed detector, which in practice means under the deployed key.
