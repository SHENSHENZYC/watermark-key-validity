## Q1 Null and what is random
Observed. PRF (p.3): "The PRF is deterministic: for a given context window, secret key, and candidate token, it always" / "returns the same value. However, its output is indistinguishable from uniform randomness to anyone" / "who does not know K (see subsection A.1 for implementation details of the PRF)." Detection (p.4): "For unwatermarked text, Rt values are essentially random, yielding a" lower score; the score is compared with what is "expected under the null hypothesis H0 (no watermark). In practice, we choose a threshold τ (depending" / "on the desired false positive rate) and flag a text as watermarked if ST > τ ." Non-distortion (p.4): "Proposition 1 establishes that Gumbel-max is single-token non-distortionary (Definition 1): in expectation over the random key, the selected token follows exactly the LLM’s original distribution. The" corollary gives R | V* ∼ Beta(1/p, 1). The validity statement (p.5): "Proposition 2 (p-value under H0 ). Under H0 (text not watermarked), the score ST follows a Γ(T, 1)" / "distribution. The p-value associated to a score s is:" the upper incomplete gamma ratio; "This provides an exact false positive rate: given any desired significance level α, we can compute a" / "detection threshold τ such that the probability of wrongly flagging unwatermarked text as watermarked" / "is exactly α." The appendix makes the averaging explicit (p.24): "Yet, on expectation over the secret keys, i.e., over r.v. R = (R1 , . . . , RV ), the" / "distribution of the chosen token follows the distribution given by the LLM." and (p.26) "For unwatermarked tokens, Rt ∼ U[0, 1] and" E[s_t] = 1. Dual-key fusion (p.6): "aggregate them later. Under H0 , si is a weighted combination of independent exponentials with mean" 1. Independence is enforced by deduplication (p.8): the detector deduplicates "(context window, token) tuples, because the PRF is deterministic and repeated tuples would yield" / "identical scores, violating the independence assumption of the statistical test (Fernandez et al., 2023)." In the radioactivity test (p.23): "unaware of K), the scores are independent and follow their null distribution, enabling exact p-value" computation.

Reader's inference. The null treats the PRF values R_t of an unwatermarked text as iid U[0,1], i.e., it is a statement in which the randomness is the key-derived pseudo-random values (equivalently the key), with the text fixed; the authors' own phrase for the non-distortion side is "in expectation over the random key". The "exact false positive rate" is therefore exact on average over the key, not conditional on one deployed key. The paper is aware that a fixed key makes repeated (context, token) tuples deterministic and patches this by deduplication, but does not otherwise analyse the fixed-key null.

Reader's inference. The empirical FPR check is large on the text side (10^6 null passages, nominal down to 1e-5) but silent on the key side; reading 1e-5 on 10^6 texts means about ten expected false positives per curve, so the lowest decades are noisy. Conservativeness of the curves (above the diagonal) is consistent with deduplication and the Gamma moment matching; nothing is said about whether another key would give the same curve. The SynthID baseline's p-values come from a central-limit (Z-score) approximation, whereas TextSeal's come from an exact Gamma (single key) or moment-matched Gamma (entropy-weighted).

## Reader's inference
TextSeal is the one document that behaves as if the false-positive rate were key-dependent: it screens secret keys with a KS test for uniform PRF hashes on unwatermarked text, fixes a key in deployment-style experiments, and deduplicates repeated (context, token) tuples to restore independence. Yet its stated guarantee ("exact false positive rate") is derived under iid U[0,1] values, i.e., averaged over the key, and its 10^6-passage calibration check does not report how many keys were used or how the curve varies across keys. The gap between the key-averaged guarantee and the key-screening practice is unexplained in the paper.
