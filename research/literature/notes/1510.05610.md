## Q1 Datasets

Observed: no real dataset is analysed. The empirical motivation is borrowed from the psychology/economics literature: "Various papers [DM59, ML65, Tve72, BW97] have provided experimental results in which these parametric modeling assumptions fail to hold." (p.2), and the authors quote Ballinger and Wilcox (1997) verbatim: "All of these parametric c.d.f.s are soundly rejected by our data. However, SST usually survives scrutiny." (p.2)

Observed (synthetic settings): a “bad” block matrix M* (Figure 1(a), p.7) used to show a constant approximation gap for parametric fits over n from 2^6 to 2^10 (Figure 1(b), pp.6–7); and "In our simulations, we generate the ground truth M ∗ in the following five ways:" (p.13) — Uniform (sorted uniform entries), Thurstone, Bradley-Terry-Luce, High SNR (all off-diagonal entries 0.9/0.1), Independent bands (pp.13–14); "Figure 2 depicts the results of the simulations based on observations of the entire matrix Y . Each point is an average across 20 trials." (p.14) Observation model: one Bernoulli comparison per pair, Y ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} (p.4); partial observation via Erdős–Rényi sampling with probability p_obs (Section 3.5, p.12).

## Q2 Models and ties

Observed: the SST class C_SST (a permutation under which rows increase and columns decrease, p.5, eq. (3)); the parametric class C_PAR(F) with M*_ij = F(w*_i − w*_j) for a valid F, recovering Thurstone (F = Φ) and Bradley-Terry-Luce (logistic F) (p.5, eqs. "This class includes parametric models including the BTL and Thurstone models as special cases, but is considerably more general." (p.1)

Observed (ties): binary outcomes only; the diagonal is fixed at 1/2 and M*_ji = 1 − M*_ij (pp.3–4). Not found: any tie model or treatment of draws (0 hits for tie, draw, Rao, Kupper; "Davidson" is only the 1959 Davidson–Marschak reference, p.37).

## Q3 Adequacy tests and results

Observed: no hypothesis test of any kind is proposed or run (0 hits for test, testing, goodness, lack of fit, likelihood ratio, saturated, triad, cycl). Model inadequacy is treated as an approximation-theory and estimation question:

- Proposition 1 (p.6): there exist SST matrices whose normalised squared Frobenius distance to every parametric model (over all valid F) is bounded below by a universal constant c > 0; the proof (Appendix B, pp.42–43) uses the ordering condition that any parametric M with M_{i1 i2} > M_{i3 i4} must satisfy M_{i1 i3} ≥ M_{i2 i4} (Lemma 12), violated by the two-factor car example (p.6). "In any situation where a single (latent) parameter per item does not adequately explain our preferences, one can expect that the class of parametric models to provide a poor fit to the pairwise preference probabilities." (p.6)
- Simulation evidence (pp.6–7, 14–15): on the bad matrix, "Consistent with the lower bound (6), the error in the Thurstone-based estimator stays bounded above a universal constant." (p.6); on High SNR and Independent-bands data the Thurstone MLE incurs constant error while SVT decays as O(1/√n) (p.15). "In summary, we see that while the Thurstone MLE estimator gives minimax optimal rates of estimation when the underlying model is parametric, it can be inconsistent when the parametric assumptions are violated." (p.15)
- Class relations (Proposition 5, p.45; Figure 3): C_PAR is a strict subset of C_SST; C_FULL is neither a subset nor superset of C_SST or C_PAR.
- Weaker transitivity is unestimable: "We show that under these moderate stochastic transitivity (MST) and weak stochastic transitivity (WST) models, the Frobenius norm error of any estimator, measured in a uniform sense over the class, must be almost as bad as that incurred by making no assumptions whatsoever." (p.3)

Observed (positive results on estimation): minimax rate over C_SST of order log²(n)/n via constrained least squares (Theorem 1, p.7); soft/hard SVT achieves exactly order n^{-1/2} (Theorem 2, p.8); a two-stage FAS-plus-isotonic estimator achieves Õ(1/n) on C_HIGH(γ) (Theorem 3, p.10); parametric MLE achieves order 1/n on C_PAR(F) (Theorem 4, p.11); partial-observation analogues (Theorem 5, p.12).

Reader's inference: the paper argues BT/Thurstone inadequacy by construction and simulation, not by testing data; it supplies the model class (SST) against which a nonparametric or Bayes-factor-type adequacy check could be framed, but no test statistic.

## Q5 Limitations and future work

- "Establishing the best possible rates for polynomial-time algorithms over the full class CSST is a challenging open problem." (p.36)
- "The effect of allowing a data-dependent choice of the regularization parameter remains to be studied, although we suspect it may improve the minimax risk by a constant factor at best." (p.36)
- "Finally, in some applications, choices can be systematically intransitive, for instance when objects have multiple features and different features dominate different pairwise comparisons." (p.36) and "In these situations, the SST assumption may be weakened to one where the underlying pairwise comparison matrix is a mixture of a small number of SST matrices." (p.36)
- SVT may return matrices outside the SST class; "In a companion paper [SBW16], we provide an alternative computationally-efficient estimator with similar statistical guarantees that is guaranteed to return a matrix in the SST class." (p.9)

## Reader's inference

Reader's inference: Shah et al. (2016) is a theory paper on estimating the full pairwise-probability matrix under strong stochastic transitivity; its relevance to the gate is that it (i) formalises SST ⊋ BT and shows BT/Thurstone fits can be inconsistent off-model, (ii) cites the psychology literature's rejections of parametric models while SST "usually survives scrutiny" (p.2), and (iii) gives rate theory that could underpin an SST-vs-BT adequacy comparison. It runs no test, uses no real data, treats no ties, and says nothing about LLM arenas or rank uncertainty; it does not pre-empt an arena-vote lack-of-fit study.
