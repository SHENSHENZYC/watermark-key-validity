## Q1 What is random; conditional or marginal

Observed.

- What is derandomised, the knockoff draw (p.1): "Due to the randomness inherent to the method, different runs of model-X knockoffs on the same dataset often result in different sets of selected variables, which is undesirable in practice." and "multiple runs of knockoffs on the same observed dataset can in general lead to different selected sets Skn ."
- Claimed guarantee (p.1): "We prove that the derandomized procedure controls the FDR at the desired level, without any additional conditions (in contrast, previously proposed methods for derandomization are not able to guarantee FDR control)."
- Limit behaviour (p.2): "while for large M , the output is derandomized (i.e., as M → ∞, the set Skn–derand becomes a deterministic function of the observed data)."
- Target (p.2): "The goal here is to include in this set as many non-nulls as possible while controlling the false discovery rate" [FDR := E[|S ∩ H0| / (|S| ∨ 1)]].
- E-values and e-BH (p.3): "we call a non-negative random variable E an “e-value” if E[E] ≤ 1 under the null"; (p.4): "Wang and Ramdas (2022) prove that the e-BH procedure satisfies FDR ≤ α · |H0 |/p ≤ α. Importantly, this result allows for arbitrary dependence among the ej ’s."
- Relaxed e-values (p.5): "Clearly, this condition is strictly weaker than requiring E[ej ] ≤ 1 for all j ∈ H0 ." [condition (9): Σ_{j∈H0} E[e_j] ≤ p] and "Theorem 2 (Wang and Ramdas 2022). Suppose the values e1 , e2 , . . . , ep satisfy condition (9). Then the selected set Sebh of the e-BH procedure (6) satisfies FDR ≤ α."
- Why averaging is allowed (p.6): "One major advantage of using e-values for multiple testing is that validity depends only on the expected values E[ej ]—in particular, no assumptions are needed on the dependence structure among the ej ’s (Wang and Ramdas, 2022)."
- Procedure (p.6): M knockoff copies drawn "from this distribution (drawn i.i.d. conditional on the observed data" (X, Y)); "we aggregate the e-values obtained from these M knockoff copies by taking the average"; then e-BH at level α_ebh.
- Main theorem (p.6): "Theorem 3. For any αkn , αebh ∈ (0, 1), and any number of knockoff copies M ≥ 1, the selected set Skn–derand computed in Algorithm 1 satisfies FDR ≤ αebh ." Proof idea (p.6): per-run bound (8) gives Σ_{H0} E[e_j^(m)] ≤ p for each m; "Taking the average over" m preserves it; apply Theorem 2.
- Role of the per-run level (p.9): "the parameter αkn does not affect this FDR guarantee, but may instead affect the power of the method."
- Convergence (p.10): "In Algorithm 1, conditional on the data (X, Y), for each feature j the ej ’s are i.i.d. for m = 1, . . . , M . By the strong law of large numbers, we then have" [e_j^avg → e_j^∞ := E[e_j^(1) | X, Y]]; "Note that Skn–∞ is now a deterministic function of the data (X, Y)." and, via Hoeffding, "On this event, then, the definition of the e-BH procedure implies that Skn–derand = Skn–∞ ."

Reader's inference. The auxiliary randomness is the knockoff sampling; the FDR in Theorem 3 is an expectation over the data and over the M knockoff draws jointly, i.e. marginal. The paper does not claim FDR control conditional on the realised knockoffs; instead it shows that averaging makes the procedure converge to a deterministic function of the data, so that in the limit M → ∞ the conditional and marginal error rates coincide. Validity rests on the relaxed e-value condition being preserved under averaging, which holds because expectations are linear and no dependence assumption is needed.

## Q2 Fixed key / per-key / conditional vs marginal

Observed.

- The paper names the two levels of randomness and defines separate variability measures (p.11): "Note that there are two sources of variability: the randomness conditional on the dataset (i.e., arising from the knockoffs construction), and that from the dataset itself." "We define two different measures, to capture the overall (“marginal”) variability, and to capture the “conditional” variability that arises from the knockoffs construction (note that it is this latter measure that derandomized knockoffs aims to reduce)."
- Conditional selection probabilities (p.11): "where pbj is the empirical probability of selection on average over a random draw of the datasets and the knockoffs, while pbj,d is the empirical probability of selection conditional on the d-th draw of the dataset."
- Results (p.12): "In particular, the conditional variability (that is, the variability arising from the randomness in the knockoffs method, while conditioning on a fixed dataset) is substantially decreased for the derandomized method, as expected." and "the original knockoffs often has a high selection probability for the nulls that are rarely selected by derandomized knockoffs (i.e., many red points are above the 45-degree line); this phenomenon is more pronounced when we focus on the conditional selection probability."
- Why earlier derandomisation lacked a guarantee (p.2): "For this method, Ren et al. (2021) establish a guaranteed bound on the expected number of false discoveries, but it appears impossible to prove a bound directly on the FDR." and (p.4): "Nguyen et al. (2020) introduce a aggregation method aiming at FDR control, but under strong assumptions such as that the null feature importance statistics are i.i.d." Comparison (p.27): "For each v, the aggregated selected set Sb(v) has PFER controlled at level 2v, but is not guaranteed to control the FDR;"
- Finite-sample versus asymptotic (p.8): "In contrast, our result in Theorem 3 offers finite-sample FDR control without any additional assumptions."
- Heterogeneous runs may be pooled (p.7, Remark 3): "More generally, combining different knockoff configurations across multiple runs can potentially improve the robustness of our procedure, and this is an interesting direction for future research."
- A statement of validity conditional on the knockoff draw, per-draw calibration, screening of draws, key, Type I, false positive: not found (searched: key ("key insight" p.1, "key step" p.5, "key to establish" p.8 only), Type I (0; the case-insensitive hit is "genotype information" p.12), false positive (0), screening (p.20 "sequentially screens the hypotheses", a different sense), deployed (0), per-key (0), calibrat (p.33 reference title only), seed (0)).

Reader's inference. This is the most explicit treatment among the five of the conditional-versus-marginal distinction, but it is applied to variability of the selected set rather than to validity: the authors measure selection probabilities conditional on a fixed data set across knockoff draws and show derandomisation shrinks them, while the only guarantee stated remains the marginal FDR. The gap between conditional and marginal validity of a single randomised run is acknowledged indirectly (Figures 3–4: null features with high selection probability under one draw) but is not formalised.

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure

Observed.

- Simulation settings (p.10): "In both settings, we choose sample size n = 1000. In the linear case, the feature dimension p = 800 with |H1 | = 80 non-nulls, and in the logistic case, p = 600 with |H1 | = 50 non-nulls." Target level (p.10): "the selection set Skn is computed via the knockoff filter run with target FDR level α = 0.1." Derandomised version (p.11): "we draw M = 50 copies of the" knockoffs, α_kn = 0.05, α_ebh = 0.1.
- Replication design (p.11): "the data set (X, Y) is generated independently 100 times. For each draw of the dataset (X, Y), we also replicate the knockoffs procedure (i.e., original or derandomized) 20 times." Power and FDR are averaged over 100 × 20 runs; marginal and conditional selection variability as defined on p.11.
- Results (p.12): "In all cases, both methods have FDR bounded by the target level α = 0.1, whereas derandomized knockoffs consistently achieves an FDR substantially lower than the original knockoffs." and "while there is some power loss when the signal strength is weak".
- HIV data (p.13): "we have a dataset of n = 1840 samples and p = 219 mutations." "The derandomized method is run with M = 100 copies of the knockoff matrix." "we repeat the experiment 50 times in order to examine the variability of the selected set across different generations of the knockoffs." Outcome (p.30): "the average number of discoveries made by the derandomized knockoffs procedure is 63.5, and the average number of discoveries made by the original knockoffs is 77.0." and "For derandomized knockoffs, Out of the 63 discoveries, 38 mutations are verified. For original knockoffs, out of the 74 discoveries, 39 are verified."

Reader's inference. The nested design (100 data sets × 20 knockoff draws) is precisely what is needed to separate draw-conditional from marginal error rates, and the paper uses it to report both marginal and conditional selection probabilities; the realised FDR is reported only marginally (averaged over both levels) and compared with the nominal 0.1.

## Q4 Remedies and guarantees

Observed.

- Remedy: rewrite knockoffs as e-BH with e-values e_j = p·1{W_j ≥ T}/(1 + #{W_k ≤ −T}) (eq. (7), p.4; Theorem 1 equivalence p.4), run M independent knockoff draws, average the e-values, apply e-BH (Algorithm 1, p.9). Guarantee: Theorem 3, FDR ≤ α_ebh for any M and any α_kn (p.6).
- Tuning (p.9): "Empirically, we find that the combination of c = 1 and αkn = αebh /2 consistently works well in different scenarios; whether these parameters are optimal, or can be chosen in a better way, remains an open question for future work." and (p.7, Remark 5): "In practice, we recommend letting M be the largest acceptable number given the computational constraint of the user."
- Extensions with the same guarantee: fixed-X knockoffs (Theorem A.1, p.16), approximate knockoffs with "approximate FDR control" (p.16; Theorem A.2, p.17), multi-environment knockoffs (Theorem A.3, p.19), side information (Theorem A.4, p.21).

## Reader's inference

1. The randomised ingredient is the knockoff draw; a single draw gives a valid procedure only marginally, and the paper's own figures show null features with high selection probability under a given draw (pp.12, 14). This is the knockoff analogue of a fixed key with an inflated conditional false-positive rate, though the paper frames it as instability rather than as conditional invalidity.
2. The remedy is aggregation across independent draws of e-values satisfying a relaxed average-validity condition; because that condition is linear, the guarantee survives averaging without any dependence assumption, and as M grows the output becomes a deterministic function of the data (Section 3.3). The guarantee itself is marginal for every finite M; conditional validity is approached only in the limit.
3. The experimental design (100 data sets × 20 knockoff draws; marginal versus conditional selection probabilities) is the methodological template the gate should borrow for measuring key-conditional versus key-marginal false-positive rates.
4. Most decisive quotation: p.11, "Note that there are two sources of variability: the randomness conditional on the dataset (i.e., arising from the knockoffs construction), and that from the dataset itself."
