## Q1 What is random; conditional or marginal

Observed.

- What is derandomised, the data split (p.2): "a limitation of split-conformal inference is that it is randomized—its results for a given data set are unpredictable because they depend on how the reference samples are divided between the training and calibration subsets." and for AdaDetect (p.4): "AdaDetect still suffers from the same limitation that it must calibrate its inferences based on a single random data subset Dcal , and thus its results remain aleatory."
- Base validity of a conformal p-value (p.4): "the null hypothesis for Xj is true, Ŝj is exchangeable with Ŝi for all i ∈ Dcal , and û(Xj ) is uniformly distributed on {1/(1 + ncal ), 2/(1 + ncal ), . . . , 1}." Dependence through the shared calibration set (p.4): "the p-values û(Xj ) and û(Xj ′ ) for two different test points j, j ′ ∈ Dtest are not independent of one another, even conditional on Dtrain , because they share the same calibration data."
- Why e-values (p.2): "The advantage of e-values is that they make it possible to aggregate the results of mutually dependent tests in a relatively simple way, enabling an effective approach to derandomize conformal inferences."
- The e-values are relaxed (p.5): "it is not a proper e-value according to the original definition of Vovk and Wang (2021), in the sense that its expected value may be larger than one even if Xj is an inlier." "Instead, we will show that our e-values satisfy a relaxed average validity property (Ren and Barber, 2023) that is sufficient to guarantee FDR control while allowing more numerous discoveries."
- Aggregation rule (p.5): over K independent splits, "we aggregate the evidence against the j-th null hypothesis into a single statistic ēj by taking a weighted average:" [ē_j = Σ_k w^(k) e_j^(k), Σ_k w^(k) = 1].
- Assumption and theorem (p.6): "Assumption 3.1. The inliers in D and the null test points are exchangeable conditional on the non-null test points." "Theorem 3.2. Suppose Assumption 3.1 holds. Then, the e-values computed by Algorithm S1 satisfy:" [eq. (6): Σ over null test points of E[ē_j] ≤ n_test]. "Corollary 3.3 (Ren and Barber (2023)). The eBH filter of Wang and Ramdas (2022) applied at level α ∈ (0, 1) to e-values {ēj }j∈Dtest , satisfying (6) guarantees FDR ≤ α."
- Hyper-parameter independence (p.6): "Remark 3.5. Theorem 3.2 holds regardless of the value of the hyper-parameter αbh of Algorithm S1," and (p.7): "Such extension does not affect the validity of (6) due to the linearity of expected values."
- Adaptive weights (p.6): valid "as long as each weight w(k) is invariant to permutations of the test point with the corresponding calibration" samples; "Theorem 3.6. Suppose Assumption 3.1 holds. Then, the e-values computed by Algorithm S3 satisfy (6), as long as the adaptive weights obey (7)."
- What the guarantee averages over (p.7): "The average false discovery proportion defined in (8) is not the FDR, which is the quantity we can theoretically guarantee to" [control] and "with expectation taken with respect all randomness in the data."

Reader's inference. The source of randomness being derandomised is the auxiliary random split (training/calibration), which plays the role of the key. The guarantee after aggregation is still marginal: FDR is an expectation over the data and over the K splits jointly; nothing is claimed conditionally on the realised splits. Aggregation shrinks the split-conditional variability (variance metric (9)) and, as K grows, pushes the procedure toward a deterministic function of the data, but the paper proves no conditional error-rate statement.

## Q2 Fixed key / per-key / conditional vs marginal

Observed.

- The paper separates what it can guarantee (FDR, marginal) from what it measures (average FDP over repeated analyses of fixed data) (p.7): "we assess the performance of each method over M = 100 independent analyses based on the same fixed data and the same test set." then the sentence quoted in Q1 that the average FDP "is not the FDR", followed by "Nonetheless, we will see that this average false discovery proportion is also controlled in practice within all data sets considered in this paper." and the variability metric "we can evaluate the average variance in the rejection events:" [eq. (9)].
- Consistency across repetitions as the target (Figure 1 caption, p.2): colours "indicate how often each test point is reported as a possible outlier over 100 independent analyses of the same data."
- Effect of K (p.8): "the results show that the variability of the findings obtained with E-AdaDetect decreases as K increases. The average proportion of false discoveries obtained with E-AdaDetect also tends to decrease when K is large," explained by "spurious findings are less likely to be reproduced consistently across multiple independent analyses of the same data."
- The words marginal, key (as secret key), Type I, false positive, screening, deployed key: not found (searched: key (pp.4, 10, "key idea" p.4, "key properties" p.10 only), marginal (0), Type I (0), false positive (0), screening (0), deployed (p.7, "deployed using a binary logistic regression classifier"), per-key (0), calibrat (as conformal calibration throughout), fixed (p.7 "same fixed data"; p.5 "fixed rejection threshold"; p.15 "fixed model weights")).

Reader's inference. The paper makes the conditional-versus-marginal distinction explicitly for variability (the "same fixed data" design and metric (9)) and implicitly for validity (FDR is marginal, average FDP on fixed data is what is observed). It does not analyse the split-conditional FDR of a single split, which is the direct analogue of a key-conditional false-positive rate; it instead reduces that conditional variability by averaging.

## Q3 Empirical FPR procedure

Observed.

- Design (p.7): M = 100 repeated analyses of the same data (quoted in Q2); target "methods designed to control the FDR below the target level α = 0.1."
- Synthetic data (p.7): "the test set contains 90% inliers and 10% outliers, independently sampled from N (µ, I100 )." and "The size of the reference set is n = 2000, with 1000 samples in the training subset and 1000 in the calibration subset. The size of the test set is ntest = 1000." E-AdaDetect uses K = 10 splits (p.8; K varied 1–30 in Figure 3, p.9).
- Results (p.8): "The results confirm both methods control the FDR but ours is less variable, as expected." Supplementary check over data draws (p.19): "showing that the FDR is controlled over 100 independent realizations of the data." Real data (p.10, Figure 5 caption): "all while controlling the FDR below the nominal 10% level."
- Hyper-parameter (p.7): "In this paper, we adopt αbh = α/10, which we have observed to work generally well in our context,"
- Real data sets (p.9): musk, shuttle, KDDCup99, credit card, with reference set 3000 inliers, test set 1000, K = 10 (p.29), K = 70 for one-class conformal (p.32).

Reader's inference. The empirical error rate is reported two ways: averaged over 100 re-randomisations of the split for one fixed data set (conditional on data, marginal over splits), and averaged over 100 fresh data realisations (fully marginal); both are compared against the nominal α = 0.1. This two-level design is a usable template for separating key-conditional from key-marginal false-positive rates.

## Q4 Remedies and guarantees

Observed.

- Remedy: martingale-based conformal e-values (eq. (5), p.5) from K independent splits, aggregated by a weighted average (p.5) with permutation-invariant data-driven weights (eq. (7), p.6; t-statistic weighting Algorithm S5, p.16), then the eBH filter (Algorithm S2, p.15).
- Guarantee: Theorem 3.2 / Theorem 3.6 relaxed average validity (6) and Corollary 3.3, FDR ≤ α, under Assumption 3.1 (p.6); robust to αbh (Remark 3.5) and to averaging over αbh values (p.7).
- Comparison with p-to-e calibrators and soft-rank e-values (Supplementary S5, pp.24–29): the martingale e-values are more powerful; the VS calibrator "does not produce a valid e-value" (p.24).

## Reader's inference

1. The derandomised randomness is the data split, the exact analogue of an auxiliary key: a single split gives a valid but aleatory procedure; averaging e-values over K splits gives a procedure whose FDR guarantee remains marginal (expectation over data and splits) while its split-conditional variability falls with K.
2. The paper does not prove, and does not claim, control of the error rate conditional on one realised split; it treats the conditional quantity as something to stabilise empirically (metric (9), "same fixed data" design), not as something to guarantee. For the gate, this is evidence that the standard remedy in the derandomisation literature is aggregation toward the marginal procedure, not per-draw calibration.
3. The relaxed e-value condition (sum of expectations ≤ n_test, p.5–6) and its linearity under averaging (p.7) are the technical reason aggregation over draws is free; a watermark analogue would need a statistic whose expectation under the null is controlled marginally over keys, with averaging over independently generated keys.
4. Most decisive quotation: p.7, "The average false discovery proportion defined in (8) is not the FDR, which is the quantity we can theoretically guarantee to" [control], "with expectation taken with respect all randomness in the data."
