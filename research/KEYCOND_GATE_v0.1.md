# Key-conditional validity of randomised detection tests (C07) — source gate v0.1

Date: 2026-10-08. **Status: PASS** (as a transfer-and-formalisation contribution: theory plus adequacy check plus a small method). The question was named as open in October 2025 and measured once in an ICLR 2026 appendix; no retrieved work gives its theory, its dependence on text length, vocabulary concentration and subdomain, or remedies with guarantees and costs. Search absence is not proof of novelty; one named scoop risk (below).

## Candidate (one sentence, in parts)
**Decision:** a deployer of a key-randomised detection test (a token-level LLM watermark such as KGW, Gumbel-max or inverse-transform; an activation-level watermark; a model fingerprint or a copyright trap) must set a detection threshold and state a false-positive rate for the **one secret key they hold**. **Information set and timing:** the detector sees the text (or the suspect model's outputs) and the key, never the generating distribution; at design time it may also hold a corpus of null (human) texts. **Target:** the conditional false-positive rate given the deployed key, P(reject | H0, key = k), and its dispersion across keys for a fixed human-text distribution, against the nominal level α that is exact only on average over the key draw. **Comparator:** the marginal tests as published (the KGW z-score; Kuditipudi et al.'s permutation p-value over fresh keys; the pivotal statistics and optimal rules of Li, Ruan, Wang, Long and Su; e-process detection), with the known repair for within-text repetition (scoring unique n-grams only). **Failure mechanism:** marginal validity over the key draw is not conditional validity for a fixed key. A human-text distribution with concentrated n-gram frequencies makes some keys' green lists or pseudorandom scores systematically favoured, so the per-key false-positive rate sits above α for some keys and below for others; the dispersion should shrink with the effective number of independent key-context draws a text exposes (context width, vocabulary concentration, repetition). Existing methods state the marginal guarantee and leave the per-key rate uncharacterised and unremedied.

## Residual gap (type: theory + adequacy check + small method)
Observed: no retrieved work (i) defines the key-conditional false-positive rate α_P(k) of a key-randomised detection test as the object, (ii) gives a law for its dispersion across keys, (iii) measures that dispersion as a function of text length, context width, statistic, corpus and subdomain beyond one appendix table, or (iv) evaluates remedies (per-key null calibration with a sample-size rule; stratified key splits; context width; key ensembles or e-value aggregation over keys) for their per-key guarantee and power cost.
Inference (my derivation, a proposal to be pre-registered, not a result): for a green-list statistic with context width h, write p_h for the law of (h+1)-tuples under the human-text distribution P and G_k(u) ∈ {0,1} for the key's colouring. For a fixed key the null mean of the green fraction is γ + δ_k with δ_k = Σ_u p_h(u)(G_k(u) − γ); over the key draw E δ_k = 0 and Var δ_k = γ(1−γ) C_h, where C_h = Σ_u p_h(u)² is the collision probability of the tuple law (the inverse effective number of distinct tuples). For the z-test on n scored tuples the key-conditional level is about Φ̄(z_α − δ_k √(n/(γ(1−γ)))), so the dispersion is governed by √(n C_h): it grows with text length and falls steeply with context width (C_0 ≫ C_1 ≫ C_2 for Zipfian text), which is the ordering in Gloaguen et al.'s Table 2 and the length effect Fernandez et al. describe; it depends on P, hence on subdomain and language (Jovanović et al.'s Cyrillic remark); the same structure holds for Gumbel and inverse-transform pivots with the key's score function in place of G_k, and for an activation-level statistic with a shared component (the previous project's lesson). Remedies follow with costs: per-key null calibration needs N null tuples with N ≫ 1/C_h and a null corpus matching deployment (SAC-Copula's portability failure); stratifying the key by tuple frequency removes the between-stratum part of Var δ_k (the generalisation of Jovanović et al.'s fix); context width trades C_h against edit robustness (Kirchenbauer et al. 2024); averaging over K independent keys divides Var δ_k by K at a design cost (multi-key embedding; Fu & Russell's multiplicity). The training-conditional analogy (Vovk; Bian & Barber) and the stability route (Liang & Barber) give the paper its statistical frame.

## Is the residual gap meaningful? (who would act differently)
- A deployer holds one key: they would calibrate the threshold on their own null texts per key, per language and domain, with a stated sample size, instead of trusting the marginal level; today's practice (SynthID's empirical quantile; TextSeal's screening) does this ad hoc.
- A scheme designer would choose statistics and key constructions whose key-conditional level concentrates (larger context width, stratified splits, multi-key averaging) and would report per-key dispersion beside the marginal rate, as Gloaguen et al. began to.
- An auditor or standard-setter would ask for key-conditional (and subdomain-conditional) false-positive statements; NIST AI 100-4 already asks which "edge cases that are not captured by average metrics may be important" (p.40), and the subdomain effect is a fairness question (a fixed key accusing one language's writers more often).
- A methodologist gets a clean instance of training-conditional validity outside predictive inference, with a closed-form dispersion law.
Whichever way the measurements come out (dispersion negligible at deployed context widths and lengths, or material), the result changes what a deployer should report. Verdict on meaning: yes.

## Data and precision feasibility (nominal counts labelled nominal)
- Null side: hash-and-count on human corpora, no model; candidate corpora whose licence texts are checked at Stage 4: C4 realnewslike (ODC-BY, unverified), WikiText-103 (CC BY-SA 3.0, unverified), HC3 human answers (CC BY-SA 4.0, card field), per-language Wikipedia extracts for the subdomain study (CC BY-SA 4.0, unverified). Nominal design: 500–1,000 keys × 5,000–50,000 texts per corpus × statistics (KGW with h ∈ {0,…,4}; Gumbel; inverse-transform; a SynthID-style tournament if the open code's licence allows; the previous project's activation statistic as a labelled transfer); minutes to hours on the CPU.
- Power side: watermarked generation with Qwen2.5-1.5B or Llama-3.2-1B/3B at 111–268 tokens/s batched (compute check): 2,000–5,000 texts per setting, one to three hours each.
- Independent units: keys (hundreds) and texts (thousands); per-key FPR at α = 1% needs about 10⁴ null texts for a 10% relative standard error (nominal; the statistical-power skill sets the counts at Stage 5). Theory and simulation need no data.
- Rights: public access does not grant redistribution; corpora stay local with hashed manifests; only statistics are published.

## Wording constraints for any draft
1. Never "first to observe": cite Jovanović et al. 2025 (named open), Gloaguen et al. 2026 (first LLM measurement), Fernandez et al. 2026 and TextSeal (mechanism in words; key screening), Three Bricks (the h = 0 residual).
2. The frame is a transfer of training-conditional validity (Vovk 2012; Bian & Barber 2023; Liang & Barber 2025) and of Ren & Barber's two sources of variability; say so in the introduction.
3. Within-text collisions (Fernandez et al. 2023; Kirchenbauer et al. 2024; Cai et al. 2025) and many-keys multiplicity (Fu & Russell 2025) are distinct, known mechanisms; never conflate them with the fixed-key effect.
4. Per-key null calibration exists in practice (Aremu et al. 2026; implicitly Xie et al. 2025); claim only the theory, the sample-size rule and the measured costs.
5. Marginal theory (Li et al.; Huang et al.; Kuditipudi et al.) is correct on its own terms; the paper adds a conditional statement, it does not refute them.
6. SynthID: only what the Nature paper's text says; no claim about deployment.
7. The previous project's activation statistic enters as one setting, labelled a transfer of its own result.
