## 1. What the studies need from the data
Null (unwatermarked, key-independent) text, tokenised with pinned tokenizers, across **registers**, **languages** and **lengths**, for: Study 2 (the per-key false-positive rate of each statistic measured over hundreds of keys on the same texts; its dispersion against text length, context width, statistic, corpus and subdomain); Study 3 (per-key calibration and its transfer across corpora and languages; one alternative remedy); Study 4 (prompts for watermarked generation on one model). Study 1 (theory and simulation) needs no data; the empirical occupancy weights of the open partition (collision statistics per corpus, width, retention rule and tokenizer) are an input to its simulation design.

**The null-text target.** The null hypothesis of a watermark test is "not watermarked with this key": any text produced without knowledge of the key is a null sample, whether written by a person or by a model without the watermark. The target is the **key-independent null**, not human authorship; a vintage is provenance, not proof of authorship. Because the object of study is the text law itself, every document keeps its source and whatever vintage field its source provides (§7), measurements are reported by source stratum, and no AI-text detector is used to certify the population.

## 4. Proceed rule (fixed before any download; conjunctive; only Yichen may relax it)
All counts are of **open-partition, deduplicated, eligible units** (one representative per duplicate cluster, §5) after cleaning and the final partition assignment; counts before filters, before clustering or before the split do not count. Eligibility is defined under the Llama-3.2 tokenizer; the Qwen-eligible subset is reported beside it and matched-document analyses use the intersection. Precision statements are nominal relative standard errors of a per-key rate at a true rate of 1% with independent units: 0.10 percentage points at N = 10,000 (10%), 0.20 at N = 2,500 (20%), 0.70 at N = 200 (70%); at smaller true rates the relative error is larger.
- **P1 Licence.** Every frozen document's licence label is one of the complete label strings enumerated in the loader (five strings observed in the data, mapped byte-for-byte to cc-by-4.0, cc-by-sa-2.5, cc-by-sa-3.0, cc-by-sa-4.0, public-domain; no pattern matching); for a Stack Exchange thread `all_licenses` must be a non-empty list every entry of which is admitted (no fallback to `license`); Gutenberg is restricted to "Public Domain"; for Wikipedia the project licence inherited by every article is recorded once; a document with a missing, `None`, malformed or other label is excluded and counted. P1 is computed from the admitted units and is part of the verdict. Tokenizers under Apache-2.0 and the Llama 3.2 Community License (local use). No exception to the brief's rule is in force.
- **P2 Registers.** Three English registers (news, encyclopedic, forum), each with ≥ 10,000 units of ≥ 256 tokens; the forum register with ≥ 20 sites of ≥ 200 eligible threads each (representation only: sites and outlets are descriptive strata, never inferential cells); register comparisons are made at a common length.
- **P3 Languages.** Five non-English Wikipedia languages (de, ru, ar, zh, ja), each with ≥ 10,000 articles of ≥ 256 tokens; claims at longer lengths need their own counts.
- **P4 Length.** ≥ 5,000 units of ≥ 1,024 tokens in each of two sources (Gutenberg windows; Wikipedia-en), with cross-source deduplication enforced before counting.
- **P5 Reconciliation.** Every frozen file's repo id, path, revision, UTC time, byte size and SHA-256 in `outputs/corpora_manifest_v0.1.json`, with the Hub's own LFS SHA-256 recorded beside the local hash as the independent checksum for the 13 data files (the four READMEs have none). A file with a decompression failure or a failed accounting identity (physical lines = parsed + unparseable) is **quarantined before any count**, with the reason recorded. **JSON-lines shards (Common Pile):** decompression failures, physical lines, successfully parsed records and semantic exclusions are counted separately per file (malformed input is recorded or rejected, never counted as admitted); the one complete source (news) reconciles **exactly** to the card's 126,673 records; for a partial selection of shards the card total is a whole-source comparator only (a smaller shard total is expected, not a failure). **Parquet files (Wikipedia):** the footer row count reconciles to the rows read. A file with a mismatch is excluded **with the reason recorded**, never patched, and an excluded file never lets a required count pass. A count flow (raw records → parsed units → clusters → eligible units → open and held-out) is kept per source.
- **P6 Holdout and permitted preprocessing.** The seal (§5.7) binds to the finalised map; the fixture suite `tools/test_seal.py` and the audit's accessor test pass before any **scientific** use of text. Permitted on both partitions before the seal: parsing, licence and language checks, tokenisation for length eligibility, exact and near-duplicate detection (which reads text and emits counts and cluster identities only). Not permitted on sealed units: detector scores, collision or occupancy estimates, calibration, example reading or selection.
- **P7 Confirmatory precision (binding).** The ten target confirmatory cells are: news-en, wiki-en, forum-en and wiki-{de, ru, ar, zh, ja} at 256 tokens, and wiki-en and books at 1,024 tokens. Each included cell must hold **≥ 2,500 held-out eligible units** after exclusions. A target cell below the floor after the capped expansion (P8) is **excluded from confirmatory claims before the lock**, the audit reports it as "failed/excluded" separately from a full ten-cell success (so that the reduced final scope is visible), and the final protocol may not claim it. There is no other fallback.
- **P8 Expansion and failure.** Initial shards are those in §3. If a count fails, further shards are added in this fixed order and no other: Wikipedia, `train-00001` then `train-00002` of the language concerned; StackExchange, `-0011` then `-0033`; Gutenberg, `-0001` then `-0005`; news has no further shard, so a failed news count is a FAIL. Expansion happens **before** the final clustering and partition assignment (§5), never after any scientific use; the hash split is never re-drawn. If a mandatory count still fails after the cap, the audit records FAIL for that cell (P7) and, if a P2–P4 count fails, FAIL overall; Yichen decides.
The crossed key-by-document analysis (keys and documents as random factors; units after deduplication; confidence levels; simultaneous against per-cell claims) is specified in each protocol; the holdout floor and the unit definition above are fixed at the freeze.

## 5. Holdout rule: **option A decided by Yichen on 2026-10-09 (R01 decision D3)**; deterministic specification
1. **Canonical identifier** per document: Wikipedia `<lang>:<id>`; Common Pile `metadata.url` (news article, thread, ebook), with the record's `id` as a fallback namespaced by source.
2. **Normalisation and clustering** (frozen before use): text normalised (NFC, lower case, whitespace collapsed, punctuation stripped); exact duplicates by SHA-256 of the normalised text; near duplicates: candidate pairs through LSH over MinHash signatures (128 permutations, seed 20261009) of the 5-gram shingles of the normalised-text prefix (20,000 characters; 8,000 for character shingles), **32 bands × 4 rows (D4)**, every pair within a bucket considered; each candidate pair **confirmed by the exact Jaccard similarity of the full-text shingle sets, ≥ 0.65 (D4)**; clusters are the connected components (union–find, transitive); run **across all frozen sources and languages at once**. **Edge rules (R01 round 3, F21; refined at implementation and at R02):** shingle units are characters when the normalised text is CJK-dominant (≥ 30% of the first 2,000 non-space characters in the Han, kana, Hangul or full-width ranges; Chinese and Japanese Wikipedia fall here, where punctuation-delimited clauses would make word shingles too coarse), otherwise whitespace-separated words when the normalised text has ≥ 5 of them, otherwise characters; a text with fewer than 5 units of either kind yields no shingles and forms a singleton cluster (exact-hash matching still applies; empty shingle sets are never matched to each other); LSH is approximate (at the chosen settings a pair of true shingle Jaccard 0.65 is proposed with probability 1 − (1 − 0.65⁴)³² ≈ 0.998, and 0.70 with ≈ 0.99985; this is candidate coverage, not linkage coverage), the estimate-against-exact confusion of every candidate is recorded, and residual near-duplicate leakage (pairs never proposed, and content rewritten beyond the threshold) is a stated limitation; the preprocessing is validated before use on planted duplicates in each script; canonical identifiers are unique by construction (namespaced), and any residual tie is broken by (source, record id, SHA-256 of the normalised text).
3. **Cluster identity and unit:** the cluster's identifier is the lexicographically smallest canonical identifier among its members; the analysis unit is one representative per cluster (the member with the smallest canonical identifier), with the cluster size recorded; non-representative members are never scored.
4. **Assignment:** a cluster is held out when `int(sha256(cluster_id)[:16], 16) % 5 == 0`; clusters containing any exposure-record document (§2) are forced open, the exposure identifiers having first been resolved to stable records (the news entries are `metadata.provenance` values `news-dolma-0000.json.gz:1` … `:30`, matched on that field, not on physical line offsets; the Gutenberg entries are ebook ids matched on the record id, which is the id in `metadata.url`); the realised held-out fraction is counted afterwards and reported (it is approximate and can be below 20%).
5. **Order of operations (the provisional loop, R01 round 3, F21):** freeze → parse, licence and eligibility audit → **provisional** clustering, assignment and counts on the current pool → if a P2–P7 count fails, add the next shard allowed by P8 and repeat the provisional step (within the cap; the hash seed and the rule never change; a provisional cluster identifier may change when members are added, which is harmless before any exposure) → the **final** cluster-to-partition map, written once → seal test → scientific use. A later provenance correction that links a used open unit to a held-out unit **quarantines** that cluster from confirmation; nothing already used moves into the sealed sample.
6. **Keys:** development keys are seeds 0–999 of a named PRNG stream (NumPy PCG64 with the seed as the stream key); confirmatory keys are a disjoint range (10,000–10,999) fixed in the final lock and never used before it; a screened deployed key is evaluated on text it was not screened on. The ideal independent-key law and the PRNG implementation are distinguished in every protocol.
7. **Seal (v2):** the sealed accessor binds to the finalised partition map `partition_v0.2.parquet` and refuses to bind if the map's digest differs from `partition_v0.2.sha256`; every requested unit is resolved against that map (an unknown id, or an id in another partition than requested, is refused); the held-out partition opens only for a lock file whose SHA-256 is registered in `outputs/corpora_seal_allowlist.json` and which carries `final: true`, the study name (which must match the request), the manifest digest, the partition digest and a non-empty list of cells containing the requested cells; every opening is logged with the study, the cells, the unit count and the partition digest; analyses that change hypotheses after the opening are exploratory.
Options B (English sources only) and C (one whole source sealed) were the alternatives; key reservation would have applied under every option.

## 7. Definitions found in the cards (what a count and a date mean)
- **Common Pile (Kandpal et al. 2025, arXiv 2506.05209):** "public domain and openly licensed text"; per-document licence in `metadata.license`; the "filtered" versions are what trained Comma v0.1; every card warns of possible licence misassignment. **Dolma JSON-lines shards.** News: about twenty outlets listed by opennewswire as CC BY or CC BY-SA; title and byline prefixed to the text; `created` is the outlet's publication string (for example "Published on December 13, 2021"). StackExchange: community dumps of December 2024 plus the last official dumps of July 2024; one document per question with its comments, answers (accepted first, then by votes) and their comments, Markdown converted to plain text; `created` is the **question's** creation time, which does not date later answers, comments or edits. Gutenberg: English books marked public domain plus PG19; headers and footers removed but "many scanned books include preamble information"; **no creation field**, so the vintage is "unknown (public domain)" per document and not "pre-1929" per document.
- **Vintage schema:** news, the publication string; StackExchange, the question's creation time (a lower bound for the thread); Wikipedia, the dump date 2023-11-01 (content accumulated over years); Gutenberg, unknown. Vintage comparisons are made only where a field exists, and "pre-LLM" is never asserted per document.
- **wikimedia/wikipedia:** "cleaned articles of all languages" from the 20231101 dumps, parsed with `mwparserfromhell`, "markdown and unwanted sections (references, etc.)" removed; one row per article including stubs; the dumps site no longer lists that date.
- **C4 realnewslike (not frozen):** card counts 13,799,838 train and 13,863 validation; the TensorFlow Datasets catalogue gives 13,804,817 for its own version.

## 8. Limitations and not done
- Token counts are capped-prefix counts (12,000 characters; 24,000 for books): a long document's count is censored, flagged, and does not establish full-text eligibility or ineligibility above the cap (two open books, ids 13286 and 61765, have capped counts of 2,930 and 2,634 tokens although their texts run to 585,527 and 1,472,520 characters; they fail the 3,048-token test for a 2,048-token window on the prefix convention only). Medians quoted in §9 are prefix medians.
- The Common Pile's licence labels are the collectors' best effort; the paper states this and keeps the per-document labels. The allow-list establishes application of the declared labels, not the copyright status of every document.
- Post-2022 content may include unwatermarked LLM text: a valid key-independent null; reported by source stratum, never as "human-only".
- A shard is not a random sample of its source (Common Pile shards appear ordered by site or id); the target population is the frozen shards, and conclusions are stated for them.
- The compute cost of the null-data grid is not covered by the brief's model benchmarks; it is timed with the actual implementation at Stage 5 validation, and any efficient surrogate for KGW's seeded permutation is labelled as such.
- SynthID-Text code and any Gemma tokenizer are Stage 5 inputs, not covered here.
