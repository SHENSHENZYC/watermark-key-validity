## 1. Question and type
**Adequacy check of the first-order theory, by simulation.** For the red–green (KGW-style) z-test deployed with one fixed key, the gate gives (i) an **exact identity** for the across-key variance of the per-key mean green fraction under the fixed-size and the independent colouring models, for any weights, and (ii) a **first-order approximation** to the across-key dispersion of the per-key false-positive rate. Study 1 asks, on fully specified synthetic text laws where every quantity is computable: Q1, does the exact identity hold in simulation (it must, up to key-sampling error: a check of the simulator and the finite-population correction, R2–R3); Q2, is the first-order predictor of the across-key SD of the level adequate, and where does it fail (R1); Q3, how does the dispersion behave with text length under each repeated-context rule, including whether it saturates (H-length, target T1; R4 reported as a rule only for the raw rule); Q4, how does it behave with context width (H-width; R5). No real text is used; no real-corpus false-positive rate is measured (Study 2).

## 2. Information timing
Everything is synthetic and generated inside the run from fixed seeds. The detector knows the key; the simulation knows the text law. The only "deployed" quantity is the per-key level over a fixed key; the key law is the uniform draw over the seed range. The held-out corpus partition is not touched (the lock carries `final: false`).

## 3. Target, sign, units
Per cell (law, width h, length L, retention rule, colouring model, γ): the per-key level α̂_k at nominal α ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.001} over N texts, for K keys; the across-key SD of α̂_k corrected for text-sampling noise (SD_corr); the per-key mean green fraction q̂_k and its across-key variance (exactly Σ_u ŵ(u)G_k(u) with the realised document-averaged weights ŵ); the exact-identity variance at ŵ (fixed-size and independent); the first-order predicted SD of the level; the key quantiles and the worst sampled key. Units: keys (K = 500 for the core laws with the independent colouring; 200 for the fixed colouring on the core subset, which is a reported comparison only; 2,000 for the illustration laws) and texts (N = 4,000 per chain, shared across keys and truncated for each L). Sign convention: a "larger dispersion" means a larger SD_corr; a predictor is "adequate" when the observed-to-predicted ratio lies within [2/3, 3/2].

## 4. Data, split and holdout
None (synthetic). Text laws: (a) i.i.d. Zipf over V tokens with exponent s: (V, s) ∈ {(1,000, 1.0), (10,000, 1.0), (50,000, 1.0), (10,000, 1.2)}; (b) first-order Markov–Zipf chains with per-context supports of S = 50 tokens and global-escape probability ε = 0.1: V ∈ {1,000, 10,000}; (c) illustration laws, reported separately and excluded from R1: Zipf V = 10, Zipf V = 100, the reviewer's two-token chain (p_stay = 0.9). Texts of length L_max = 2,048 are generated once per (law, h) chain from PCG64 seeded by `text_seed_base + sha256(law name | h)`; each L ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024, 2,048} is a prefix. Keys are seeds 0–499 (core; 0–199 for the fixed colouring on the core subset) or 0–1,999 (illustration) of the development range; confirmatory keys (10,000–10,999) are not used. The colourings of a chain are computed once per (colouring, γ) over the whole tuple dictionary and reused for every length and rule (the same realisation across cells of a chain, so curves within a chain are paired). The corpora's sealed holdout is untouched (`final: false`).

## 5. Competitors and baselines
The detector under study is the green-fraction z-test with centre γ and denominator √(Mγ(1−γ)), threshold z_α, γ ∈ {0.5, 0.25 (two laws only)}, with three repeated-context rules: **raw** (every position scored), **distinct** (each distinct (h+1)-tuple once per text; Three Bricks), **first** (the first token after each distinct context once per text; textGrain's rule), and two colouring models: **independent** (green iff U(key, tuple) < γ; the implementation surrogate for Study 2) and **fixed** (per context class a uniformly random ⌊γV⌋-subset, realised exactly on the queried tokens by a hypergeometric count and a uniform choice; KGW's seeded permutation), the latter on the illustration laws and on the core subset h ∈ {0, 1}, L ∈ {256, 2,048}. The comparator statement is the first-order predictor: SD_pred = φ(z_α) · E[√M] · SD_K(q̂) / √(γ(1−γ)), with SD_K(q̂) the exact identity at ŵ under the cell's colouring.

## 6. Primary metric and contrast
**R1 (adequacy, primary):** over the core cells with the independent colouring, γ = 0.5, rules raw and distinct, mean M ≥ 30 and SD_pred ≥ 0.05 percentage points at α = 0.01, the ratio SD_corr / SD_pred lies in [2/3, 3/2] in at least 80% of cells. Failing cells are reported by (law, rule, h, L). Secondary at α = 0.05 and 0.001 (descriptive).

## 7. Secondary diagnostics (descriptive)
The level-SD curves against L under each rule (T1: saturation and non-monotonicity allowed); the key quantiles (1%, 5%, 50%, 95%, 99%) and the worst sampled key per cell; the share of keys above 2α and 5α; the fraction of texts with M = 0 (no detection) per cell; the collision statistics A, B and C_cond at the realised weights; the "first" rule's behaviour at h = 0 (one token per text, by construction); the exact ratio fixed/independent on the core subset (reported with its key-sampling error, expected indistinguishable from 1 at V ≥ 1,000).

## 8. Uncertainty
Keys are independent draws: the across-key SD estimate has relative Monte Carlo error ≈ 1/√(2K) (3.2% at K = 500; 1.6% at 2,000). Texts are shared across keys within a chain: the per-key level has binomial text-sampling variance α_k(1−α_k)/N_eff, removed in expectation by the noise correction SD_corr² = Var_K(α̂_k) − mean_k α̂_k(1−α̂_k)/N_eff (negative corrected variances are reported as such and treated as "below the noise floor"); the overall rejection fraction R̄ = mean over keys of α̂_k has two sampling components in the crossed design, keys and texts: SE² = SD_corr²/K + Var_i(r̂_i)/N_eff, with r̂_i the per-text rejection fraction over keys (the text component) and SD_corr² the noise-corrected across-key variance (the key component); the input validation found the text component alone understated the SE by a factor of about three on the validation cell, which is why both are carried. R4's and R5's monotonicity checks allow two such errors per adjacent pair. Seeds: text_seed_base 20261010, key seeds 0–499 / 0–1,999, colouring hash splitmix64 with the key mixed in, hypergeometric draws seeded by (key, 20261010); all fixed in `grid_v0.1.json` and `s1_core.py`.

## 9. Pre-registered rules and materiality thresholds
- **R1 adequacy (pass / fail):** as in §6; pass if ≥ 80% of the eligible core cells are within the band.
- **R2 simulator validity (pass / fail):** under the independent colouring and the distinct rule, for every cell and α, the overall rejection fraction lies within 3 SE (both components, §8) of the exact binomial key-mean level (computable in closed form for each text: P(Bin(M, γ) > γM + z_α√(Mγ(1−γ)))); pass if ≥ 99% of the (cell, α) checks hold. A failure here invalidates the simulator, not the theory.
- **R3 finite-population correction (pass / fail):** for every cell with both colourings, the observed ratio of the across-key variances of q̂ (fixed / independent) lies within 3 key-sampling SEs (≈ ratio·√(4/K)) of the exact ratio (V − 1/C_cond)/(V − 1); pass if ≥ 95% hold; and the reported statement "the relative reduction is below 1% for V ≥ 10,000" is confirmed or refuted from the exact ratio at the realised weights.
- **R4 H-length under the raw rule (pass / fail):** in every core chain (law, h, γ) with the independent colouring, SD_corr at α = 0.01 is non-decreasing in L within noise; pass if every chain holds. Under the distinct and first rules the curves are reported with no monotonicity claim (T1).
- **R5 H-width under the raw rule (pass / fail):** in every core chain (law, L, γ) with the independent colouring, the collision sum A at the raw weights is non-increasing in h, and SD_K(q̂) is non-increasing in h within noise; pass if every chain holds for both.
- **Integrity checks:** the runner verifies the lock (protocol, code and grid hashes) before any study cell; the illustration laws are excluded from R1, R4, R5; cells with M = 0 texts record their share; the two-token chain's h = 0 and h = 1 fixed-size variances are reported beside the reviewer's 0 and 0.08.

## 10. Stop and pivot rules, and what a null result means
If the validation projects more than the 6-hour budget, the grid is reduced in the pre-registered order (K 500 → 300; N 4,000 → 2,000; drop γ = 0.25; drop L = 2,048) before the lock, never after it. If R2 fails, the run stops and the simulator is corrected in a new version (the failed version is kept). If R1 fails, the paper's theory section reports the predictor's range of validity from the failing cells, and Study 2 uses the empirically calibrated form; the research question stays answerable by measurement. R4 or R5 failing refutes the corresponding hypothesis for the simulated laws, which is itself a result of the paper (the gate states them as hypotheses). There is no pivot of the project on Study 1's outcome.
