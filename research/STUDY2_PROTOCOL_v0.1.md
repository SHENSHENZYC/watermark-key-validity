## 1. Question and type
**Applied evaluation of deployed-style detectors on real null text, plus an out-of-sample adequacy check of Study 1's first-order theory (target T3).** Study 1 established, on synthetic laws, that the exact across-key identities hold and that the common-factor first-order predictor of the across-key SD of the per-key false-positive level is adequate at context widths h ≥ 1 (152 of 152 resolved cells consistent, ratios 0.79–1.20) and unresolved at h = 0. Study 2 asks, on the frozen null corpora (ten register–language–length cells; two pinned tokenizers), for the red–green (KGW-style) z-test and for the Gumbel-max test with their published repeated-context rules: **Q1**, how dispersed is the per-key false-positive rate α_P(k) across keys, by width, retention rule, length, tokenizer, register and language (the deployer's view, with crossed key-by-document uncertainty: gate constraint 12; R04 F7); **Q2**, is Study 1's predictor adequate on real text at h ≥ 1 (the primary rule S1), and at h = 0, where a predictor that simulates the across-key law of the mean shift from the realised weights of the heaviest tuples (a moment-matched shape diagnostic, §3) is reported beside it (the discreteness-and-skew hypothesis Study 1 left open; R04 F4); **Q3**, do H-length and H-width hold on real text for the observed dispersion (S4, S5); **Q4**, how far does one key's level transfer across registers, languages and tokenizers (H-domain; the correlation structure of the per-key levels, descriptive; the quantitative transfer theory is Study 3's). No remedy is evaluated, no power side is measured, no held-out text is touched, and no theorem is claimed.

## 3. Target, sign, units
Per cell (corpus cell c, tokenizer τ, family F, width h, length L, retention rule, colouring, γ), over the N_eff texts with M_i > 0 retained positions and K keys:
- **Primary target (conditional on the realised texts):** the per-key level α̂_k = N_eff⁻¹ Σ_i 1{reject_i(k)} and its across-key SD, SD_K(α̂_k), with a key-bootstrap interval (200 resamples; keys are independent draws). No text-noise correction: the texts are fixed and the finite-text term is part of the target (Study 1 §3).
- **The predictor (Study 1's, unchanged):** the moment-matched common-factor approximation whose inputs are exact second moments from the identities at the realised weights (μ_δ, ρ̄, σ²; `s1_core.population_estimates`, `mixture_predictor`) and whose structure is an assumption (one Gaussian shift common to all texts; residuals independent across texts with one variance). For the Gumbel family the same model carries the exact per-text Gamma threshold in z-units and is evaluated **in closed form** (`s2_core.mixture_gaussian_closed`: E[α_k] = mean_i Φ̄(a_i) with a_i = (τ_i − μ_δ)/√(ρ̄ + σ²); E[α_k²] = the mean over pairs of texts of the bivariate normal orthant probability P(Z_i > τ_i, Z_j > τ_j) at correlation ρ̄/(ρ̄ + σ²) through Owen's T over the distinct thresholds; the finite-text floor (E[α_k] − mean_i P(Z_i > τ_i, Z_i′ > τ_i))/N_eff; exact for every σ² ≥ 0, including σ = 0 where the level is the indicator mean; for one threshold it is Study 1's closed form exactly, which is checked in every KGW cell and recorded as `pred_per_text_vs_closed_form_abs_diff_sd`); σ² = mean_i Var_K(z_i) − ρ̄ with Var s = 1 and μ_δ = 0 exactly (E s = 1). No quadrature enters a Gaussian-shift prediction (a fixed Gauss–Hermite rule cannot integrate the indicator at σ = 0; R05 F6). Diagnostics: the unit-variance variant; the pairwise Gaussian predictor on two disjoint 300-text subsamples with the covariance-heterogeneity diagnostics (Study 1 §3; for Gumbel with Var s = 1 and per-text thresholds); the theory share; the σ² flags.
- **The simulated-shift predictor (new; secondary; a diagnostic of the shift's non-Gaussianity):** the across-key law of the mean shift δ = Σ_u ŵ_z(u)(score_u − E score) is simulated from the realised document-averaged weights with **fresh ideal colourings or scores** (not keys): the T = 2,000 heaviest tuples exactly (Bernoulli(g) − g; Exp(1) − 1), the remainder as a Gaussian with its exact variance, B = 20,000 draws, the sample rescaled to variance ρ̄ (the realised-weight identity carries the finite-text part too); the common-factor model is then averaged directly over this discrete sample (`mixture_general`: the mean over the B draws of the texts' rejection probabilities and of their squares) with the same σ² and thresholds. The rescaling matches the sample's variance to ρ̄ in expectation over the simulation (not exactly in a finite sample); the predictor is a **moment-matched shape diagnostic** that keeps the realised weights' heavy-tuple shape, the Gaussian residual and the common-factor structure, and is not the exact law of the population shift. If it resolves cells the Gaussian-shift form leaves unresolved by more than the simulation and subsampling noise, the shift's discreteness or skew is the suggested cause; if both fail alike, the residual (the discreteness of a short count) or the structure is. Independent colouring only. Reported, never decided on.
- **Secondary population target:** the two-way decomposition s_K² − MS_E/N_eff with a two-way bootstrap SE (Study 1 §3).
- **Also per cell:** q̂_k (KGW: the per-key mean green fraction; Gumbel: s̄_k, the per-key mean score, E = 1 exactly) with the known-mean variance estimator against the identity (S3); z̄_k; the M distribution; the coverage; the between-text collision Ã; the key quantiles (1, 5, 50, 95, 99%), the sampled worst and best keys, the shares of keys above 2α and 5α; the exact binomial key-mean under independent colours and the distinct rule (KGW) or exactly α (Gumbel, distinct); the structural-zero flag; **on the deployer subset** (§7) the **crossed key-by-document bootstrap** (200 resamples of the texts, by multinomial weights, and of the keys, with replacement) of the quantiles, the shares and the sampled maximum (percentile intervals; the maximum's interval is the sampled maximum's under resampling, never a population extreme). Per-key storage: the per-key level counts (integers k = α̂_k·N_eff, exact) at every α; q̂_k or s̄_k to 6 decimals; no per-document record. **What the record supports:** every key-only statistic (key means, SDs, quantiles, exceedance shares, endpoint and tie counts, correlations and paired differences across cells, fresh key-only bootstraps) can be recomputed from the counts; the crossed key-by-document intervals, the two-way residual component and any document-level curve cannot be reconstructed from the committed cells and are therefore computed inside the run and stored (the crossed block on the deployer subset, with every promised key asserted by the analyzer); the S3 estimates and SEs are the record (the 6-decimal arrays do not reproduce them to full precision); a separately authorised recomputation from the pinned texts and code is a new analysis, not a reading of the record.
Units: keys (K = 1,000, production seeds 22,000–22,999; decision D8) and texts (N = 10,000 per corpus cell, the first 10,000 of the seeded draw; decision D8; the same N texts of a cell shared across keys, widths, lengths (prefixes), rules, families and γ, and **the same texts under both tokenizers**, so every comparison inside a cell is paired). Sign convention: "larger dispersion" means a larger SD_K(α̂_k); the predictor is "adequate" in a cell when the observed-to-predicted ratio lies in [2/3, 3/2] (Study 1's band, kept).

## 5. Competitors and baselines
**Detectors under study.** (i) The red–green z-test: green fraction over the retained positions, z = (G − γM)/√(Mγ(1−γ)) centred at the requested γ with the realised g = ⌊γV⌋/V recorded (both γ ∈ {0.25, 0.5} give integer γV at both V, so g = γ and μ_δ = 0), reject at z > z_α, α ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.001}; **γ = 0.25 is the primary γ** (the published convention of KGW's experiments, of Three Bricks' detection and of Genhatharan's measurement; Study 1's primary was 0.5, kept as a secondary block). Colourings: **independent** (green iff U(key, tuple) < g; the implementation surrogate; every cell) and **fixed-size** (a uniformly random m-subset per context class; exact; at h = 0 only, where the class is the whole vocabulary and the colouring is one hypergeometric draw over the covered tokens per key; at h ≥ 1 the exact fixed-size colouring over V = 128k tokens per context is infeasible at scale and its effect is the identity-known factor (V − 1/C_cond)/(V − 1), reported from the realised weights). (ii) **Gumbel-max** (Aaronson; Three Bricks' Gamma test; TextSeal; vLLM): the per-position score −ln(1 − U(key, tuple)), summed over the retained positions, against the exact Gamma(M, 1) upper-α quantile per text (reject iff the sum exceeds it; in z-units (S − M)/√M the threshold is per text). The same uniforms U(key, tuple) serve both families, so the two families' per-key levels are paired through the keys. **Retention rules** (both families): raw (every position), distinct (each distinct (h+1)-tuple once per text; Three Bricks), first (the first token after each distinct context once per text; textGrain). **Widths** h ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}. **Comparator statements:** Study 1's predictor (§3); the exact binomial key-mean given the texts (S2); the exact identities (S3); the published measurements (Gloaguen et al. 2026: 100 keys, 10,000 C4 texts of 200 tokens, three context settings; Genhatharan 2026: 10 and 128 KGW keys, two tokenizers, 128–512 tokens; AutoMark's soundness-given-a-key test) are the record cited in §12, not re-run.

## 8. Uncertainty
Keys are independent draws given the texts: every across-key statistic carries a key-bootstrap interval (200 resamples). The normal-theory benchmark for the relative error of an SD, 1/√(2(K−1)), is 3.2% at K = 500 and 2.2% at K = 1,000; per-key levels are bounded and skewed, so the bootstrap reflects part of the extra error; for a nearly Bernoulli per-key level with success probability p the delta-method relative SE of its SD is about |1 − 2p|/(2√(K p(1−p))), 15.6% at p = 0.01 and K = 1,000, and a sample with no rare key has a degenerate bootstrap; S2's exact bound governs the key-mean, and S1's eligibility governs the finite-text floor, but neither assures the bootstrap's coverage or the resolution of the tail in such cells (R05 F7). The 95% intervals are nominal; the power table of §11 is an eligibility count with a normal benchmark, not a guarantee that every eligible cell resolves; the report shows, per cell, the number of keys at level 0 and at level 1, the number of distinct levels and the number of keys above 5α (all from the stored counts). Text sampling enters ρ̄, Ã and the identities at the realised weights: a text bootstrap gives their SEs (descriptive here; no rule uses them), with **5 resamples per cell in production** (`grid_v0.1.json` `text_bootstrap_B`; Study 1 used 30; the text bootstrap is the dominant cost of a cell's statistics on a 10-million-position chain, and these SEs decide nothing in Study 2). The KGW cell statistics are a faithful copy of Study 1's function with the resample count as a parameter: with 30 resamples the copy reproduces Study 1's output field for field (checked in the validation on an N = 600, L = 256 synthetic chain at the real vocabulary size). With 5 resamples every deterministic quantity (the identities, ρ̄, σ², the predictors, the per-key levels and their SD) is unchanged, but the text bootstrap consumes the cell's random stream before the key bootstrap, the pairwise subsample, the two-way bootstrap and the diagnostic seeds, so those Monte Carlo realisations (the intervals, not the estimands) differ from what 30 resamples would have given (R05 F5). The crossed key-by-document bootstrap (200) gives the deployer's intervals on the subset. The two-way population estimate's SE is a two-way bootstrap (100). S2 uses no estimated SE (Hoeffding's KL bound, exact given the texts). S3's SE is the exact fourth-moment SE of the known-mean estimator (independent colouring: κ₄ = g(1−g)(1−6g(1−g)); Gumbel: κ₄ = 6) or a key bootstrap (fixed colouring). S4's and S5's tolerances are sums of key-bootstrap SEs for adjacent members and give no simultaneous guarantee. Seeds: `text_seed_base` 20261012 (the draw); the bootstrap and subsample streams per cell from sha256 of the cell file name with 20261013; the simulated-shift and crossed-bootstrap streams derived from the cell stream; the colouring hash is Study 1's splitmix64 with the key mixed in; validation tokens from seed 99261012; all fixed in `grid_v0.1.json`, `s1_core.py` and `s2_core.py`. A hash supplies computational, not statistical, independence; S3 is the empirical check.

## 9. Pre-registered rules and materiality thresholds (every tolerance the code applies is stated here; R04 F1)
- **S1 adequacy (pass / fail):** as in §6. Band [2/3, 3/2]; eligibility mean M ≥ 30 and theory share ≥ 0.75; at least 30 resolved; at least 80% consistent.
- **S2 the key-mean audit (pass / fail; a failure is an audit flag):** under the **distinct** rule, for every live cell of the KGW family (independent colouring, both γ) and of the Gumbel family, and every α ∈ {0.05, 0.01, 0.001}: the key-mean level ā against its exact value given the texts (KGW: mean_i P(Bin(M_i, g) > γM_i + z_α√(M_iγ(1−γ))); Gumbel: exactly α, the scores being continuous), by the two-sided Hoeffding bound min{1, 2·exp(−K·KL(ā ‖ μ_a))}, **flagged below 10⁻⁶** (μ_a = 0: flagged iff ā > 0). S2 passes iff no check is flagged. Calibration: the per-check false-audit probability is at most 10⁻⁶ for any K and any dependence, so the experiment-level false-audit probability is at most the number of checks × 10⁻⁶ (1,020 distinct-rule cells × 3 levels = 3,060 checks: at most 3.1·10⁻³) by the union bound, whatever the dependence between texts or cells (the keys are independent draws of the ideal key law); power (the bound's price, computed by root-finding on the two-sided bound; the table is in `VALIDATION.json`): at K = 1,000 and μ_a = 0.01 the rule flags ā ≥ 0.0312 and cannot flag any under-rejection (even ā = 0 has bound 2(1 − μ_a)^K = 8.6·10⁻⁵); at μ_a = 0.05 it flags ā ≥ 0.0912 or ā ≤ 0.0177; at μ_a = 0.001, ā ≥ 0.0102; at K = 500 the thresholds are Study 1's (0.0422 at μ_a = 0.01; 0.1104 or 0.0076 at 0.05; 0.0158 at 0.001); a dead detector (key-mean 0) is flaggable iff 2(1 − μ_a)^K < 10⁻⁶, that is iff the exact key-mean μ_a exceeds 0.01440 at K = 1,000 (0.02860 at K = 500); by the key-free inputs at N = 10,000 that holds in every distinct-rule cell at α = 0.05, in 20 of the 680 KGW distinct-rule cells and in none of the 340 Gumbel cells at α = 0.01 (where μ_a is exactly 0.01), and in no cell at α = 0.001. Under-rejection is therefore flaggable at nominal 0.01 only in those 20 KGW cells (for example gutenberg/en@1024 under Llama at h = 4, L = 64, distinct, γ = 0.25, exact mean 0.01526: an observed key-mean of 0 has the two-sided bound 4.2·10⁻⁷) and in no Gumbel cell; at nominal 0.001 in no cell; the bound depends on the exact cell mean, not on the nominal level. Structural zeros (exact 0 and observed 0) are counted separately. On real text this check is also the test that the hash-based colouring behaves as the ideal colouring on real tuples at the level of the key-mean; subtler faults are S3's business. A failure invalidates no theory: it calls for an implementation audit, and no outcome is reported until it is resolved in a new version (the failed version is kept).
- **S3 the exact identities (pass / fail):** for every live cell, the known-mean variance estimator s₀² = K⁻¹ Σ_k (m̂_k − E m̂)² of the per-key mean score (KGW: q̂_k with E q̂ = g Σ_u ŵ_q(u); Gumbel: s̄_k with E s̄ = 1) against the exact identity at ŵ_q under the cell's colouring (independent: g(1−g)·A; fixed: g(1−g)/(V−1)·(V·A − B); Gumbel: A), with the exact fourth-moment SE (independent and Gumbel) or the key-bootstrap SE (fixed). A check is a **gross failure** when |s₀² − σ²_exact| > 6·SE + τ, with τ = 10⁻⁹·g(1−g)·A (KGW) or 10⁻⁹·A (Gumbel) the scale-aware tolerance; **structural zeros** (the fixed-size variance vanishes when V·A − B ≤ 10⁻⁹·V·A; A = 0) require s₀² ≤ τ; observed sample constancy is recorded, never used to override an SE. S3 passes iff there is no gross failure; the nominal-95% coverage of the key-bootstrap intervals (ddof-1 variance) is a diagnostic. The 6-SE threshold is heuristic (Study 1 §9).
- **S4 H-length on the observed dispersion (per-chain; the counts are the result):** for every chain (corpus cell, tokenizer, h) of the KGW family, independent colouring, γ = 0.25, **raw rule**, α = 0.01: the observed SD_K(α̂_k) is non-decreasing in L within the tolerance (SD_{L′} ≥ SD_L − 2(se_L + se_{L′}) for adjacent lengths, se the key-bootstrap SE of the SD). The pre-stated expectation is that every chain holds (the gate's H-length: the shift dispersion grows with √M while the weights stay concentrated); a failing chain refutes H-length for that cell and width under the raw rule and is reported with its ρ̄(L) and M(L) (a templated prefix, whose early positions collide more than later ones, is the mechanism the gate allows). The distinct and first rules, γ = 0.5 and the Gumbel family are reported as curves (saturation and non-monotonicity allowed; T1).

## 10. Stop and pivot rules, and what a null result means
