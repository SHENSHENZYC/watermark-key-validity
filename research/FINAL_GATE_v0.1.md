### 2.1 What it is (OBSERVED, with the file)
- README.md: "Course project for ML8502 Machine Learning Security (MBZUAI, Fall 2026)." The headline: "The false-positive promise of keyed LLM watermarks (KGW and relatives) holds over a *random* key. A deployed detector uses *one* key, and under it the false-positive rate on human text is a lottery: with Three Bricks scoring (each (context, token) pair counted once, exact binomial test), window h = 1 and the OPT tokenizer, the FPR averaged over 1000 keys stays below the promised alpha = 1e-3, but the 1-in-100 key flags 2.7–3.0x the promised rate on English text, 5.7x on code and 9.9x on Russian Wikipedia. The project explains this exactly (variance over keys of the corpus-mean z), predicts a key's FPR from public text, and tests an audit that deploys only keys that pass, together with an adaptive attack on that audit."

### 2.3 Its theory, term for term against §3.3 of the paper (OBSERVED, then INFERENCE)
OBSERVED (proposal.tex, Proposition 1; the same formula in kl/nullstats.py's docstring and `predicted_var`, and in tests/test_exact_var.py): "For $n$ texts let $A_{xu}=1$ if pair $u$ occurs in text $x$, $T_x=\sum_uA_{xu}$, $z_x=(G_x-\gamma T_x)/\sqrt{\gamma(1-\gamma)T_x}$ with $G_x$ the green count, $\bar z=\frac1n\sum_xz_x$ and $a_u=\frac1n\sum_xA_{xu}/\sqrt{T_x}$. **Proposition 1.** If each window's green list is a uniformly random set of $\gamma V$ of the $V$ tokens, independently across windows, then $\E_k\bar z=0$ and $\mathrm{Var}_k\bar z=\big(V\sum_ua_u^2-\sum_cS_c^2\big)/(V-1)$, where $S_c$ sums $a_u$ over pairs $u=(c,w)$ with window $c$; for i.i.d. Bernoulli$(\gamma)$ colours $\mathrm{Var}_k\bar z=\sum_ua_u^2$." The text continues: "A pair in a fraction $p_u$ of texts contributes about $p_u^2/T$, so a few frequent pairs carry the variance. The formula matches measurement in 203 of 210 cells (7.1 deviations expected by chance) over four orders of magnitude, for $h\in\{0,1\}$ and two hash functions." Its "Proposition 2" is a predictor of a given key's rate from that key's colours on a set $F$ of frequent pairs: $\E[\FPR(k)\,|\,g_F(k)]=\hat F(k)$ with $\hat F(k)=\E_{x\sim D}\Pr(\mathrm{Bin}(T^R_x,\gamma)\ge c_\alpha(T_x)-S^F_x(k))$, "exact by construction" when $F$ is the set of pairs shared within the evaluated corpus, and tested from public text through the correlation of a key's mean bias across sets (0.82–0.996) with the out-of-sample test of $\hat F$ named as "the first deliverable".

INFERENCE. Their $a_u$ is this paper's standardised weight $b(u) = N^{-1}\sum_i n_{iu}/\sqrt{M_i}$ (§3.4; indicators under their uniq rule, counts under their all rule), their window $c$ is the seed class $c$ of §3.3, $\sum_u a_u^2 = A$ and $\sum_c S_c^2 = B$ in those weights, and with $\gamma V$ an integer ($g = \gamma$) their two expressions are, term for term, the pair of identities of claim A2, $\mathrm{Var}_K\delta_K(w) = g(1-g)(VA - B)/(V-1)$ under the fixed-size split per seed class and $g(1-g)A$ under independent colours, applied to the colour coefficients $w_z = b/\sqrt{\gamma(1-\gamma)}$ (the mean $z$). They state it for the raw and distinct rules at $h \in \{0, 1\}$ with both colouring models and validate it in 203 of 210 cells, and the post-hoc sweep validates it at $h$ up to 8. So the identity that claim A2 lists as this project's addition ("the identity for the context-indexed tuple weights of a text law (B = Σ_c w(c)² over the seeded context classes)") is stated and validated **independently and concurrently** by this project: the pre-registration is self-dated 3 October 2026 and untimestamped by git, the repository is public since 2026-10-20; this project's derivation is in the reviewed gate `KEYCOND_GATE_v0.3.md` (committed 2026-10-09 local, 2026-10-10 UTC; the R01 review memos of 2026-10-09) and in `STUDY1_PROTOCOL_v0.3.md`, locked on 2026-10-10 before any outcome, both in this repository's git history; neither project cites or could have seen the other (this repository is private).
