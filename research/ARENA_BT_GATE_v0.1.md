# Bradley–Terry adequacy on public arena preference data (C05) — source gate v0.1

Date: 2026-10-08. **Status: FAIL (no-go as the project's question).** The core is occupied (a formal rejection of Bradley–Terry on 1.49M arena battles; a released audit tool with a public case study covering ties, cycles, clustering and position; heterogeneity, fragility, rank-uncertainty and winner's-curse papers; and misspecification-robust sandwich intervals already in the arena's own pipeline), and the residual is a thin adequacy note. Search absence is not proof of novelty.

## Candidate (one sentence, in parts)
**Decision:** an arena operator (or a reader of its leaderboard) must choose the paired-comparison model that turns crowd votes into a ranking with stated uncertainty, and decide how much of the published order the votes support. **Information set and timing:** the public vote releases (55k, 100k, 140k) with the model pair, the winner including two tie kinds, the prompt, category tags, language, timestamp and, in the 140k release, an evaluation-session id; no voter identity beyond a judge hash in the 100k release. **Target:** the adequacy of the Bradley–Terry model (with the arena's tie treatment and style covariates) as a description of the votes — lack of fit against a saturated pairwise model, the tie model (Rao–Kupper against Davidson against half-win), stochastic transitivity (triads), homogeneity across prompt category, language and session — and the propagation of each detected violation into the arena's stated rank uncertainty (Arena-Rank's closed-form intervals and rank spread). **Comparator:** Bradley–Terry as used (Arena-Rank), style-controlled Bradley–Terry, arenakit's probes (tie convention, intransitivity, voter clustering, position bias, simultaneous separation), Prompt-to-Leaderboard and pluralistic models (heterogeneity), and Tang, Wang and Jin's misspecification study. **Failure mechanism:** a misspecified model yields overconfident rank intervals, and the arenas report Bradley–Terry intervals without a lack-of-fit check.

## Early observation (2026-10-08, before full texts)
arenakit's committed audit of the 100k release already reports, on 106,134 votes over 55 models: 1 of 54 adjacent pairs separated with family-wise control (5 without); a median 95% rank interval of 7 ranks; position bias −0.012 log-odds (interval excluding zero, inside its ±0.05 immaterial band); ties 38.4% of battles with the order surviving dropping or Rao–Kupper modelling (θ 2.35); 493 of 14,164 decided triads cyclic (3.5%) against 4.3% predicted by the fit; voter-clustered resampling widening standard errors by 1.03×. Inference: the tie-model, transitivity, clustering and propagation parts of this candidate's battery are already occupied by a released tool with a public case study; the residual narrows to formal lack-of-fit tests (likelihood ratio against a saturated pairwise model; Singh and Davidov's 2026 transitivity tests) and homogeneity across category, language and session on the 140k release.

## Residual gap (type: adequacy check)
Observed: no retrieved work runs a classical specification battery on human arena votes (likelihood-ratio test against a saturated pairwise model; Rao–Kupper against Davidson against half-win ties; homogeneity across category, language and evaluation session on the 140k release) and propagates each violation into the arena's stated rank uncertainty. Inference: the battery would most likely confirm what Tang et al. (rejection), `arenakit` (cycles at the predicted rate; order unchanged under tie models; clustering widens intervals by 3%) and the arena's sandwich intervals already imply; the one unoccupied data opportunity (session-level dependence through `evaluation_session_id`) is a paragraph, not a paper.

## Is the residual gap meaningful? (who would act differently)
Arena operators already report model-robust intervals and style controls; a confirmed small misfit would not change their pipeline, and readers already have `arenakit`'s rank intervals. A statistician gains a reusable battery, but the methodological part (powerful lack-of-fit tests for binary Bradley–Terry) is named as open by Singh & Davidov and belongs to that literature. Verdict on meaning: low to moderate; not enough for the A6 standard (a single adequacy study cannot fill 4–6 figures and tables with substance).
