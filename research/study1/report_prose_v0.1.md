## 5. Unanticipated observations (labelled; not pre-registered; the conductor's reading of the generated tables)
- **R4b's locked analyzer carries a tolerance the protocol's text does not state (a text/code discrepancy of the locked protocol, disclosed, not changed).** The protocol says "within 3 text-bootstrap SEs of the analytic limit"; the pinned analyzer compares the residual with 3·SE + 10⁻⁹. At the longest length the four residuals are floating-point remainders of an exactly saturated quantity (at most [[r4b_max_residual]]) and the text-bootstrap SEs are smaller still (three times the largest is [[r4b_max_3se]]), so the literal text passes in [[r4b_literal_pass_n]] of [[r4b_tol_rows]] cells while the implemented rule passes in all (Table 8b). The tolerance was in the analyzer at the lock (commit `b994105`, before any outcome), so the verdict "reproduced" is the locked rule's and no rule was changed after the fact; the protocol's text and code should have agreed, and this report records that they did not. A future formal criterion belongs to a new protocol version.
- **The fixed-size reduction depends on the retention rule; the pre-registered "below 1% for V ≥ 10,000" statement is refuted under the distinct rule only, and the identity behind it is not.** Under the raw rule the largest reduction over [[red_raw_n]] cells with V ≥ 10,000 is [[red_raw_max]] and under the first rule [[red_first_max]] (no cell at or above 1%); under the distinct rule [[red_distinct_n_above]] of [[red_distinct_n]] cells reach 1%: [[red_distinct_h0_n_above]] of [[red_distinct_h0_n]] at h = 0 (largest [[red_distinct_h0_max]]) and [[red_distinct_h1_n_above]] of [[red_distinct_h1_n]] at h = 1 (largest [[red_distinct_h1_max]]), none at h ≥ 2 (Table 6). The gate's residual-gap item 2 defines the conditional collision C_cond = A/B for the weights actually scored and gives the reduction (1/C_cond − 1)/(V − 1) exactly, with "of order 1/(V·C_cond)" as its asymptotic form; that identity and that order expression are not refuted. A retrospective explanation of the pre-registered number (no pre-run derivation documents its premise): it is what the order expression gives when C_cond stays near the raw law's unigram collision C_0, as it does for an i.i.d. law scored raw, where A/B = C_0 at every width as a population identity (A = C_0^(h+1), B = C_0^h), not the tuple collision C_h. On the Zipf V = 10,000 law: analytic C_0 = [[z10000_C0_analytic]], realised h = 0 collision A/B = [[z10000_h0_raw_C0]] at L = 2,048, realised A/B at h = 1 = [[z10000_h1_raw_AB]], analytic C_1 = [[z10000_h1_raw_Ch]]; the order expression 1/(V·C_0) gives [[red_order_z10000_raw]]. Under the distinct rule deduplication flattens the occupancy weights over the covered vocabulary (the gate's residual-gap item 3), so 1/C_cond approaches the size of the covered support: on that law at h = 0 and L = 2,048, 1/C_cond = [[inv_ccond_z10000]] with all [[coverage_z10000_distinct_Lmax]] tokens retained in some text, and the reduction is [[red_z10000_h0_Lmax]]. The identity holds in these cells (R3; on that cell the exact ratio is [[fx_z10000_exact]] against an observed [[fx_z10000_obs]] ± [[fx_z10000_se]], and on Zipf V = 1,000 [[fx_z1000_exact]] against [[fx_z1000_obs]] ± [[fx_z1000_se]]; Table 7). Three collision quantities must stay distinct in the paper: A/B of the scored weights (which sets the reduction), the tuple collision C_h (which the raw rule's between-text collision reproduces, Table 11), and the between-text collision Ã(h) of R5. The statement stays refuted as pre-registered.

## 6. Interpretation (inference, for the claim gate; not a pre-registered result)
- **Q1, the identities and the simulator.** Both colouring models obey the exact across-key identities at every live cell (R3: [[r3_gross]] gross failures in [[r3_checks]] checks; coverage [[r3_coverage]]), and no R2 check triggered the registered audit of the simulator's key-mean level (with the rule's stated power limits). The finite-population correction of KGW's fixed-size lists is set by the conditional collision A/B of the scored weights (gate item 2): it is below 1% at V ≥ 10,000 in every cell that scores every position or the first token after each context, and material for a deduplicating detector at h ≤ 1 (up to [[red_distinct_h0_max]] at V = 10,000; the exact ratio down to [[fixed_subset_ratio_min]] at V = 1,000 on the longest texts). The paper's theory section should state it through A/B and keep A/B, C_h and Ã(h) distinct.
- **Q2, adequacy of the first-order predictor.** The common-factor predictor with σ² from the identities meets the registered criterion (R1 PASS). Descriptively, the resolved point ratios lie between [[r1_ratio_min]] and [[r1_ratio_max]] on these laws (a measured range, not an accuracy guarantee for real text); every eligible cell with h ≥ 1 ([[h1p_n]] cells) is consistent; at h = 0 two groups are not resolved: [[h0d_n_unresolved]] distinct-rule cells across every length (L = [[h0d_unres_L_span]]; point ratios [[h0d_unres_ratio_range]], intervals too wide to decide) and the four shortest raw-rule cells of the heavier laws, where the prediction exceeds the observation by a factor [[h0r_unres_overpred_range]]; in the far tail (α = 0.001) three short cells are inconsistent, with factors [[a001_overpred_range]]. The pairwise Gaussian predictor's point ratio is closer to 1 than the common-factor one's in [[pair_closer_n]] of [[pair_n]] eligible primary cells, both lie inside the band in all [[pair_n]], and on one small-vocabulary illustration cell under the fixed colouring the pairwise form is markedly better (Table 8); so the common-factor structure is adequate on the core laws and not in general. The h = 0 pattern is consistent with the Gaussian score model failing through discreteness and skew; the study does not isolate that cause from sampling variation. No theorem is claimed.
- **Q3, length (T1, H-length).** At h = 1, under the raw rule ρ̄ grows in proportion to M (×[[rho_growth_raw_range]] from L = 64 to 2,048 while M grows ×[[M_growth_raw_range]]); under the distinct rule it grows ×[[rho_growth_distinct_range]] while M grows ×[[M_growth_distinct_range]], and under the first rule ×[[rho_growth_first_range]] for M ×[[M_growth_first_range]]: the repeated-context rules replace the proportional growth with a slow drift toward a plateau set by the covered support, the saturation the support control reproduces exactly (ρ̄ = [[r4b_rho_indep_Lmax]] independent and [[r4b_rho_fixed_Lmax]] fixed at L = 1,024 against the limits [[r4b_lim_indep]] and [[r4b_lim_fixed]], from [[r4b_rho_indep_L64]] at L = 64 where M = [[r4b_M_L64]] of 50). H-length holds as stated in every raw-rule chain and, descriptively, under both other rules.
- **Q4, width (H-width).** Ã(h) falls by a factor [[A_drop_zipf_range]] per unit of context width on the Zipf laws (C_0^(h+1)) and [[A_drop_markov_range]] on the Markov chains; the level's across-key SD at L = 2,048 falls from [[h0raw_sd_range_L2048]] pp at h = 0 to [[sd_h1_raw_Lmax_range]] pp at h = 1 and [[sd_h2_raw_Lmax_range]] pp at h = 2 under the raw rule ([[sd_h1_distinct_Lmax_range]] and [[sd_h2_distinct_Lmax_range]] pp under the distinct rule), and at h ≥ 3 it is [[h3plus_sd_range_Lmax]] pp at L = 2,048, where [[h3_n_eligible_Lmax]] of the [[h3_n_candidates_Lmax]] candidate cells reach the eligibility floor (over all lengths [[h3plus_n_eligible]] of [[h3plus_n_candidates]], all at h = 3 and all consistent, SD [[h3_sd_range]] pp; §5), and no h = 4 cell does. ## 7. Limitations
- Synthetic laws only (i.i.d. Zipf, a first-order Markov chain with 50-token supports, a two-token chain, a covered support); no real text, no tokenizer, no model; no statement about real-text false-positive rates (Study 2) or about other score families (protocol §12).
- One key sample of [[K]] keys (2,000 for the small-vocabulary illustration laws), shared across every cell of the grid under the independent colouring (the same seeds; the same token ids hashed across laws), so comparisons across those cells are paired and the key-sampling error is common to all of them; under the fixed colouring the hypergeometric draws are paired across lengths and rules within a chain only (protocol §4). The across-key SD carries a relative Monte-Carlo error of about 3% on the normal benchmark and more for skewed per-key levels (protocol §8).
- The predictor's structure (one common Gaussian shift, one residual variance, conditional independence across texts) is an approximation whose adequacy was measured, not derived; its failures at h = 0 are described, not explained by a theorem.
- The target is conditional on the realised texts: the [[N]] texts per law are fixed and the finite-text term is part of it; the population diagnostic (the two-way decomposition) is noisy and reported as such.
- R3's 6-SE threshold is heuristic (protocol §9); R2 is exact but, at K = 500, cannot flag under-rejection at α = 0.01 and flags a dead detector only above a key-mean of 2.86%.
- The core fixed subset uses K = 200 keys and is reported, not decided on; its observed fixed/independent ratios carry SEs of about 10%.
- The deployer's view (Table 14, Figure 4) carries no crossed key-by-text uncertainty for the per-key quantiles or the worst sampled key; only the key-bootstrap interval of the SD, conditional on the texts, is available. A population-extreme statement would need a separately authorised analysis or a new study.
- Priority and wording: the phenomenon is attributed to the published record (gate constraints 1, 11, 13, 16); the exact identities are elementary; nothing here is a theorem.
