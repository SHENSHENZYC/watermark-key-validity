# Study 1 — design notes v0.1 (theory and simulation for the red–green family; not a protocol, not locked)

Date: 2026-10-09. Written while the Stage 4 audit ran, from the gate v0.2 §Residual gap (items 1–7, hypotheses H-length, H-width, H-domain, H-transfer, targets T1–T3) and the R01 record. The protocol (`research/STUDY1_PROTOCOL_v0.1.md`) is drafted from these notes after the data verdict; nothing here is a result.

## 1. Question and type
Adequacy check of the first-order approximation to the key-conditional level of a red–green (KGW-style) z-test, and the shape of the across-key dispersion against text length under each repeated-context rule (target T1), by exact identities and Monte Carlo simulation on synthetic text laws, with the empirical occupancy weights of the open partition as an optional extra input. No real-text false-positive rate is measured here (Study 2).

## 2. Objects
- **Detector:** green fraction over the retained scored positions of a text; z = (G − bM)/√(M b(1−b)) with centre b = γ (the published convention) and the realised fraction g = ⌊γV⌋/V recorded; reject at z > z_α for α ∈ {0.01, 0.05} (and 0.001 where M allows).
- **Colouring models:** (i) exact fixed-size per-context green lists (a uniformly random m-subset per effective seed class; KGW's seeded permutation); (ii) independent colours (green iff PRF(key, context, token) < γ; the implementation surrogate). Both are generated from the same key stream so that their difference is measurable per key.
- **Retention rules:** raw (every position), all-distinct-tuples (Three Bricks), first-occurrence-per-context (textGrain).
- **Context widths:** h ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}; **lengths:** L ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048}; **γ ∈ {0.25, 0.5}**.
- **Text laws (synthetic, fully specified):** (a) i.i.d. Zipf over V tokens with exponent s ∈ {0.8, 1.0, 1.2}, V ∈ {100, 1,000, 10,000, 50,000}; (b) first-order Markov chains with Zipfian stationary law and Dirichlet-sparse transition rows (concentration κ ∈ {0.1, 1}), V ∈ {1,000, 10,000}; (c) a two-token chain with V = 2 to reproduce the reviewer's counterexamples (sanity); (d) optionally the empirical (h+1)-tuple laws of one open corpus cell per language, as a bridge to Study 2.
- **Keys:** development seeds 0–999 (PCG64 streams); **texts:** N per cell fixed by the precision rule below; fresh texts per key or shared texts with the crossed analysis (decide in the protocol; shared texts are cheaper and match Study 2's design).

## 3. Quantities per cell
Per key k: the empirical level α̂(k) over N texts; the exact mean shift δ_k(w) for the cell's weights (raw: the tuple law p_h; repaired: the occupancy weights a(L) estimated from the simulated texts); per cell: the across-key SD of α̂ (noise-corrected), the worst sampled key with its uncertainty, the key-mean of α̂ (marginal check), the predicted SD from the first-order formula (gate item 4, with σ from the cell's scores), the exact across-key variance of the mean shift under both colourings (gate item 2) and its ratio, the collision statistics C(w) and C_cond.

## 4. Candidate pre-registered rules (to be fixed in the protocol with the statistical-power skill)
- **R1 adequacy:** over cells where the normal approximation applies (M ≥ 30 retained scores in ≥ 95% of texts; 0 < g < 1), the predicted across-key SD of the level is within a factor of 1.5 of the observed noise-corrected SD in at least 80% of cells; report the failing cells by (law, rule, h, L).
- **R2 marginal validity (simulator check):** the key-mean of α̂ lies within ±3 Monte Carlo SE of α in every cell (a failed cell invalidates the simulator, not the theory).
- **R3 finite-population correction:** the relative difference between the fixed-size and independent colourings' across-key variance of the mean shift equals the exact ratio (gate item 2) within Monte Carlo error in every cell, and is below 1% for V ≥ 10,000 under the Zipf and Markov laws (a prediction of the gate, to be confirmed or refuted).
- **R4 H-length:** under the raw rule, the across-key SD of the z-shift is non-decreasing in L in every (law, h) chain; under the repair rules it is reported as a curve with its saturation level compared with the limiting-occupancy prediction (T1); no monotonicity is claimed.
- **R5 H-width:** under the raw rule, C(p_h) and the across-key SD are non-increasing in h in every chain of the Zipf and Markov laws (projective laws); under the repair rules reported, with the two-token chain reproducing the reviewer's reversal.
- **What a null result means:** if R1 fails broadly, the paper's theory section reports the approximation's range of validity from the simulation, and Study 2's predictions use the empirically calibrated form; the question stays answerable by measurement.

## 5. Precision (nominal)
With K = 1,000 keys the across-key SD estimate has relative Monte Carlo error ≈ 1/√(2K) ≈ 2.2%; with N = 2,000 texts per key the per-key level at α = 1% has SE 0.22 pp, which the noise correction removes in expectation (observed variance − mean binomial variance under shared texts, with the crossed-design covariance); N = 2,000 and K = 500 are the cheaper fallback with 3.2% and 0.22 pp.

## 6. Implementation sketch and compute
Per cell: simulate N texts; retain tuples per rule; build the sparse text-by-tuple incidence (N × D, D distinct tuples); build the tuple-by-key colouring (D × K, uint8) from the key stream (fixed-size per context by ranking hashed values within each context's V tokens, feasible for V ≤ 50,000 by a vectorised per-context permutation; independent colours by thresholding); counts = incidence @ colouring → N × K; levels per key. Cost per cell ≈ nnz(incidence) × K ≈ N · M · K multiply-adds (6e8 at N = 2,000, M = 600, K = 500: seconds in SciPy). Cells: 2 colourings × 3 rules × 5 widths × 6 lengths × (12 Zipf + 4 Markov + 1 two-token) laws × 2 γ = 6,120 cells, many degenerate (h ≥ 1 with V = 100 saturates); budget ≈ 2–4 hours on the CPU after pruning; **timed on a representative cell at input validation before the lock** (R01 F10).

## 7. Open design questions for the protocol
Shared texts across keys (Study 2's design) against fresh texts per key (cleaner noise correction); whether to include the empirical tuple laws here or leave them to Study 2; the treatment of M_L = 0 texts (no detection; the eligible-text rate scaled by P(M > 0)); the number of α levels; whether the two-token and V = 100 laws count toward R1 (they are illustrative; propose excluding them from the 80% rule and reporting them separately).

## 8. What the protocol kept, deferred or dropped (added 2026-10-10 after R03 round 1, F10)
Kept: the detector, both colourings at the common g, the three rules, h ∈ {0..4}, L ∈ {64..2048}, γ ∈ {0.25, 0.5}, shared texts with the crossed analysis, the two-token chain, R1–R5 in revised form. **Changed:** the primary target is conditional on the realised texts and the predictor is the normal mixture of the level approximation with σ from the identities (protocol v0.2 §3); R2 is calibrated and decided at α ∈ {0.05, 0.01}; R3 is per colouring; R4 is on ρ̄(L) under the raw rule with a saturation check on a support control law; R5 is on the between-text collision Ã(h). **Deferred to Study 2 or dropped:** Zipf exponents 0.8 and V = 100 as core laws (V = 100 is an illustration law; s ∈ {1.0, 1.2} only); Dirichlet-sparse Markov transitions (replaced by fixed Zipf-weighted supports of size 50 with escape 0.1); the empirical (h+1)-tuple laws of the open corpus cells (Study 2 measures real text directly); fresh texts per key (shared texts kept; the conditional-on-texts target makes the noise correction unnecessary); K = 1,000 (K = 500 core, 2,000 for the smallest illustration laws); α = 0.001 is reported, not decided. The gate's "on the frozen corpora's empirical weights" clause of the hypotheses belongs to Study 2.
