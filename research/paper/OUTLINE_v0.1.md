## 2. One-paragraph pitch (the abstract's skeleton; the numbers enter as placeholders)

A text watermark detector is deployed with one secret key, but its false-positive guarantee is stated on average over the key draw. We study the key-conditional false-positive rate of key-randomised detectors (the red–green z-test of Kirchenbauer et al. with three repeated-context retention rules, and Gumbel-max) for a fixed null text law: how dispersed it is across keys, what governs the dispersion, and what restoring per-key validity costs. The frame is a transfer of training-conditional validity (Vovk; Bian and Barber; Liang and Barber) to a key-randomised test (constraint 2), and the phenomenon is the published record's: named open by Jovanović et al., measured by Gloaguen et al. and by Genhatharan, formalised as PAC-over-keys with a multiplier remedy by AutoMark (constraints 1, 13, 16). We give exact across-key identities for the mean shift of the score under a text law's context-indexed tuple weights and each retention rule, building on the single-context and deduplicated-count variances of Unigram's appendix and the finite-vocabulary case of Jovanović et al., and a first-order predictor of the across-key dispersion of the level; we validate both on synthetic laws and on frozen open null corpora (four sources, six languages, two tokenizers, five context widths, 1,000 keys per cell) in four pre-registered, locked studies with an independent second-model review at every checkpoint. On real text one fixed key's rate at a nominal 1% is not 1%: the registered predictor passes with a measured systematic under-prediction in one block, length and width behave as registered except in named groups of chains, and a key's level co-moves across registers and little across languages. Per-key conformal and tolerance calibration on the deployment law's null texts restore the key-mean and shrink the dispersion to the reference law's order statistic level, AutoMark's multiplier is conservative where its prerequisite holds, pooled calibration controls only the key-mean, and a threshold transferred to another law disperses about three and a half times more across keys, which a key-free predictor from the cross-set covariance identity anticipates. On one model's watermarked generations, per-key calibration costs a median of [[s4:…]] percentage points of power under the raw rule at the weak strength and about nothing under the distinct rule, the per-key costs track the per-key thresholds (W1), the across-key dispersion of power after calibration is unresolved at this design (W2, a stated limit), and the predictor anticipates the dispersion on the model's own null text (W3). Nothing here is a theorem, a guarantee or a ranking; every per-key statement is conditional on the realised texts and the frozen calibration set.
