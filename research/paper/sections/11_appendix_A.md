# Appendix A Notation and derivations

**A.1 Colourings, weights and the exact across-key law.** The detector scores tuples $u = (c, v)$, a context $c$ of $h$ tokens and a token $v$, over a vocabulary of $V$ tokens. The key $K$ colours each seed class (a context, or the class of contexts the seeding hash merges) by a uniformly random $m$-subset of green tokens, $m = \lfloor \gamma V \rfloor$, $g = m/V$, independently across classes: the *fixed-size split* of KGW's seeded permutation. Under the *independent-colour* model each tuple is green independently with probability $g$; it is the implementation surrogate of Studies 1–4 (the colour is the indicator $U(\text{key}, u) < g$ of a seeded hash) and the model Study 1 compares with the fixed-size split on its core subset. With $G_K(u) \in \{0, 1\}$ the colour and $w$ fixed coefficients over tuples (a probability weight, or the colour coefficients below), $\delta_K(w) = \sum_u w(u)(G_K(u) - g)$ has $\mathbb{E}_K \delta_K = 0$. Under the fixed-size split $\operatorname{Var} G_K(u) = g(1-g)$, two distinct tokens of one class have covariance $-g(1-g)/(V-1)$ and tokens of different classes are independent, so with $A = \sum_u w(u)^2$, $A_c = \sum_v w(c, v)^2$ and $B = \sum_c w(c)^2$,
$$\operatorname{Var}_K \delta_K(w) = g(1-g)\left[A - \frac{1}{V-1}\sum_c \bigl(w(c)^2 - A_c\bigr)\right] = \frac{g(1-g)}{V-1}\,(V A - B);$$
under independent colours $\operatorname{Var}_K \delta_K(w) = g(1-g) A$. The ratio is $(V - 1/C_{\mathrm{cond}})/(V-1)$ with $C_{\mathrm{cond}} = A/B$, and the relative reduction $(1/C_{\mathrm{cond}} - 1)/(V-1)$ is $0$ when every class puts its mass on one tuple and $1$ when every class is spread uniformly over the vocabulary. A detector centred at $\gamma$ rather than $g$ carries the offset into its level: in $z$-units the exact across-key mean of the shift is $\mu_\delta = (g - \gamma)\sum_u \hat w_z(u)$, zero when $\gamma V$ is an integer. The single-context case ($B = 1$) is Unigram's Lemma C.21, $\gamma(1-\gamma)/(V-1)\cdot(V\lVert p_t\rVert^2 - 1)$, Lemma F.2 being the general finite-population variance [@zhao2024unigram, pp. 31, 36]; the same pair for the mean $z$ under the raw and distinct rules, with a fixed-size and an independent colouring, is stated and validated by the concurrent course project [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery]; the hypergeometric green count among the alive codes of a finite vocabulary is Jovanović et al.'s Equation 9 [@jovanovic2025image]. The identities are checked at the realised weights in every cell, R3 ([[s1:r3_checks]] checks, [[s1:r3_gross]] gross failures) and S3 ([[s2:s3_checks]], [[s2:s3_gross]]), with the known-mean variance estimator $s_0^2 = K^{-1}\sum_k (\hat q_k - g)^2$ of the per-key mean green fraction against the identity $g(1-g)A$ (independent colours; its SE from the exact fourth moment of a weighted sum of independent Bernoulli variables, $\kappa_4 = g(1-g)(1 - 6g(1-g))$) or $g(1-g)(VA - B)/(V-1)$ (fixed-size split; a key-bootstrap SE), and a gross failure declared at $|s_0^2 - \sigma^2_{\mathrm{exact}}| > 6\,\mathrm{SE} + \tau$ with the scale-aware tolerance $\tau = 10^{-9} g(1-g) A$; for Gumbel-max the same check takes the per-key mean score $\bar s_k$ against $\mathbb{E}\,\bar s = 1$ with the identity $\operatorname{Var}_K \bar s_k = A$ (the scores $-\ln(1-U)$ are Exp(1), unit variance), the SE from the exponential fourth moment ($\kappa_4 = 6$; the squared terms are unbounded) and $\tau = 10^{-9} A$ (Appendix B).

**The weights.** Under the raw rule, with $n_{iu}$ the number of scored positions of text $i$ at tuple $u$ and $M_i = \sum_u n_{iu} > 0$, three normalisations of the count table are used and named apart: the *colour coefficients* $\hat w_z(u) = N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}\sum_i n_{iu}/\sqrt{M_i\gamma(1-\gamma)}$, under which the mean $z$ is $\sum_u \hat w_z(u)(G_k(u) - \gamma)$ and $\operatorname{Var}_K(\bar z) = g(1-g)\sum_u \hat w_z(u)^2$ under independent colours; the *fraction weights* $\hat w_q(u) = N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}\sum_i n_{iu}/M_i$, a probability weight with $\hat q_k = \sum_u \hat w_q(u) G_k(u)$ exactly; and the *standardised weights* $b_i(u) = n_{iu}/\sqrt{M_i}$ per text, $b(u) = N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}\sum_i b_i(u)$ per set, which multiply a colour standardised to unit variance, $(G - g)/\sqrt{g(1-g)}$, or a Gumbel-max score minus its mean, so that $\hat w_z = b/\sqrt{\gamma(1-\gamma)}$ and, at $g = \gamma$ under independent colours (or for unit-variance scores), $\operatorname{Cov}_K(z_i, z_j) = \sum_u b_i(u)\,b_j(u)$ and $\operatorname{Var}_K(z_i) = \sum_u n_{iu}^2/M_i$; A.5's cross-set covariance is written in $b$. A repeated-context rule replaces the counts by the retained set $U_L(X)$ of a text of length $L$ and gives the occupancy weights $a_u(L) = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf 1\{u \in U_L(X)\}/M_L]$ on $\{M_L > 0\}$, a text with $M_L = 0$ contributing no detection. Their collision $\sum_u a_u(L)^2$ is not monotone in $L$: a process that draws one of three equally likely prefixes $(a_i, b_i, z)$ with six distinct private tokens $a_i, b_i$ has, under the distinct rule at $h = 0$, collision $6\cdot(1/6)^2 = 1/6$ at $L = 2$ and $6\cdot(1/9)^2 + (1/3)^2 = 5/27$ at $L = 3$. The retained set reaches the vocabulary only under a coverage condition: under the distinct rule a document process every path of which eventually covers the same fixed support of $s$ tokens has limiting occupancy weights uniform on that support, $A = 1/s$ and $B = 1$ at $h = 0$, hence $\operatorname{Var}_K \delta_K \to g(1-g)(V-s)/(s(V-1))$, zero only at $s = V$ (Unigram's Remark E.2, whose $m$ counts the unique tokens, our $s$, divided by $s^2$ [@zhao2024unigram, p. 34]); under the first rule the limit is set by the limiting occupancy weights themselves (at $h = 0$ that rule keeps one token per text, so an i.i.d. two-token law $(0.9, 0.1)$ with $V = 2$ and $m = 1$ has limiting variance $4/25$ although every text covers the vocabulary). Three collision quantities stay apart: $C_{\mathrm{cond}} = A/B$ of the scored weights, which sets the fixed-size ratio; the tuple collision $C_h = \sum_u p_h(u)^2$ of the law (for an i.i.d. law $C_h = C_0^{h+1}$); and the between-text collision $\tilde A(h) = \frac{N}{N-1}\bigl[A(\hat w_q) - N^{-1}\operatorname{mean}_i A_i^q\bigr]$ with $A_i^q = \sum_u (n_{iu}/M_i)^2$, the average over pairs of texts of $\sum_u w_i(u) w_j(u)$.

**A.2 The first-order predictor.** For a cell with $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ texts and $K$ keys the model is $z_i = \delta_k + e_i$ with $\delta_k \sim \mathcal N(\mu_\delta, \bar\rho)$ common to a key's texts and residuals independent across texts with one variance $\sigma^2$, so $\alpha_k = \bar\Phi((z_\alpha - \delta_k)/\sigma)$ and $\operatorname{Var}_K(\hat\alpha_k) = \operatorname{Var}(\alpha_k) + \mathbb{E}[\alpha_k(1-\alpha_k)]/N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for one common threshold; with per-text thresholds $\tau_i$ (Gumbel-max) the finite-text term is $\mathbb{E}_D[\operatorname{mean}_i \pi_i(D)(1-\pi_i(D))]/N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with $\pi_i(D) = P(Z_i > \tau_i \mid D)$ the rejection probability of text $i$ given the common shift $D = \delta_k$ and the outer expectation over the shift, computed as $(\mathbb{E}[\alpha_k] - \operatorname{mean}_i P(Z_i > \tau_i, Z_i' > \tau_i))/N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for two texts sharing the shift and the same threshold; it vanishes at $\sigma = 0$, where $\pi_i(D)$ is an indicator, whereas the marginal probabilities $P(Z_i > \tau_i)$ would not give it, and $\mathbb{E}[\alpha_k(1-\alpha_k)]/N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with the averaged level would not vanish either. Its inputs are exact second moments at the realised weights under the cell's colouring: $\mu_\delta$ as above; $\bar\rho = \frac{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}{N_{\mathrm{eff}}-1}\bigl[\operatorname{Var}_K(\bar z) - N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}\operatorname{mean}_i \operatorname{Var}_K(z_i)\bigr]$, the average over pairs $i \ne j$ of $\operatorname{Cov}_K(z_i, z_j)$, which removes the finite-text part the realised-weight identity carries; $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{mean}_i \operatorname{Var}_K(z_i) - \bar\rho + \frac{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}{N_{\mathrm{eff}}-1}\operatorname{mean}_i(\mu_i - \mu_\delta)^2$ with $\mu_i = (g-\gamma)\sqrt{M_i/(\gamma(1-\gamma))}$. In closed form $\mathbb{E}[\alpha_k] = \bar\Phi(t)$ with $t = (z_\alpha - \mu_\delta)/\sqrt{\bar\rho + \sigma^2}$, and $\mathbb{E}[\alpha_k^2]$ is the bivariate normal orthant probability of two scores sharing the shift, at correlation $\bar\rho/(\bar\rho + \sigma^2)$, through Owen's $T$ function [@owen1956tables]; $\sigma = 0$ gives correlation $1$ and $\alpha_k = \mathbf 1\{\delta_k > z_\alpha\}$. The identities determine means and covariances only: the common shift, the common residual variance, the conditional independence and the normality are assumptions, and averaging covariances before the nonlinear tail is a further approximation. The *theory share* is $\operatorname{Var}(\alpha_k)$ over the total; a cell enters an adequacy rule at a share of at least [[grid:study2|primary|min_theory_share]] and, in Studies 1 and 2, a mean $M$ of at least [[grid:study2|primary|min_mean_M]]. Diagnostics: the unit-variance variant ($\sigma = 1$); the delta-method limit $\varphi((z_\alpha - \mu_\delta)/\sigma)\sqrt{\bar\rho}/\sigma$; the observed within-key variance against $\sigma^2$; a flag when $\sigma^2$ is materially negative or non-finite. The *pairwise Gaussian predictor* drops the common-factor and homogeneity assumptions and keeps normality: on two disjoint seeded subsamples of 300 texts it uses the exact across-key covariance matrix of the scores (the bilinear form of the cell's colouring), $\operatorname{Var}_K(\hat\alpha_k) = \operatorname{mean}_i \pi_i(1-\pi_i)/N_{\mathrm{eff}} + (1 - 1/N_{\mathrm{eff}})\operatorname{mean}_{i\ne j}[P(z_i > z_\alpha, z_j > z_\alpha) - \pi_i\pi_j]$ with $\pi_i = \bar\Phi((z_\alpha - \mu_i)/\sqrt{V_i})$, and records the covariance heterogeneity of the subsample; the *simulated-shift predictor* of Study 2 draws the shift from the realised weights with fresh ideal colourings (the heaviest tuples exactly, the remainder Gaussian, the draws rescaled to variance $\bar\rho$) and averages the common-factor model over that discrete sample, a moment-matched shape diagnostic. For Gumbel-max the same model carries each text's exact Gamma threshold $\tau_i$ in $z$-units: $\mathbb{E}[\alpha_k] = \operatorname{mean}_i \bar\Phi(a_i)$ with $a_i = (\tau_i - \mu_\delta)/\sqrt{\bar\rho + \sigma^2}$ and the second moment the mean over pairs of the orthant probability at the distinct thresholds, exact within the Gaussian predictor for every $\sigma^2 \ge 0$ (the score law itself is Exp(1); the Gaussian residual is a measured approximation), with $\operatorname{Var} s = 1$ and $\mu_\delta = 0$.

**A.3 The hypotheses as registered.** H-length: under the raw rule $\bar\rho$ grows with $M$ while the occupancy weights stay concentrated, and under the repeated-context rules it drifts toward the plateau the covered support sets; R4 tests $\bar\rho$ non-decreasing in $L$ within $2(\mathrm{se}_L + \mathrm{se}_{L'})$ for adjacent lengths of every core chain on the synthetic laws (text-bootstrap SEs as tolerances; the other rules reported without a rule), R4b the support control's $\bar\rho$ within three text-bootstrap SEs of its analytic limit, and S4 the observed $\mathrm{SD}_K(\hat\alpha_k)$ non-decreasing in $L$ within $2(\mathrm{se}_L + \mathrm{se}_{L'})$ of the key-bootstrap SEs in every real-text chain under the raw rule at $\gamma = 0.25$ and $\alpha = 0.01$. H-width: the tuple collision $C(p_h) = \sum_u p_h(u)^2$ of a projectively consistent raw tuple law is non-increasing in $h$ (a finer partition), which says nothing by itself about the empirical between-text collision or the observed SD; R5 tests $\tilde A$ non-increasing in $h$ within $2(\mathrm{se}_h + \mathrm{se}_{h+1})$ in every core chain, and S5 the observed SD non-increasing in $h$ at the longest length under the raw and distinct rules. The occupancy weights of different widths are not a projective family and $M = L - h$ changes with $h$, so the width rules are expected, not implied.

**A.4 Calibration and the comparators as thresholds on $p$.** Each method is a threshold $t$ on the published test's per-text $p$-value ($\bar\Phi(z)$ of the $z$-test centred at $\gamma$; the exact Gamma upper tail for Gumbel-max), with strict rejection $p < t$. Nominal: $t = \alpha$. AutoMark's multiplier: $t = \alpha\beta$ with $\beta$ = [[grid:study3|beta]], Proposition 1's correction of a detector sound over the key [@gloaguen2026automark]; the prerequisite holds exactly for the distinct-rule Gumbel-max test under ideal randomness, asymptotically for the distinct-rule $z$-test, and fails under the raw rule, where a repeated tuple makes a text's $p$-value a function of one colouring. Empirical multiplier (scheme level, on the calibration set $C$ of $N_C$ texts with $K_{\mathrm{fit}}$ = [[s3:K_fit]] fitting keys disjoint from the evaluation keys): $t^*_k$ is the $(\lfloor\alpha N_C\rfloor + 1)$-th smallest $p$ of fitting key $k$, the largest threshold whose realised level on $C$ is at most $\alpha$; $t_{\mathrm{emp}}$ is the smaller of $\alpha$ and the $(\lfloor\beta K_{\mathrm{fit}}\rfloor + 1)$-th smallest $t^*_k$, so that at most $\lfloor\beta K_{\mathrm{fit}}\rfloor$ fitting keys exceed $\alpha$ on $C$, and $\hat\kappa = \alpha/t_{\mathrm{emp}}$; it runs neither AutoMark's Algorithm 2 nor its Algorithm 3. Pooled: $t_{\mathrm{pool}}$ is the $(\lfloor\alpha N_C K_{\mathrm{fit}}\rfloor + 1)$-th smallest of the fitting keys' $p$-values on $C$, the largest threshold whose key-averaged level on $C$ is at most $\alpha$, not capped at $\alpha$. Per-key conformal: $t_k$ is the $j$-th smallest of key $k$'s $p$-values on the first $N_{\mathrm{cal}}$ calibration texts, $j = \lfloor\alpha(N_{\mathrm{cal}} + 1)\rfloor$ in exact rational arithmetic ($j = 0$ never rejects); exchangeability of the calibration texts and a fresh text gives the marginal bound $j/(N_{\mathrm{cal}} + 1)$ on average over the calibration draw, and i.i.d. continuous scores give the level $\mathrm{Beta}(j, N_{\mathrm{cal}} + 1 - j)$ given the calibration set [@vovk2012conditional], the reference law whose adequacy T1 measures. Per-key tolerance [@vovk2012conditional, Proposition 2b]: the $j_\delta$-th smallest with $j_\delta = \max\{j \ge 1 : P(\mathrm{Bin}(N_{\mathrm{cal}}, \alpha) \le j - 1) \le \delta\}$ and $\delta$ = [[grid:study3|delta]], so that a fixed key's population level exceeds $\alpha$ with probability at most $\delta$ over calibration draws; where no such $j$ exists the method abstains. For discrete scores both per-key methods are conservative (a tie at the order statistic is not rejected). The null-side cost of a threshold is $z(t) - z_\alpha$ with $z(t) = \bar\Phi^{-1}(t)$.

**A.5 The calibrated-threshold and transfer predictor.** On a text set $S$ key $k$ gives $z_i = D_k^S + e_i$, the residuals i.i.d. $\mathcal N(0, \sigma_S^2)$ across texts and keys. Inputs, exact at the realised weights: $\bar\rho_S$ as in A.2; $\sigma_S^2 = \operatorname{mean}_i V_i - \bar\rho_S$ with $V_i = \sum_u n_{iu}^2/M_i$; $\rho_{CE} = \sum_u b_C(u)\, b_E(u)$, the mean over cross pairs of $\operatorname{Cov}_K(z_i, z_j)$, with $b$ the set's document-averaged standardised weight $N^{-1}\sum_i n_{iu}/\sqrt{M_i}$ (A.1) over the integer tuple ids, exact under independent colours at $g = \gamma$ or for unit-variance scores, because the hash is a function of the integer ids alone. A per-key threshold with order index $j$ sits at $D_k^C + a$ with $a = \sigma_C\Phi^{-1}(U)$ and $U \sim \mathrm{Beta}(N_{\mathrm{cal}} + 1 - j, j)$, and the realised level on $E$'s law is $\ell = \bar\Phi\bigl((X + \sigma_C\Phi^{-1}(U))/\sigma_E\bigr)$ with $X = D^C - D^E \sim \mathcal N(0, v)$ and $v = \bar\rho_C + \bar\rho_E - 2\rho_{CE}$. The estimate of $v$ obeys an exact identity: since $\bar\rho_S = A_S - \sigma_S^2/n_S$ for each set with $A_S = \lVert b_S\rVert^2$, $v + \tau_v = \lVert b_C - b_E\rVert^2 \ge 0$ with $\tau_v = \sigma_C^2/N_{\mathrm{cal}} + \sigma_E^2/N_E$, so a negative estimate within that floor is noise around a non-negative quantity (set to $0$, recorded, unflagged) and a value below $-\tau_v$ is impossible and flagged, the pair ineligible (the implemented comparison is $v_{\mathrm{raw}} < -\tau_v(1 + 10^{-9}) - 10^{-300}$, a relative slack and an absolute guard with no practical effect, as the Study 3 protocol's integrity section states). Given $U$ the integral over $X$ is closed (the mean $\bar\Phi(a/s)$ with $s = \sqrt{\sigma_E^2 + v}$; the second moment through Owen's $T$ at correlation $v/(\sigma_E^2 + v)$); the $U$ integral is a [[grid:study3|beta_quadrature_nodes]]-node midpoint rule on the probability scale; the finite-evaluation term $\mathbb{E}[\ell(1-\ell)]/N_E$ is added for the conditional SD, and the theory share is the population variance over the total. Two predicted shares of keys above a level $a$ are different estimands: the population share $P(\ell > a)$, in closed form, and the finite-evaluation share $P(\mathrm{count} > \lfloor a N_E\rfloor) = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{BinomSF}(\lfloor a N_E\rfloor; N_E, \ell)]$, which a measured share over $N_E$ texts estimates; through $\mathrm{BinomSF}(m; N_E, \ell) = P(W < \ell)$ with $W \sim \mathrm{Beta}(m + 1, N_E - m)$ the latter is $\mathbb{E}_{U, W}\bigl[\Phi\bigl((\sigma_E\bar\Phi^{-1}(W) - \sigma_C\Phi^{-1}(U))/\sqrt v\bigr)\bigr]$, a smooth integrand on two probability scales integrated by a midpoint rule in each (1,000 nodes per scale for the per-key methods, 4,000 nodes of $W$ for a fixed threshold, where the integrand is $\bar\Phi((t_z - \sigma_E\bar\Phi^{-1}(W))/\sqrt{\bar\rho_E})$; at $v = 0$ the share is $\mathbb{E}_U[\mathrm{BinomSF}(m; N_E, \ell(U))]$ on the nodes of $U$, and at $\bar\rho_E = 0$ the binomial tail at the constant level; a first version with a fixed Gauss–Hermite rule missed the tail's narrow transition and was corrected before any outcome at checkpoint R07). Within one law $v$ estimates $0$ and the level is exactly $\mathrm{Beta}(j, N_{\mathrm{cal}} + 1 - j)$ when $\sigma_C = \sigma_E$. Fixed thresholds go through A.2's predictor on $E$; the scheme-level thresholds use their model-implied values $z_{\mathrm{emp}} = \max\{z_\alpha,\ \sigma_C z_\alpha + \sqrt{\bar\rho_C}\, z_\beta\}$ (the empirical threshold is capped at $\alpha$ on the $p$ scale, so its $z$ is bounded below by $z_\alpha$) and $z_{\mathrm{pool}} = z_\alpha\sqrt{\bar\rho_C + \sigma_C^2}$. T1 compares $\mathrm{SD}_{\mathrm{obs}}/\mathrm{SD}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ with the band $[2/3, 3/2]$ by its key-bootstrap interval; T2 compares the sign of $R_{\mathrm{obs}} = \mathrm{SD}(\mathrm{conformal})/\mathrm{SD}(\mathrm{nominal})$, on the same keys and texts, with the pre-stated side of $R_{\mathrm{pred}}$ for the material pairs ($R_{\mathrm{pred}}$ outside $[2/3, 3/2]$).

**A.6 Transfer controls the difference of means only.** Two null laws with identical occupancy weights, vocabulary and $M$ can give a calibrated level of $0$ on the calibration law and $1/2$ on the deployment law: with $V = 4$, $m = 2$, $h = 0$ and $M = 2$, let $P$ draw the text $(1, 2)$ or $(3, 4)$ and $P'$ one of $(1, 3)$, $(1, 4)$, $(2, 3)$, $(2, 4)$, each with equal probability, so that every token has occupancy weight $1/4$ under both; a threshold that rejects a text exactly when its green count exceeds the largest green count among $P'$'s texts (that count plus any amount in $(0, 1)$) rejects nothing on $P'$ for every key, while for the keys whose green set is $\{1, 2\}$ or $\{3, 4\}$, for which $P'$'s largest count is $1$, it rejects the $P$ text with both tokens green, half of $P$'s texts; for the other four green sets the largest count on $P'$ is $2$ and nothing is rejected on either law. The mean-shift term $v$ is therefore one component of a transferred level; the other is the score-tail discrepancy between the laws, which the studies measure and never bound.

**A.7 The power side.** For a prompt cell, an arm, a key $k$ and a rule, $\pi_k(m) = n^{-1}\sum_i \mathbf 1\{p_k(x_{ki}) < t_k^m\}$ over the $n$ = [[s4:n_tx]] generated texts of the key and $\Delta_k(m) = \pi_k(\mathrm{nominal}) - \pi_k(m)$, paired on the same texts (its sign is not empirical; its size and its distribution across keys are). W1 predicts the key-median cost by the threshold shift alone: $\hat\Delta_k = G(\alpha) - G(t_k)$ with $G$ the share of the cell's pilot $p$-values (the pilot law on validation keys, fixed per cell before the lock) strictly below the argument, and the realised key-median with its key-bootstrap interval is consistent when the interval lies inside $\hat\Delta_{\mathrm{med}} \pm c$ with $c = \max([[grid:study4|W1|tolerance_pp]]\ \mathrm{pp},\ 2 \times$ the cluster-bootstrap SE of the predicted median$)$ recorded before the lock. W2 reads the across-key dispersion of power as the two-way between-key component of the $K_4 \times n$ table of rejection indicators, $\hat v = s_K^2 - \mathrm{MS}_E/n$ (Study 1's locked estimator on the transposed table), $\mathrm{SD}_b = \sqrt{\max(\hat v, 0)}$, and $R_{\mathrm{pow}} = \mathrm{SD}_b(\pi(\mathrm{conformal}))/\mathrm{SD}_b(\pi(\mathrm{nominal}))$ with a paired key-bootstrap interval ([[grid:study4|key_bootstrap_B]] resamples of the keys, the prompts fixed); the target is the ratio of the prompt-conditioned functionals (the expectation of the raw estimator given the realised prompts), whose population counterpart over prompt samples is an extrapolation; a row is unresolved when the observed denominator is $0$ or fewer than [[grid:study4|W2|min_finite_resample_share#.0%]] of the resamples give a finite ratio. W3 feeds A.5's predictor the generated null set's own moments and the calibration prefix's and compares the across-key SD of the model-null level $\tilde\ell_k$ over the [[s3:K]] evaluation keys on $N_0$ = [[s4:N0]] unwatermarked texts per cell with its prediction, by the same band and eligibility.

**A.8 The secondary population diagnostic.** Studies 1 and 2 also report, per cell, the two-way estimate $s_K^2 - \mathrm{MS}_E/N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of the population across-key variance of the level (so that $\mathbb{E}[s_K^2] = v_K + v_E/N$) against the predictor's $\operatorname{Var}(\alpha_k)$, with a crossed key-by-text bootstrap SE (texts and keys resampled; $B$ = [[paper:d_B_crossed]]). The crossed resample centres at $(K-1)/K \cdot s_K^2$ rather than at the corrected component (the R09 F18 identity, checked on the [[paper:d_s1_rows_checked]] of [[paper:d_s1_rows_total]] stored rows of Study 1 and the [[paper:d_s2_rows_checked]] of [[paper:d_s2_rows_total]] of Study 2 that carry a positive bootstrap SE and a non-zero component, where the standardised discrepancy of the stored bootstrap mean from the identity has SD close to 1; Table D5); a key-only resample ($B$ = [[paper:d_B_key_only]]) measures instead the uncertainty of the text-conditioned functional (R09 F22), and under a multiplicative key-by-text interaction its SE is far below the population sampling SD. Table D5 gives the counts under both constructions and under normal theory; neither carries a validated population confidence statement.
