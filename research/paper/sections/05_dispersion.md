## 5.3 What one fixed key faces (Figure 1, Table D14)

On the frozen corpora at h = 1, one key's false-positive rate at a nominal 1% is not 1%. Under the raw rule the key-averaged level is [[s2:dep_raw_h1_keymean_range]], inflated by within-text repetition, and the sampled keys lie on both sides of it: across the [[paper:g_n_units#w]] (cell, tokenizer) units the 99% key is [[s2:dep_raw_h1_q99_range]], the worst sampled key [[s2:dep_raw_h1_worst_range]] and [[s2:dep_raw_h1_share2_range]] of the keys sit above 2α. Under the distinct rule the key-mean is near nominal ([[s2:dep_distinct_h1_keymean_range]]) and the per-key level still runs from a median of [[s2:dep_distinct_h1_median_range]] to a 99% key of [[s2:dep_distinct_h1_q99_range]] and a worst sampled key of [[s2:dep_distinct_h1_worst_range]]; Gumbel-max under the distinct rule disperses alike (its 99% key [[s2:depg_distinct_h1_q99_range]]), and [[s2:n_cells_any_key_above_2alpha]] of the [[s2:n_kgw_g025_cells]] KGW cells at γ = 0.25 have at least one sampled key above 2α. The same kind of measurement over 1,000 keys on five domains under a single tokenizer, with the 99% key several times above α on code and on Russian Wikipedia, is the concurrent project's [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery]. At h = 0 the raw rule gives nearly binary per-key levels, the per-key face of the known within-text repetition effect [@fernandez2023bricks; @kirchenbauer2024reliability]: on human text the 99% key is [[s2:dep_raw_h0_q99_range]] and on the synthetic laws [[s1:h0raw_share5a_range_L2048]] of the keys sit above 5α at L = 2,048. On the model's own unwatermarked generations (§7) the h = 0 levels are instead broad and right-skewed (medians [[s4:mn_news_h0_raw_nominal_q50_pct]] / [[s4:mn_wikipedia_h0_raw_nominal_q50_pct]] / [[s4:mn_stackexchange_h0_raw_nominal_q50_pct]]% in news / Wikipedia / Stack Exchange, with [[s4:mn_news_h0_raw_nominal_n_mid]] / [[s4:mn_wikipedia_h0_raw_nominal_n_mid]] / [[s4:mn_stackexchange_h0_raw_nominal_n_mid]] of the [[s3:K]] evaluation keys between 5% and 95%); whether the two shapes differ by law or by measurement is not tested; the concurrent project reports h = 0 as its extreme case under the deduplicated rule, with most keys flagging nothing on its Russian cell [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery].

## 5.4 Length and width (Figure 2)

On the synthetic laws the registered length hypothesis holds for the raw rule's shift dispersion (R4 PASS, [[s1:r4_chains]] of [[s1:r4_chains]] chains; ρ̄ grows ×[[s1:rho_growth_raw_range]] from L = 64 to 2,048 at h = 1, against ×[[s1:rho_growth_distinct_range]] under the distinct rule, the drift toward the plateau the covered support sets). On real text it is refuted as registered on the observed dispersion in [[s2:s4_failing_n]] of [[s2:s4_chains]] raw-rule chains (S4 FAIL), in [[paper:g_s2_s4_groups#w]] overlapping groups: [[s2:s4_fail_h4]] at h = 4, of which the [[s2:s4_fail_h4_nonnews_n]] outside the news register are where the predictor's decomposition puts the finite-text term first; [[s2:s4_fail_news]] news chains, where the between-text collision itself falls with length; and [[s2:s4_fail_h1_nonnews_n]] further h = 1 chains; [[s2:s4_holding]] chains hold. A plausible account of the h = 4 dip, not an identified decomposition, is the lattice of the z-test's exact size at γ = 0.25 ([[s2:exact_size_h4_L64]] at M = [[s2:M_h4_L64]], [[s2:exact_size_h4_L128]] at [[s2:M_h4_L128]], [[s2:exact_size_h4_L256]] at [[s2:M_h4_L256]]), which a reported "false-positive rate at 1%" inherits. The width hypothesis holds on both: Ã(h) is non-increasing in every synthetic chain (R5, [[s1:r5_chains]] of [[s1:r5_chains]]) and so is the observed SD at the longest length in every real chain (S5, [[s2:s5_chains]] of [[s2:s5_chains]]); at h ≥ 3 the finite-text term dominates most cells. The one prior data point of the width effect is Gloaguen et al.'s [@gloaguen2026diffusion], and the concurrent project's exploratory sweep over widths 1 to 8, labelled post hoc by its author, is a second [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery].

## 5.5 Domain, language and tokenizer (Figure 3)

A key's level co-moves across the [[paper:g_n_english_cells#w]] English cells (median correlation [[s2:corr_llama_kgw_h1_distinct_en_en_median]] at h = 1 under the distinct rule and [[s2:corr_llama_kgw_h0_raw_en_en_median]] at h = 0 under the raw, Llama-3.2) and much less between English and the other languages ([[s2:corr_llama_kgw_h1_distinct_en_other_median]] and [[s2:corr_llama_kgw_h0_raw_en_other_median]]), with higher cross-language correlations under Qwen2.5 ([[s2:corr_qwen_kgw_h1_distinct_en_other_median]]): correlations of sampled levels at one threshold, not transfer results [@nemecek2026crosslingual; @jovanovic2025image]. Across the [[paper:g_n_language_cells#w]] Wikipedia languages most keys differ by more than a factor 2 at h ≤ 1 (at h = 1, [[s2:lang_kgw_raw_h1_gt2_range]] of the keys under the raw rule and [[s2:lang_kgw_distinct_h1_gt2_range]] under the distinct; at h = 0, [[s2:lang_kgw_raw_h0_gt2_range]] and [[s2:lang_kgw_distinct_h0_gt2_range]]), an empirical disparity over the sampled keys and realised texts, never a discrimination claim; the concurrent project's Russian Wikipedia cell is the most dispersed of its five under the OPT tokenizer, and its upper-key false-positive quantile at matched length is about half as large under Qwen2.5's tokens, a quantile ratio and a tokenizer caveat its author reports, not an across-key SD [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery]. For the same texts, Qwen2.5 tokens disperse [[s2:tok_kgw_raw_h1_ratio_range]] times the Llama-3.2 value at h = 1 under the raw rule (Table D2a), the tokenizer dependence @genhatharan2026nominal reported, which the byte-level designs remove from detection [@li2026tiwm; @wang2026bytemark]. A labelled post-hoc computation accounts for the co-movement across tokenizers through the integer tuple ids the colouring hash reads: the identity-predicted correlation of the per-key green fraction from the shared ids matches the observed correlation cell by cell (Table D2b). That shared integer ids across scripts raise Qwen2.5's cross-language correlations is a hypothesis, not a measurement.
