# Appendix E Locks, records, digests and the review rounds

**E.1 The locks.** Each study's lock (`PRE_RUN_LOCK.json`) was written on the author's word after the protocol's checkpoint had ended in the reviewer's agreement and before any outcome existed: it names the study, the protocol, the grid, the validation record and the agreed resolution memo with their SHA-256 digests, every pinned code file ([[paper:e_n_pinned_total]] pinned entries over the [[paper:g_n_studies#w]] locks; Table E2), the key record, `approved_by`, `before_outcomes: true` and `final: false`; from Study 2 on it also pins the earlier studies' core modules, manifests and run manifests it depends on. The runner verifies the lock against its canonical identity before any study unit and refuses otherwise; every unit carries the lock's, the grid's, the code's and the manifests' digests and its own slot; the analyser requires the verified lock, derives the provenance every unit must carry from the lock and refuses an incomplete or padded inventory, a foreign unit, a mismatching digest or a unit that fails its record contract (Appendix B.5). A failed rule stays failed; a correction would be a new version with the old one kept.

**E.2 The records and the builders.** The run manifests record the launch commit, the lock's digest, the run's start, end and duration, the inventory digest and, for Studies 3 and 4, the locked analyser's results digest (Study 3's results file exceeds the repository host's file limit and is committed gzipped with both digests recorded, the raw file regenerable from the record by the locked analyser). Each report builder reads only the locked record and the analyser's output, refuses a results file under another lock or with a fixture marker, re-derives every descriptive row, every pass-1 summary and every registered interval under the protocol's seed convention and asserts equality with the analyser's file, and writes byte-identical outputs across builds (a fixed SVG hash salt, no timestamps); the paper's own scripts assert [[paper:r_n_table_checks]] table and [[paper:r_n_figure_checks]] figure values against those outputs with [[paper:r_n_table_checks_failed]] and [[paper:r_n_figure_checks_failed]] failures. Every result and count in the prose is a placeholder resolved at assembly from those registers, the grids, the locks and the manifests ([[paper:r_n_placeholders]] placeholders in [[paper:r_n_section_files]] section files); a syntactic literal scan then classifies the numerals and counting words its patterns recognise in the prose and inside the formulas, [[paper:r_n_literals]] in all ([[paper:r_n_literals_math]] of them in formulas): [[paper:r_n_literals_grid]] settings written beside their symbol and checked against the pinned grids, [[paper:r_n_literals_allowlisted]] constants allow-listed with a reason bound to their phrase (worked examples, other papers' own numbers, protocol tolerances and pinned code constants verified against their source text), and [[paper:r_n_literals_algebraic]] occurrences of the integers 0, 1 and 2 inside formulas; it sets aside, and counts by class, [[paper:r_n_literals_set_aside]] spans that carry a digit or a counting word but are not results (cross-references, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_reference]]; identifiers such as rule, checkpoint and claim ids, dates and model names, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_identifier]]; citation blocks, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_citation]]; the deployer's quantile labels in their labelled form, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_convention]]; exponents and subscripts, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_exponent_subscript]]; placeholders, code spans and markers, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_markup]]; notation, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_notation]]; the adequacy band [2/3, 3/2], asserted against the pinned grids, [[paper:r_n_set_aside_band]]); the assembly refuses the draft on any recognised literal outside these classes, and the scan carries a self-test of the sentences that escaped its earlier versions. The scan is a syntactic check with these documented exclusions: a numeral written in a form outside its patterns is not seen by it, and the drafts were read for such cases by the author at the section reviews and by the independent reviewer at the rounds of checkpoint R12. The data access layer is described in Appendix C.4.

**E.3 The independent review.** Under rule A7 of the project's guide, at every major checkpoint the conductor (the drafting model) wrote a prompt naming the scope, the files to read in order and the memo to write; the reviewer (Codex) wrote a memo of claims against evidence, method and statistics, process, an independent search and numbered findings with a verdict; the conductor verified every alleged error against the primary evidence before acting, fixed the facts and logged them as its own corrections, put every judgement call to the author with the reviewer's recommendation first, and answered in a response memo; the rounds continued until AGREE with no open dispute, recorded in a resolution memo that names the agreed commit.
