# 1 Introduction

A text watermark detector is deployed with one secret key. Its published false-positive guarantee holds under a null text law on average over the key draw, by method exactly, conservatively or asymptotically [@kirchenbauer2023watermark; @kuditipudi2024distortion; @li2025framework], and the deployer who must state a false-positive rate holds one draw. Our object is the key-conditional false-positive rate $\alpha_P(k)$ of §3.1, the rate the deployed key $k$ realises on the law $P$, and its distribution over the key law: how far one key's rate sits from the nominal $\alpha$, what properties of the statistic and of the text law govern that spread, and what restoring per-key validity costs in power.

**Marginal against conditional validity.** @vovk2012conditional distinguishes validity on average over training sets from validity conditional on the realised one: a method valid on average "might result in 0% training-conditional coverage even though the average coverage is" nominal [@bian2023training, p. 2]; Vovk's Propositions 2a and 2b give the PAC-type and tolerance-region statements and @liang2025algorithmic the stability route. We transfer the frame: the key is the training draw, the key and the realised texts are the "two sources of variability" of @ren2022derandomized (p. 11), and a per-key statement holds either for most keys with a stated failure probability (AutoMark's Equation 3 [@gloaguen2026automark]) or for the given key through calibration. The marginal theory of the published detectors is correct on its own terms; we add a conditional statement and refute none of it.

**The record.** That a fixed key makes the realised rate key-dependent is the published record's (§2): @jovanovic2025image named the question open for LLM watermarks and gave its finite-vocabulary case; @gloaguen2026diffusion report the earliest measurement we located; @genhatharan2026nominal, in a repository-published paper, measured KGW over ten and 128 keys, stated the mean-shift mechanism and its tokenizer dependence, counted the keys above nominal and priced per-key, per-length calibration; AutoMark [@gloaguen2026automark] formalised it as PAC over keys with a verification test and a 1/β multiplier whose cost it measures; @fernandez2026posthoc, TextSeal, textGrain, @delbianco2026compliance and the vLLM documentation state the limitation, screen keys or calibrate per cell [@sander2026textseal; @openai2026textgrain; @vllm2026watermarking]. Independently and concurrently, a course project states the same object, derives the across-key variance of the mean score under the all-token and deduplicated rules, measures the phenomenon over 1,000 keys on five domains under a single tokenizer, calibrates per key within and across domains and prices masking and key screening [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery]: its pre-registration is self-dated 2026-10-03 in a repository created on 2026-10-20, after this project's first protocol was locked on [[lock:study1|date]] and before its release; this project, private until its release, found and cited it at the final gate of 2026-10-21, and the repository's files at the cited commit do not cite this project. Within-text repetition [@fernandez2023bricks; @kirchenbauer2024reliability; @cai2025collision] and many-key multiplicity [@fu2025multiuse] are distinct, known mechanisms. By the retrieved record this paper is at least third in line on the phenomenon, a limited historical characterisation, not an exhaustive priority count.

**Contributions.** The contributions are the across-key dispersion identities for a text law's context-indexed tuple weights under each retention rule, building on the single-context and deduplicated-count variances of Unigram's appendix [@zhao2024unigram] and the finite-vocabulary case of @jovanovic2025image, where the pair for the mean score under the all-token and deduplicated rules is also stated and validated by the concurrent project [@yukhimchuk2026keylottery] and what is this paper's is the ratio through the weights actually scored, the first rule's occupancy weights, the cross-set term and the validation across widths, tokenizers and lengths, with a validated key-free first-order predictor of the false-positive level's dispersion (§3, §5); the systematic per-key measurement against it on frozen open corpora with [[s2:K]] keys per cell, across [[s2:n_corpus]] cells of [[paper:g_n_sources#w]] sources and [[paper:g_n_languages#w]] languages, [[s2:n_tok#w]] tokenizers, [[paper:g_n_rules#w]] retention rules, [[paper:g_n_widths#w]] widths and the length chains, conditional on the realised texts with crossed intervals (§5); the comparison of remedies on the same keys and texts on the null side, with the many-key transfer term across laws and tokenizers and its key-free prediction, where per-key calibration, its pricing, pooled and per-language calibration and two-key practice are the record's [@genhatharan2026nominal; @gloaguen2026automark; @aremu2026activation; @xie2025classroom; @delbianco2026compliance; @nemecek2026crosslingual; @lichen2026chainmark; @xlr8harder2026synthid] (§6); and the per-key power cost of those remedies on [[paper:g_n_models#w]] model's watermarked generations against a threshold-shift prediction (§7). The [[paper:g_n_studies#w]] studies were pre-registered and locked before any outcome existed, each after an independent second-model review; every result and count in the paper enters through a placeholder resolved from the locked records' registers, and the remaining numerals (settings, mathematical examples, other papers' own numbers) are checked against the pinned grids or listed with a reason at assembly (§8). Nothing here is a theorem, a power or transfer guarantee, a ranking of the remedies, a population extreme, a discrimination claim or a priority claim, and every per-key statement is conditional on the realised texts and, where there is one, the frozen calibration set (§8).

§2 places the work; §3 defines the object, the detectors and their rules, the identities, the predictor and the remedies as estimands; §4 describes the studies, the data and the locks; §5, §6 and §7 report the dispersion, the remedies with their transfer, and the power side; §8 draws what a deployer, a designer and an auditor take from the measurements, the limitations and reproducibility. Appendices A–E hold the derivations, the rules and verdicts, the data, the supplements, and the locks and review rounds.
