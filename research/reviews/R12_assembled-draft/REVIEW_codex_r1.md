## 6. Findings and recommendations

### F1 — Major: the conclusion promises given-key validity that the calibration result does not establish

**Evidence.** §8 says “per-key calibration on the deployment law's null texts restores the given-key level.” It also says deduplication brings the key-mean “to nominal,” whereas §5 gives 1.09%–1.18% against 1%. §6 correctly reports 45.6% of sampled keys above α after conformal calibration, and §3.6/A.4 correctly describe validity averaged over calibration draws plus a non-degenerate Beta reference distribution. “About half the keys sit above α by construction” in §6 is also stronger than the model-and-measurement statement.

**Inference.** These are not interchangeable targets. Calibration for each key does not make every frozen calibration threshold level-α, nor does the frozen shared calibration sample force a particular fraction of keys above α. The stronger conclusion undermines constraints 8, 21 and 26 despite the correct detail elsewhere.

**Recommendation.** State that calibration restores the key-mean approximately and shrinks the measured across-key dispersion to the reference-model scale, at the measured power cost. State the marginal-over-calibration/tolerance qualification whenever describing validity “for the given key.” Replace exact nominal language by near-nominal language where the measured means are not nominal. Preserve J7's two estimands.

### F2 — Major: the width and covered-support statements conflate distinct collision objects

**Evidence.** §3.6 and A.3 state that the empirical between-text collision tilde A(h) is non-increasing for a projectively consistent law. The fixed gate's residual-gap item 5 says this monotonicity for **C(p_h) of projectively consistent raw tuple laws**, explicitly excluding arbitrary normalized occupancy weights. A.3's last sentence partly acknowledges the distinction but does not repair its first claim. Also, §3.3 says the fixed-size reduction “approaches one as [weights] flatten over the covered support,” and §3.4 drops h = 0 from the support-limit formula.

**Independent check.** For two identical realized texts (1,2) drawn with positive probability from an i.i.d. two-token law, fraction weights give tilde A(0) = 1/2 and tilde A(1) = 1. The underlying law is projectively consistent. For a covered support of size s, the reduction is (s−1)/(V−1); at s = 2, V = 100 it is 1/99, not close to one.

**Recommendation.** Restore C(p_h) for the projective-law statement; keep R5/S5 as empirical registered hypotheses on their own estimands. State the reduction as a function of covered support relative to V, and restore h = 0 and the common per-document support condition to the limit. No registered verdict changes.

### F3 — Major: the three weight normalizations are not consistently named

**Evidence.** §3.3 defines w as a probability weight, §3.4 describes it as both pooled tuple mass and document-averaged z-weights, and §3.6 uses sum w_C w_E as a z-covariance without defining the changed scale. A.1 defines z-coefficients n/sqrt(M γ(1−γ)); A.5 calls n/sqrt(M) the z-weight, which is correct only when it multiplies a standardized unit-variance colour/score. The manuscript never explicitly makes that change. The Unigram comparison says “divided by m²” where local m is green-list size; Unigram's count m in that remark corresponds to this paper's retained-support size s.

**Inference.** A reader using §3's declared probability weights obtains the wrong cross-set covariance. With all M = 4 scored positions on one tuple and g = γ, the probability-weight dot product is 1, while the z-covariance is 4.

**Recommendation.** Name fraction weights w_q, unstandardized-colour coefficients w_z and standardized-score weights b separately. State the exact conversion and use b in the simple dot-product covariance for g = γ. Identify Unigram's m_U = s explicitly. Define A.5's a = σ_C Φ⁻¹(U); clarify that the empirical threshold is capped on the p scale, equivalently z_emp is bounded **below** by z_α, rather than “z_emp … capped at α.” These are paper clarifications of the existing computation.
