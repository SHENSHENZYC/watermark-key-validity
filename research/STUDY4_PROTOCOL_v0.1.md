## 1. Question and type
**An applied, paired evaluation (no new method, no theorem) of what the remedies of Study 3 cost on the power side, at the very thresholds Study 3 committed, per key, on one open-weight model's watermarked generations; and of the realised false-positive rate of those thresholds on the same model's unwatermarked generations, with Study 3's key-free predictor tested out of sample on that model-generated null law.** It answers the research question's last clause ("which remedies restore per-key validity at what power cost") by measurement.

Four questions:
- **Q1 (the cost).** For a key k with its committed thresholds t_k^m (nominal, AutoMark's multiplier 1/β, the empirical multiplier, the pooled calibration, per-key conformal and per-key tolerance at every N_cal), by how much does the realised power on the key's watermarked texts fall from the nominal threshold's, at the calibrated length? Is the cost the threshold shift alone (a key-homogeneous watermark effect; rule W1), and how is it distributed across keys?
- **Q2 (the dispersion of power).** Does per-key calibration, which removes the key's null shift, reduce the across-key dispersion of power, or does the key's own watermark strength dominate (rule W2)?
- **Q3 (the design remedy, in power terms).** The paired contrasts h = 2 against h = 1 and the distinct against the raw rule at every method, on the same prompts and keys (descriptive).
- **Q4 (the model's own null law).** A deployer who calibrated on human null text faces unwatermarked machine text too. At the committed thresholds, what is each evaluation key's realised level on the model's unwatermarked generations, and does the key-free predictor of Study 3, fed the generated null set's own second moments, anticipate the across-key dispersion of that transferred level (rule W3, Study 3's T1 out of sample)?

What this study does not do: generate with the Gumbel-max rule (its thresholds get a model-null level and no power side); vary the sampling (temperature, top-p), attack the texts (no paraphrase, no edits) or use another model; prove anything; rank the remedies on power beyond the measured settings; touch the held-out partition (`final: false`).

## 3. Target, sign, units
**Setting.** Each measurement is indexed by a prompt cell c (news/en@256, wikipedia/en@256, stackexchange/en@256), an arm (family KGW; width h ∈ {1, 2}; strength δ ∈ {0.5, 2.0}), a key k (K₄ = 100 evaluation keys), a rule (raw, distinct), a length L ∈ {64, 128, 256} (the first L generated tokens), a calibration source c′ (the ten same-tokenizer calibration units of Study 3 at the arm's h: the diagonal c′ = c, nine transfer sources), a method m, an N_cal where the method has one, and α ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.001}.

**The texts.** For (c, arm, k): n = 50 continuations of the first 50 production prompts of c (the same prompts for every key and arm), 256 new tokens each, under the arm's hook with key k. The detector sees the generated tokens alone: positions t < h are invalid and never scored; retention masks are prefix-stable, so a length L is the first L tokens.

**The per-text p-value** p_k(x) is the published KGW z-test's, through the locked code: z = (G − γM)/√(Mγ(1 − γ)) over the retained positions, p = Φ̄(z) (`s3_core.pvalues_kgw`; the green indicator from `s1_core` uniforms at g = ⌊γV⌋/V = γ).

**The thresholds** t_k^m(c′, N_cal, α) are read from the committed record by `s4_record` (index i ↔ key 23,000 + i; the array file re-hashed against the unit record and the run manifest): nominal t = α; multiplier20 t = αβ; empirical and pooled, one scalar per (c′, family, rule, α); conformal and tolerance, the key's own entry at each N_cal. **A threshold of 0 never rejects (abstention): the power is 0 and the key is counted as abstaining; no threshold is recomputed or repaired** (R07/R08 carried forward; the 960,000 structural and the 6 positive-index zeros of the record).

**Primary targets.**
- The realised power π_k(m) = n⁻¹ Σ_i 1{p_k(x_ki) < t_k^m} (strict).
- The cost Δ_k(m) = π_k(nominal) − π_k(m), paired on the same texts (monotone in the threshold by construction: its sign is not empirical, its magnitude and its distribution across keys are).
- The deployer's operating point per key: (ℓ_k(m), π_k(m)) with ℓ_k(m) the key's realised null level on the corpus cell from Study 3's evaluation unit (`eval__qwen__<c>__h<h>`: the count under the same threshold over N_E = 10,000 texts), and (ℓ̃_k(m), π_k(m)) with ℓ̃_k(m) the level on the model's unwatermarked generations of the same cell (N₀ = 5,000 texts).
- Across keys: the key-mean, the quantiles of π_k and Δ_k, and the **observed dispersion** of the per-key powers over the 50 prompts (s_K, the plain SD of the π_k; a descriptive quantity conditional on these prompts and texts, with no noise correction claimed). **The between-key dispersion of power that W2 tests (D16, D18) is a prompt-conditioned quantity:** v_K(D), the expectation over the keys and the generations of the **raw value** of Study 1's two-way estimator (before the floor and the square root) given the realised 50 prompts D of the cell: the variance across keys of the key's mean rejection probability over those 50 prompts, less 1/n of the variance of the key-by-prompt interaction within them (the two terms that one text per key and prompt cannot separate from the generation noise). Its expectation over prompt samples is the population key component v_K of the crossed two-way decomposition (keys and prompts both random; the interaction with the generation noise as the residual), and it equals v_K for every prompt sample when the interaction is exchangeable across cells; when the interaction has a structure that the prompt sample modulates (a key effect whose size depends on the prompt), v_K(D) moves with the sample (R09 F22). It is estimated from the K × 50 table of rejection indicators by Study 1's locked estimator (`s1_core.twoway_population_variance` on the transposed table; `s4_core.between_key_sd_twoway`): the raw value v̂ = s_K² − MS_E/n, unbiased for v_K(D) given the prompts and for v_K over prompt samples (E[s_K²] = v_K + v_e/n and E[MS_E] = v_e under the two-way random model, v_e the interaction-plus-noise component); the SD is its floored square root, SD_b = √max(v̂, 0), with the raw value, the residual mean square and the prompt component recorded; the floor, the square root and the ratio do not preserve the unbiasedness (R09 F24). It is **not** an estimate of the variance of the fixed 50 prompts' key means: for a fixed probability table with a key-by-prompt interaction it subtracts that interaction as well (R09 F13; the deterministic case in `VALIDATION.json` F9, where a fixed table's key-mean variance of 0.000404 gives a raw estimate of −0.0047), which is the 1/n interaction term of the functional. The independent-binomial proxy SD_b,bin² = Var_k(π̂_k) − mean_k π̂_k(1 − π̂_k)/(n − 1) is recorded as a secondary figure only (R09 F6). The ratio R_pow(m) = SD_b(π(m))/SD_b(π(nominal)) uses the two-way components. Its target is R(D) = √(max(v_K,m(D), 0) / v_K,nominal(D)), defined where the nominal functional is positive (an observed nominal component of 0 leaves the row unresolved, §9), and its uncertainty is the key bootstrap of §8 (D17): a nominal 95% interval for R(D), with its measured coverage on the validator's exact-target models reported in §11, and not an interval for the population ratio (D18; §8). The population ratio is the ratio of the square roots of the population components; it is not the expectation of R(D) over prompt samples (R09 F24).

**The model-null block.** Per cell, N₀ = 5,000 unwatermarked continuations (the 50 watermarked prompts among the 5,000). For every evaluation key (all 1,000), width h ∈ {0, 1, 2}, family (KGW, Gumbel), rule and length: ℓ̃_k = N₀⁻¹ #{p_k(x) < t} under every committed threshold of the same tokenizer (the fixed thresholds; every calibration source × method × N_cal × α), exactly Study 3's evaluation computation (`s3_core.sort_columns`, `counts_below`). Its key-free predictor inputs are Study 3's (`s3_core.set_weights`, `rho_bar`, `cross_cov`, `set_moments`): ρ̄_E and σ_E² of the generated null set at (h, rule, L), ρ̄_C and σ_C² of each calibration prefix at L = 256 (recomputed from the calibration token cache and checked against Study 3's pinned `INPUTS.json`), ρ_CE between them; the predictions by `predict_per_key` and `predict_fixed` with N_E = N₀.

**Sign conventions.** A positive cost means the method rejects less often than the nominal threshold on the same texts. "Calibration reduces the dispersion of power" means R_pow < 1, for the prompt-conditioned dispersion of §3 (the realised 50 prompts of the cell); a sentence about the cell's prompt law is an extrapolation (D18). "Consistent" for a ratio means its interval lies inside [2/3, 3/2] (Studies 1–3's band).

## 6. Primary metric and contrast
**W1 (is the cost of per-key calibration the threshold shift alone?; pass/fail).**
- **Setting:** the arm kgw_h1_d0.5, the raw rule (where the per-key shifts are large: Study 3's median conformal@5000 cost 0.60 z at h = 1), L = 256, α = 0.01, the diagonal source, conformal@5000 against nominal; one row per prompt cell (three rows).
- **Prediction (from the record and the pilot law):** Δ̂_k = G(α) − G(t_k), with t_k the key's committed threshold and G(t) the share of the cell's pilot p-values (the primary arm's pilot law on that cell at the raw rule and 256 tokens) **strictly below t**, the detector's own rule, so a pilot text whose p-value equals a lattice threshold counts as the detector counts it (`s4_core.PooledLaw`; an abstaining key has G(0) = 0, so Δ̂_k = G(α)). The prediction is a fixed, pre-lock quantity per key; it assumes that the pilot's pooled law on validation keys is the law of the power keys' texts on the same cell (a key-homogeneous watermark effect transferred within the cell).
- **Statistic:** the key-median of the realised Δ_k over the K₄ keys (contract failures excluded), with its 95% key-bootstrap interval (200 resamples; §8), against the key-median of Δ̂_k. **The target is conditional on the pre-lock prediction:** the interval is the realised median's key-sampling uncertainty given the fixed prediction; the prediction's own sampling error (the pilot's 8 key clusters × 64 shared prompts) enters through the tolerance below and is reported beside every row by the builder (§7).
- **Classification:** consistent when the interval lies inside [Δ̂_med − c, Δ̂_med + c] with the cell's tolerance c = max(5 pp, 2 × the cluster-bootstrap SE of the predicted key-median), fixed before the lock and recorded in `INPUTS.json` (§2); inconsistent when it is disjoint from that band; unresolved otherwise. c is a pre-outcome materiality allowance that covers the prediction's own sampling error at about two standard errors; it is not a calibrated error rate of a joint test.
- **Pass:** at least 2 of the 3 cells consistent and no cell inconsistent. Reported beside every row: the realised and predicted key-means, the share of keys whose realised cost lies within c of its prediction, the correlation across keys of the realised and predicted costs, the median nominal power, the pilot law's size.
- **Reading.** A pass says the threshold-shift account predicts the median cost within c on these cells; it does not establish that individual keys share a score law (the per-key agreement is reported descriptively: the share of keys within c of their prediction and the across-key correlation of realised and predicted costs). A failure says the account does not predict the median cost, and it does not identify the cause: the competing explanations are the pilot law's own sampling error beyond c, the transfer of the law from the pilot prompts to the production prompts of the same cell, the lattice of the raw statistic at the threshold, and a key-dependent watermark effect (the key's own strength); the mechanism diagnostic of §7 is reported either way, and the paper then reports the measured costs by key with their dispersion and no threshold-shift account.

**W2 (does per-key calibration change the across-key dispersion of power, and in the predicted direction?; pass/fail).**
- **Setting:** the arm kgw_h1_d0.5, L = 256, α = 0.01, the diagonal source, conformal@5000 against nominal; one row per (prompt cell, rule): six rows.
- **Statistic:** R_pow = SD_b(π(conformal@5000))/SD_b(π(nominal)) with the two-way between-key components of §3 (the K × 50 indicator tables at the nominal and the key's own threshold), **the target being the ratio of the prompt-conditioned functionals of §3 (D18)**, and its paired nominal 95% **key-bootstrap** interval (D17): 200 resamples in which the keys are resampled with replacement (the 200 × K index array drawn once), the same resampled keys for both tables (paired), the prompts fixed, the component re-estimated on every resample and the ratio's 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles taken. The key resample is the sampling the conditional target calls for (the keys and the generations random, the prompts given); the key resample's mean of the raw component is exactly (K − 1)/K times the estimate, a factor that cancels in the ratio; its coverage of the conditional ratio on exact-target paired tables at K = 100 and n = 50 is measured by the validator before the lock (§11, F9), as is its under-coverage of the population ratio under a structured interaction (§8). A crossed key-and-prompt resample is not used: its resamples centre at (K − 1)/K · s_K² rather than at the corrected component (R09 F18; recorded in §11). A resample whose denominator's estimate is 0 gives no ratio, and a row with fewer than 90% finite resamples or a zero observed denominator is unresolved (`s4_analyze.w2_decide`). - **Predicted sides (pre-stated per rule; heuristic predictions under explicit assumptions, not derivations).** Study 3's record for the three prompt cells under Qwen at h = 1, α = 0.01 (the evaluation units' nominal levels, read by `s4_record`): the across-key SD of the nominal null level is 1.94, 2.92 and 1.28 pp under the raw rule and 0.61, 0.62 and 0.44 pp under the distinct rule (news, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange), against a binomial benchmark √(ℓ̄(1 − ℓ̄)/N_E) of 0.16–0.19 pp (raw) and 0.105–0.106 pp (distinct): the distinct rule's dispersion is 4 to 6 times the benchmark, not near the floor; Study 3's median conformal@5000 threshold shift is 0.60 z under the raw rule and 0.03 z under the distinct rule (Table 11). ## 8. Uncertainty
- **Keys** are the unit of inference on the power side (K₄ = 100; one seeded sample; every comparison between methods, rules, lengths and sources is paired on the same keys and texts). **Texts:** each π_k is a share over n = 50 texts with binomial SE ≤ 0.071; the dispersion estimators are §3's, each labelled by its estimator in every table. The 1,000-key null block as in Study 3 (relative SE of an SD 2.2% on the normal benchmark).
- **The key bootstrap** (200 resamples; W1 and W3): the same resampled key indices for every quantity of a row (paired). **W2's key bootstrap** (200 resamples; D17): the keys resampled with replacement (the 200 × K index array drawn once by `s4_core.boot_indices`), the same keys for both tables of a row (paired), the prompts fixed, the two-way component re-estimated on every resample and the ratio's 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles taken (the production decision in `s4_analyze.w2_decide`). The prompts are not resampled because the target is prompt-conditioned (D18): the interval is a nominal 95% interval for the ratio R(D) of the conditional functionals of §3, under which the keys and the generations are the random elements; its measured coverage by model, conditional on an interval being available, is in §11 (F9), and no exact 95% calibration is claimed. **What the interval does not cover (R09 F22):** the population ratio over the cell's prompt law. The prompt main effects cancel exactly from s_K² and MS_E in the balanced decomposition, and an interaction that is exchangeable across cells leaves v_K(D) equal to v_K for every prompt sample; but a structured interaction (a key effect whose size depends on the prompt) makes v_K(D) move with the prompt sample, and the pairing of the two tables on the same texts cancels that movement in the ratio only when the modulation is proportional in both tables, not when the same additive interaction sits under different key main effects (the reviewer's model of R09 F22). The validator measures both coverages of the key-bootstrap interval on that model (§11, F9). With one observation per key-and-prompt cell the design cannot separate the cell interaction from the generation noise, which is why the functional carries the 1/n interaction term; it does not follow that no bound on the population uncertainty exists. The two constructions with a prompt channel that were evaluated (the keys resampled with a random half of the prompts; a two-way jackknife sum on the log ratio) are recorded in §11 on two models with their coverage of the population ratio and their widths; they were declined under D18 for their width and the rows they leave unresolved, not for invalidity (R09 F25). A crossed key-and-prompt percentile resample centres at (K − 1)/K · s_K² instead of the corrected component (R09 F18; recorded in §11). **Seeds:** `numpy.random.default_rng([int(sha256(row name)[:8], 16), 20261019])` with the row names `W1|<cell>`, `W2|<cell>|<rule>`, `W3|<cell>|h<h>|<rule>|<source>|<variant>` (`s4_core.rng_for`); a second implementation regenerates every interval.
- **The pilot law** (512 texts per arm and cell: 8 key clusters × 64 shared prompts) has its own sampling error, which the independent-text binomial figure understates: the key-and-prompt cluster bootstrap of §2 gives the SE of the predicted key-median per cell, recorded in `INPUTS.json` before the lock (R09 F5 measured 3.1–3.5 pp on the news-only law of round 1 against 1.6–2.0 pp from an independent-text bootstrap); W1's tolerance per cell is at least twice that SE. The 200-replicate bootstrap intervals have about five replicates in each tail; they are nominal.
- **Generation seeds:** per unit, (int(sha256(unit name)[:8], 16) + batch offset, 20261016); the pilot law: sha256("pilot_law|<arm>|<cell>|<key>|<offset>")[:8] xor 20261018; the cluster bootstrap: 927; the key subset: 20261016; the pilot prompts: 99261015 plus the cell's hash. MPS bfloat16 generation is not bit-reproducible, so the record is the token arrays (§4).
