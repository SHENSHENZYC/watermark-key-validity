## 5. Unanticipated observations (labelled; not pre-registered; the conductor's reading of the generated tables)
- **The common-factor predictor under-predicts the across-key SD on real text by about a fifth at γ = 0.25 and α = 0.01; the deficit is larger at α = 0.001, smaller at α = 0.05 and absent at γ = 0.5 (a measured pattern across blocks whose eligible and resolved sets differ; not a controlled contrast).** S1 passes as registered, but the resolved ratios have median [[s1_ratio_median]] and run from [[s1_ratio_min]] to [[s1_ratio_max]] (10th–90th percentiles [[s1_ratio_q10]]–[[s1_ratio_q90]] over the eligible cells; Figure 1), where Study 1's synthetic laws at its primary γ = 0.5 gave 0.79–1.20 (Study 1 report, §2, the resolved R1 ratios; the comparison changes both the text law and the primary γ, and Study 2's own γ = 0.5 block is the like-for-like reading). Across the blocks of Table 5 (each median over the resolved subset of that block's eligible cells; the eligible-set median in parentheses): at α = 0.05 the median ratio is [[sec_alpha0.05_primary_cells_ratio_median]] ([[sec_alpha0.05_primary_cells_ratio_median_eligible]]; [[sec_alpha0.05_primary_cells_share]] of [[sec_alpha0.05_primary_cells_resolved]] consistent, a smaller discrepancy, not zero); at α = 0.01, [[s1_ratio_median]] ([[s1_ratio_median_eligible]]); at α = 0.001, [[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_ratio_median]] ([[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_ratio_median_eligible]]) with [[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_share]] of [[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_resolved]] resolved cells consistent ([[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_inconsistent]] inconsistent); at γ = 0.5 the median is [[sec_gamma0.5_kgw_indep_raw_distinct_ratio_median]] ([[sec_gamma0.5_kgw_indep_raw_distinct_ratio_median_eligible]]) with [[sec_gamma0.5_kgw_indep_raw_distinct_share]] of [[sec_gamma0.5_kgw_indep_raw_distinct_resolved]] consistent (range [[sec_gamma0.5_kgw_indep_raw_distinct_ratio_range]]). The blocks' eligible and resolved sets differ, the cells share texts and the one key sample, so these are not independent replications. All [[nc_n]] non-consistent primary cells have ratios above 1 ([[nc_ratio_min]]–[[nc_ratio_max]]); [[nc_n_L64]] of them are at L = 64, [[nc_n_h1]] at h = 1, [[nc_n_raw]] under the raw rule and [[nc_n_qwen]] under Qwen2.5 (Table 4). The two alternative predictors reduce the discrepancy a little and do not remove it: on these cells the pairwise Gaussian predictor's ratios are [[nc_pw_ratio_range]] (closer to 1 than the common-factor one in [[nc_pw_closer_n]] of [[nc_n]]) and the simulated-shift predictor's [[nc_ss_ratio_range]] (closer in [[nc_ss_closer_n]], by small margins); over all eligible primary cells the medians are [[pair_ratio_median]] and [[ss_ratio_median]] against the common-factor [[s1_ratio_median_eligible]] (on the resolved subset [[pair_ratio_median_resolved]] and [[ss_ratio_median_resolved]] against [[s1_ratio_median]]), and the unit-σ predictor is worse ([[sec_unit_share]] consistent, resolved median [[sec_unit_ratio_median]]). A plausible account that this study does not isolate (protocol §3: a suggestive explanation, not an attribution): the per-text model treats the green count's z-score as Gaussian with one residual variance, and at γ = 0.25 the count is right-skewed and discrete, so the exact size of the z-test is a lattice function of M rather than α ([[exact_size_h1_L64]] at M = [[M_h1_L64]], [[exact_size_h1_L128]] at M = [[M_h1_L128]], [[exact_size_h1_L256]] at M = [[M_h1_L256]]; the `exact_marginal_if_indep_distinct` field) and the tail probability may respond to a common shift more steeply than a Gaussian tail does; the symmetric count at γ = 0.5 removes the skew but not the discreteness, so the γ = 0.5 agreement points at skew without proving it, and neither alternative predictor isolates the residual's shape from the remaining Gaussian assumptions and the estimation error. The Gumbel-max family, whose residual is an Exp(1) sum approximated by a Gaussian, shows the same sign more strongly ([[sec_gumbel_raw_distinct_share]] of [[sec_gumbel_raw_distinct_resolved]] consistent, resolved median [[sec_gumbel_raw_distinct_ratio_median]], eligible median [[sec_gumbel_raw_distinct_ratio_median_eligible]]; the first rule [[sec_gumbel_first_share]], resolved median [[sec_gumbel_first_ratio_median]]), as the protocol anticipated for a measured, not validated, approximation. ## 6. Interpretation (inference, for the claim gate; not a pre-registered result)
- **Q1, the dispersion a deployer faces.** On real null text with the published repeated-context rules, one fixed key's false-positive rate at a nominal 1% is not 1%. Under the raw rule the key-averaged null level is elevated by within-text repetition (key-mean [[dep_raw_h1_keymean_range]] at h = 1; the known mechanism of Fernandez et al. 2023 and Kirchenbauer et al. 2024) and the sampled keys lie on both sides of the nominal level ([[dep_raw_h1_keys_below_nominal_range]] of 1,000 below 1% per cell; 99% key [[dep_raw_h1_q99_range]]; nearly binary at h = 0). Under the distinct rule at h = 1 the key-mean is close to nominal ([[dep_distinct_h1_keymean_range]]) and the per-key level ranges from a median of [[dep_distinct_h1_median_range]] to a 99% key of [[dep_distinct_h1_q99_range]] and a worst sampled key of [[dep_distinct_h1_worst_range]] across registers, languages and tokenizers, with [[dep_distinct_h1_share2_range]] of the keys above twice the nominal level; at h = 2 the 99% key is [[dep_distinct_h2_q99_range]]. Gumbel-max passed the key-mean audit under the distinct rule and disperses alike. The dispersion falls with width in every chain (S5) and at h ≥ 3 the finite-text term dominates in most cells. The key-dependence of the realised rate is the published record's (Jovanović et al. 2025; Gloaguen et al. 2026; Genhatharan 2026; AutoMark, Gloaguen, Staab and Vechev 2026; gate constraints 13 and 16); what this study measures is its size per rule, width, length, register, language and tokenizer on fixed texts. "Worst key" is the worst of the [[K]] sampled keys on the [[N]] realised texts, with its crossed interval (gate constraint 12); no population extreme is claimed.
- **Q2, adequacy of the first-order theory on real text.** The registered criterion passes (S1: [[s1_share]] of [[s1_resolved]] resolved cells consistent; [[s1_inconsistent]] eligible cells inconsistent and [[s1_unresolved]] unresolved, so the pass is an aggregate statement, not a pointwise factor-1.5 guarantee) and the measured accuracy has a systematic sign: the predictor under-predicts by about a fifth at γ = 0.25 and α = 0.01 (resolved median ratio [[s1_ratio_median]], eligible median [[s1_ratio_median_eligible]]; primary ratios up to [[s1_ratio_max]]), by a resolved median factor [[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_ratio_median]] at α = 0.001 (eligible [[sec_alpha0.001_primary_cells_ratio_median_eligible]]), by a smaller factor at α = 0.05 (median [[sec_alpha0.05_primary_cells_ratio_median]], every resolved cell consistent), and by nothing at γ = 0.5 (median [[sec_gamma0.5_kgw_indep_raw_distinct_ratio_median]]), each on its own eligible and resolved sets. The paper's theory section should state the predictor's range of validity on real text in these terms (by γ, α, rule, width and tokenizer) and treat the median factors as descriptions of these cells, not as correction factors; the tested alternatives (the pairwise covariance; the simulated shift) do not remove the discrepancy, and the residual skew and discreteness of the per-text count at γ = 0.25 is a plausible explanation that a later study would have to isolate. A theorem is not claimed.
- **Q3, length and width.** H-width holds on real text in every chain (S5, [[s5_holding]] of [[s5_chains]]). H-length is refuted as registered in [[s4_failing_n]] of [[s4_chains]] raw-rule chains, in three overlapping groups ([[s4_fail_memberships]] memberships): [[s4_fail_h4]] at h = 4, of which the [[s4_fail_h4_nonnews_n]] outside news are the ones where the predictor's decomposition puts the finite-text term first and the discrete test's lattice size gives a plausible account of the 64 → 128 dip; [[s4_fail_news]] news chains at h = 1–4 ([[s4_fail_news_h4_n]] of them also at h = 4), where ρ̄ itself falls with length (the templated prefix the gate allowed); and [[s4_fail_h1_nonnews_n]] further h = 1 chains ([[s4_fail_h1_nonnews_cells]]) whose dip at L = 128 has the same shape as the news dip. The [[s4_holding]] holding chains satisfy the observed-SD tolerance; that is the registered statement and no more: in [[s4_hold_rho_nondecreasing_n]] of them ρ̄ is non-decreasing in L and in [[s4_hold_rho_decreasing_n]] it decreases somewhere while the tolerance still holds (news/en@256 Qwen2.5 at h = 2, for instance: ρ̄ [[s4_news_qwen_h2_rho_seq]], SD [[s4_news_qwen_h2_sd_seq]] pp). Any narrower statement of H-length (for instance, restricted to the regime where the shift term dominates and to registers without a templated prefix) is a post-hoc hypothesis for a later protocol, not a result of this one, and the paper should keep every observed exception visible; the lattice size of the z-test at γ = 0.25, which a reported "false-positive rate at 1%" inherits, is a property of the test worth stating on its own.
- **Q4, transfer across domains, languages and tokenizers (descriptive; the quantitative transfer theory is Study 3's).** The per-key levels of one key sample co-move across the English cells (correlations [[corr_llama_kgw_h1_distinct_en_en_median]] at h = 1 under the distinct rule, [[corr_llama_kgw_h0_raw_en_en_median]] at h = 0 raw, under Llama-3.2) and much less between English and the other languages ([[corr_llama_kgw_h1_distinct_en_other_median]] and [[corr_llama_kgw_h0_raw_en_other_median]]), with some non-English pairs correlated (ja–zh [[corr_llama_kgw_h1_distinct_ja_zh]] under Llama-3.2, [[corr_qwen_kgw_h1_distinct_ja_zh]] under Qwen2.5) and higher cross-language correlations under Qwen2.5; the disparity per key across six languages exceeds a factor 2 for most keys at h ≤ 1; across tokenizers the identity-predicted overlap of the hash inputs accounts for the co-movement of q̂ at h = 0 and h = 1, and Qwen2.5 tokens give a larger dispersion than Llama-3.2 tokens at h ≥ 1. None of this is a transfer result: a correlation of sampled levels at one threshold does not determine the error of a threshold estimated on one law and applied to another. For Study 3 these are hypotheses to test, not premises: whether per-key calibration estimated on one English register's null texts transfers to another English register, to another language or to another tokenizer, with the calibration-sample error and the evaluation on the target law measured; the baseline predictor's bias at γ = 0.25 belongs in its error budget.

## 7. Limitations
- Ten corpus cells (four sources, six languages, two lengths) and two tokenizers from the frozen open partition; no model-generated text, no watermarked text, no power side; no statement about the held-out partition (`final: false`).
- One key sample of [[K]] keys shared across every cell (the same seeds; the same token ids hashed across cells and tokenizers), so comparisons across cells are paired and the key-sampling error is common to all of them; the between-cell correlations are of this one sample.
