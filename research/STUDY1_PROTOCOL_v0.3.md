## 1. Question and type
**Adequacy check of the first-order theory, by simulation.** For the red–green (KGW-style) z-test deployed with one fixed key, the gate gives (i) **exact identities** for the across-key variance of any weighted sum of colours under the fixed-size and the independent colouring models and (ii) a **first-order approximation** to the per-key false-positive rate, α_P(k) ≈ Φ̄((z_α − δ_k)/σ), with δ_k the key's mean shift of the z-score. Study 1 asks, on fully specified synthetic text laws where every quantity is computable: Q1, do the exact identities hold in simulation (a check of the simulator and of the finite-population correction, R2–R3); Q2, is the first-order predictor of the across-key dispersion of the level adequate, and where does it fail (R1); Q3, how does the dispersion behave with text length under each repeated-context rule, including whether it saturates (H-length, T1; R4 as a rule for the raw rule, R4b for the support control, curves otherwise); Q4, how does it behave with context width (H-width; R5). No real text is used; no real-corpus false-positive rate is measured (Study 2).

## 2. Information timing

## 3. Target, sign, units
Per cell (law, width h, length L, retention rule, colouring model, γ), over the N_eff texts with M_i > 0 retained positions and K keys:
- **Primary target (conditional on the realised texts):** the per-key level α̂_k = N_eff⁻¹ Σ_i 1{z_ik > z_α}, a deterministic function of the fixed texts and the key, and its across-key SD, SD_K(α̂_k), with a key-bootstrap interval (keys are independent draws). No text-noise correction is applied: the texts are fixed, and the across-key variation that the finite texts induce is part of the target.
- **The predictor (the common-factor approximation; no outcome enters it):** its *inputs* are exact second moments from the identities at the realised weights; its *structure* is an assumption, stated here: across keys the texts' scores share one Gaussian shift δ_k ~ N(μ_δ, ρ̄) and have residuals that are independent across texts with one variance σ² (so every pairwise across-key covariance is replaced by the average ρ̄, every text's variance by the average, and the rejection indicators of distinct texts are conditionally independent given δ_k); hence α_k = Φ̄((z_α − δ_k)/σ) and **Var_K(α̂_k) = Var(α_k) + E[α_k(1 − α_k)]/N_eff**, both in closed form: E[α_k] = Φ̄(t) with t = (z_α − μ_δ)/√(ρ̄ + σ²) (the convolution), E[α_k²] = P(Z_1 > z_α, Z_2 > z_α) for two scores sharing the shift, a bivariate normal orthant probability with correlation ρ̄/(ρ̄ + σ²) evaluated through Owen's T function (σ = 0 gives correlation 1 and α_k = 1{δ_k > z_α}; ρ̄ = 0 gives correlation 0). Inputs: μ_δ = (g − γ) Σ_u ŵ_z(u) (the exact across-key mean of the z-shift; zero when γV is an integer); ρ̄ = (N_eff/(N_eff − 1))·[Var_K(z̄) − N_eff⁻¹ mean_i Var_K(z_i)] = the average over pairs i ≠ j of Cov_K(z_i, z_j), computed exactly from the identity at the realised document-averaged z-weights ŵ_z(u) = N_eff⁻¹ Σ_i n_iu/√(M_i γ(1−γ)) and at each text's own weights, under the cell's colouring (unbiased for the population mean-shift variance Var_K E_X[z | K] under i.i.d. texts; it removes the finite-text floor that the realised-weight identity carries); σ² = mean_i Var_K(z_i) − ρ̄ + (N_eff/(N_eff − 1))·mean_i(μ_i − μ_δ)² with μ_i = (g − γ)√(M_i/(γ(1−γ))) (the key-averaged ddof-1 within-key sample variance across the fixed texts, exact under the identities; the last term vanishes when γV is an integer). The identities determine means and covariances only; they do not imply the common shift, the common residual variance or the conditional independence, and the threshold indicators' covariance depends nonlinearly on each pair's covariance, so averaging covariances first is a further approximation. Diagnostics: the unit-variance variant (σ = 1); the delta-method limit φ((z_α − μ_δ)/σ)·√ρ̄/σ; the observed within-key variance σ_k² against σ²; the theory share Var(α_k)/(Var(α_k) + floor); a flag when σ² is materially negative or non-finite (the σ = 0 limit is then used and the cell is listed).
- **The pairwise Gaussian predictor (secondary; for attribution of R1 failures):** on a seeded subsample of 300 texts with M > 0, the exact across-key covariance matrix of their scores (the bilinear form of the cell's colouring: g(1−g)·Σ_u w_i(u)w_j(u) for independent colours; with the context term for the fixed-size model), the per-text means μ_i and variances V_i; under a Gaussian model with exactly that covariance, Var_K(α̂_k) = mean_i π_i(1−π_i)/N_eff + (1 − 1/N_eff)·mean_{i≠j}[P(z_i > z_α, z_j > z_α) − π_iπ_j] with π_i = Φ̄((z_α − μ_i)/√V_i) and the orthant probabilities through Owen's T function. It drops the common-factor and homogeneity assumptions (it reproduces the reviewer's two-block counterexample, where the common-factor form is off by a factor 2) and keeps normality. It is computed on two disjoint seeded subsamples of 300 texts where the cell has at least 600, and the difference between the two values is reported as its subsampling error (a single subsample estimates the full-text average of nonlinear pairwise tail covariances with error; a negative estimated variance is clipped to zero and the signed value kept). With it the **covariance heterogeneity** of the subsample is saved (the coefficient of variation of the pairwise covariances; the 50%, 90% and 99% quantiles and the maximum of the pairwise correlations), so that an inconsistent R1 cell can be *suggestively* attributed to heterogeneity (the pairwise predictor fits, the common-factor one does not), to non-normality or discreteness (neither fits), or to the finite-text term; where an attribution matters the two subsamples must agree, and a single subsample cannot uniquely attribute every disagreement.
- **Secondary population target:** Var_K(α_P(k)) estimated by s_K²(α̂) − MS_E/N_eff from the two-way rejection table (E[s_K²] = v_K + v_E/N), with a two-way bootstrap SE; compared with Var(α_k) under the mixture (descriptive).
- **Also per cell:** q̂_k (the per-key mean green fraction, exactly Σ_u ŵ_q(u) G_k(u)) and its across-key variance with the exact identity at ŵ_q under both models (R3); z̄_k and its across-key variance against the identity at ŵ_z; the M distribution (mean, min, max, quantiles); the coverage (distinct retained tuples; the share of V at h = 0); the between-text collision Ã = (N_eff/(N_eff − 1))·[A(ŵ_q) − N_eff⁻¹ mean_i A_i^q] with the i.i.d. laws' analytic C_h = C_0^(h+1); the key quantiles (1, 5, 50, 95, 99%), the worst and best sampled keys, the shares of keys above 2α and 5α; the exact binomial key-mean level under independent colours and distinct tuples; the structural-zero flag.
Units: keys (K = 500 for the core laws under the independent colouring; 200 for the fixed colouring on the core subset, a reported comparison; 2,000 for the illustration laws with V ∈ {2, 10}; 500 for V = 100 and for the support control) and texts (N = 4,000 per law, shared across keys and across widths, truncated for each L). Sign convention: "larger dispersion" means a larger SD_K(α̂_k); the predictor is "adequate" in a cell when the observed-to-predicted ratio lies in [2/3, 3/2].

## 8. Uncertainty
Keys are independent draws given the texts: every across-key statistic (SD_K(α̂_k), Var_K(q̂_k), Var_K(z̄_k)) carries a key-bootstrap interval (200 resamples of the K keys). The figure 1/√(2K) for the relative error of an SD (3.2% at K = 500, 1.6% at 2,000, 5% at 200) is a normal-theory benchmark; per-key levels are bounded and skewed (a Bernoulli level with p ≈ 0.008, as in the covered support control, has a relative SD error of about 25% at K = 500), the bootstrap reflects part of that, and a sample with no rejecting key cannot recover the unseen rare keys by resampling itself, which is why the rare-key regime is handled by exact bounds in R2 and by exclusion from R1 (illustration laws). The R1 ratio's interval is the key bootstrap of the numerator; the predictor is fixed given the texts. Text sampling enters ρ̄, Ã and the identities at the realised weights: a text bootstrap (30 resamples of the texts, the weights and identities recomputed) gives their SEs, used as the tolerances of R4 and R5 (two SEs of each adjacent member). The two-way population estimate's SE is from a two-way bootstrap (100 resamples of texts and keys; the percentile interval is biased for a variance component, so a normal interval and the bootstrap mean are reported). R2's decision uses no estimated SE: given the texts the per-key levels are i.i.d. in [0, 1] with a known mean under the null, so Hoeffding's KL bound applies exactly (§9); the normal SE SD_K(α̂_k)/√K is a diagnostic. R3's SE is the exact fourth-moment SE of the known-mean variance estimator (independent colouring; a mean of K i.i.d. bounded terms) or a key-bootstrap SE (fixed colouring), or exactly 0 when the squared estimator is constant over the keys; the validation records the distribution of its standardised deviations over 400 disjoint 500-key subsamples, which establishes its central calibration in that setting and nothing about the extreme tail. R4's and R5's tolerances are sums of SEs for adjacent members and give no simultaneous guarantee; they are descriptive hypothesis checks. Seeds: text_seed_base 20261010, validation_seed_base 99261010, bootstrap streams per cell from sha256 of the cell name with 20261011, colouring hash splitmix64 with the key mixed in, hypergeometric draws seeded by (key, 20261010); all fixed in `grid_v0.3.json` and `s1_core.py`.

## 9. Pre-registered rules and materiality thresholds
- **R1 adequacy (pass / fail):** as in §6.
- **R2 simulator validity (pass / fail; a failure is an audit flag):** under the independent colouring and the distinct rule, for every live non-degenerate cell and every α ∈ {0.05, 0.01, 0.001}, the key-mean level ā = mean_k α̂_k against its exact value given the texts, μ_a = mean_i P(Bin(M_i, g) > γM_i + z_α√(M_iγ(1−γ))). Given the texts the α̂_k are i.i.d. in [0, 1] with mean μ_a under the null, so by Hoeffding (1963, Theorem 1) P(ā ≥ μ_a + t) ≤ exp(−K·KL(μ_a + t ‖ μ_a)) and the mirror bound below; since the side is chosen after seeing the deviation, the check uses the **two-sided** bound min{1, 2·exp(−K·KL(ā ‖ μ_a))} and is **flagged** when it is below 10⁻⁶ (μ_a = 0: flagged iff ā > 0). R2 passes iff no check is flagged. Calibration: the per-check false-audit probability is at most 10⁻⁶ for any K, any per-key distribution and any dependence between cells, so the experiment-level false-audit probability is at most the number of checks times 10⁻⁶ (at most 909 checks, hence at most 9.1·10⁻⁴) by the union bound; in the covered-support regime, where the per-key level is Bernoulli, the exact probability is computed by enumeration in the validation, as is the reviewer's round-3 counterexample (a Bernoulli mean chosen to make the lower tail tight). Power (the bound's price): at K = 500 and μ_a = 0.01 the rule flags ā ≥ 0.0422 (a per-cell factor of 4 of over-rejection) and cannot flag any under-rejection, since even ā = 0 has two-sided bound 2(1 − μ_a)^K = 0.013; at μ_a = 0.05 both sides are flagged (ā ≥ 0.1104 or ā ≤ 0.0076 at K = 500), and at K = 2,000 the thresholds tighten (ā ≥ 0.0242 or ā ≤ 0.0008 at μ_a = 0.01; the table is in `VALIDATION.json`). A dead detector (key-mean 0) is flagged iff 2(1 − μ_a)^K < 10⁻⁶, that is μ_a > 0.0286 at K = 500 and μ_a > 0.00723 at K = 2,000: in every registered cell at α = 0.05 (μ_a ≈ 0.05 wherever the normal approximation holds; the exact μ_a is computed per cell), at α = 0.01 only in the K = 2,000 illustration cells, and never in a structural-zero cell; an over-eager detector is flagged at every α (round 4, F21); a threshold table is not a power curve under every alternative; subtler faults are the business of R3 (the identity check on the colours' second moments, where the round-1 γ-versus-g fault showed) and of the reported normal diagnostic (the dev/SE quantiles across checks). Structural zeros (exact 0 and observed 0) are counted separately. ## 10. Stop and pivot rules, and what a null result means
If the validation projects more than the 6-hour budget, the grid is reduced in the pre-registered order (K 500 → 300; N 4,000 → 2,000; drop γ = 0.25; drop L = 2,048) before the lock, never after it; a reduction changes the precision (K = 300: 4.1% relative error of an SD; N = 2,000: the finite-text term doubles, so fewer cells reach the theory share of 0.75) and the eligibility counts are recomputed before the lock. If R2 raises the audit flag, the run stops and the implementation is audited in a new version (the failed version is kept). If R1 fails, the paper's theory section reports the predictor's range of validity from the inconsistent cells (by σ_δ, M, rule and law), and Study 2 uses the empirically calibrated form; the research question stays answerable by measurement. R4 or R5 failing refutes the corresponding hypothesis for the simulated laws, which is itself a result (the gate states them as hypotheses). There is no pivot of the project on Study 1's outcome.
