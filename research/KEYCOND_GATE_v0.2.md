## Candidate (one sentence, in parts)
**Decision:** a deployer of a key-randomised detection test (a token-level LLM watermark such as KGW, Gumbel-max or inverse-transform; an activation-level watermark; a model fingerprint or a copyright trap) must set a detection threshold and state a false-positive rate for the **one secret key they hold**. **Information set and timing:** the detector sees the text (or the suspect model's outputs) and the key, never the generating distribution; at design time it may also hold a corpus of null (human) texts. **Target:** the key-conditional false-positive rate α_P(k) = P_{X∼P, R}(reject(X; k, R)) for a null text law P, the deployed key k and any fresh test randomisation R (for a calibrated detector, α_P(k, D_cal) with the frozen calibration set in the conditioning), and the distribution of α_P(K) over the key law for fixed P (its dispersion and its tail over sampled keys), against the nominal level α, which each published method guarantees only on average over the key draw (exactly, conservatively or asymptotically, by method). **Comparator:** the marginal tests as published (the KGW z-score, asymptotic; Kuditipudi et al.'s discrete permutation p-value over fresh keys, conservative and text-conditional; the pivotal statistics and optimal rules of Li, Ruan, Wang, Long and Su, exact under i.i.d. pseudo-randomness; e-process detection under its filtration), each with its own repeated-context rule (unique (h+1)-tuples in Three Bricks; the first occurrence of each context in textGrain). **Failure mechanism:** marginal validity over the key draw is not conditional validity for a fixed key. A null text law with concentrated tuple frequencies makes some keys' green lists or pseudorandom scores systematically favoured, so the per-key false-positive rate sits above α for some keys and below for others; the dispersion is expected to shrink with the effective number of distinct key-context draws a text exposes (context width, vocabulary concentration, repetition), a hypothesis stated with its conditions below. Existing methods state the marginal guarantee; the per-key rate has been measured once in an appendix, screened in practice, calibrated per cell and named as needing empirical checks, but has no predictive account on real text laws and no remedy with a stated per-key guarantee and a measured cost.

## Residual gap (type: adequacy check + transfer + small method; theory as exact identities for the mean shift plus a first-order approximation to be validated)
Observed: no retrieved work (i) takes the key-conditional false-positive rate α_P(k) of a deployed detector as its object beyond one appendix measurement (Gloaguen et al. 2026: one statistic family, one corpus, one length, 100 keys), (ii) gives a predictive account of its dispersion across keys from corpus statistics beyond the hypergeometric special case over dead codes (Jovanović et al. 2025), (iii) measures that dispersion as a function of text length, context width, statistic, corpus and language, or (iv) evaluates remedies (per-key calibration and its transfer across text laws; stratified splits; context width; key ensembles) for their per-key guarantee and measured power cost.

Inference (the conductor's derivation, revised at R01 rounds 1 and 2; a proposal to be pre-registered and validated in Study 1, not a result). Notation: a text has raw length L; the scored units are (h+1)-tuples u = (c, v) with context c of h tokens and token v; V ≥ 2 tokens; the key K draws, for each effective seed class (a context c, or the class of contexts the seeding hash merges), a uniformly random green set of exactly m = ⌊γV⌋ tokens, independently across classes (KGW's seeded permutation; one global set at h = 0, as in Unigram); g = m/V is the realised green fraction (g = γ when γV is an integer); G_K(u) ∈ {0, 1} is the colour. These are ideal random-set identities: a seeded PRNG supplies computational, not statistical, independence, which the protocol states as an assumption and checks empirically. Let w be a probability weight over tuples (which one: item 3); A = Σ_u w(u)², B = Σ_c w(c)² with w(c) = Σ_v w(c, v).
1. **Mean shift.** δ_K(w) = Σ_u w(u)(G_K(u) − g) has E_K δ_K = 0 (centred at g; a detector centred at the requested γ carries the deterministic offset g − γ into its level). No independence within texts is needed.
2. **Across-key variance, exact for the fixed-size split:** Var_K δ_K(w) = g(1−g)/(V−1) · (V·A − B), because Var G = g(1−g) and two distinct tokens of one class have Cov = −g(1−g)/(V−1). The independent-colour value I = g(1−g)·A (v0.1's formula, with g for γ) overstates it by an exact factor: Var/I = (V − 1/C_cond)/(V − 1) with C_cond = A/B (the within-class collision probability averaged with squared class-mass weights); the relative reduction (I − Var)/I = (1/C_cond − 1)/(V − 1) is zero at C_cond = 1, one at C_cond = 1/V, and of order 1/(V·C_cond) when V ≫ 1 and 1/C_cond ≫ 1. It is computed for the weights actually scored (item 3), not for raw next-token laws. Boundaries: m ∈ {0, V} gives no colour variance and no z-test; V = 1 is excluded. (The reviewer's check: V = 4, m = 2 with class weights (1/2, 1/6, 0, 0) and (1/12, 1/12, 1/12, 1/12) gives 1/18 by the identity and by enumeration, reduction 3/11; re-derived by the conductor.)
3. **Which weights.** Raw counting with a stated convention (pooled positions, or document-averaged) gives w = p_h, the tuple law. A repeated-context rule changes the weights to **occupancy weights**: for the retention set U_L(X) of a document (all distinct tuples under Three Bricks' rule; the first token after each distinct context under textGrain's rule, a different detector) and M_L = |U_L|, a_u(L) = E_X[1{u ∈ U_L(X)}/M_L] on {M_L > 0}, with the M_L = 0 case (no detection) stated. The collision C(a(L)) = Σ_u a_u(L)² is **not** monotone in L in general (three equally likely prefixes (a_i, b_i, z) with six distinct private tokens give 1/6 at L = 2 and 5/27 at L = 3), and the retained set tends to the vocabulary only under a coverage condition. Under the **all-distinct-token rule** at h = 0, a document process every path of which eventually covers the same fixed support of s ≤ V tokens has limiting occupancy weights uniform on that support and Var_K δ_K → g(1−g)(V − s)/(s(V − 1)), zero only at s = V; under the **context-only rule** the limit is set by the limiting occupancy weights themselves (at h = 0 that rule keeps one token per document, so an i.i.d. two-token process with law (0.9, 0.1), V = 2, m = 1 has limiting variance 4/25 although it covers the vocabulary; the reviewer's example, re-derived), and heterogeneous per-document supports are averaged through their actual occupancy weights, never replaced by the size of their union. All statements condition on M_L > 0; a zero-score document contributes no detection, and the eligible-document rate is multiplied by P(M_L > 0) in the unconditional rate. The across-key dispersion against length under each retention rule is therefore a Study 1 object with explicit support, coverage and retention assumptions (R01 round 3, F20).
4. **Per-key level.** For the z-statistic with centre b and denominator variance v0 = b(1−b) on a fixed scored count M, under a fixed key with score mean q_k and long-run variance σ_k² per score (= q_k(1−q_k) for independent scores; serial dependence can raise or lower it): α_P(k | M) ≈ Φ̄((z_α√v0 − √M(q_k − b))/σ_k) wherever a conditional central limit approximation holds (0 < b < 1, M not small, α not extreme; retained scores need not be stationary, and the exact covariance of their sum is the general object). With M random, the document-level rate is a mixture over M (condition on M, then average, or justify a joint approximation). Linearising at q_k = b with a common σ: SD_K α_P(K) ≈ φ(z_α√v0/σ) · (√M/σ) · SD_K(q_K − b), with SD_K(q_K − b) from item 2; key-varying σ_k adds terms. A **local heuristic**: α_P(K) is bounded in [0, 1], its variance is at most α(1−α) when its key-mean equals α, and P_K(α_P(K) > t) ≤ α/t under a conservative key-marginal guarantee (equality to α is not assumed for every comparator).
5. **Hypotheses, not laws** (each tested in Study 1 by simulation on Zipf and Markov tuple laws and on the frozen corpora's empirical weights, then in Study 2 on real text): **H-length**, under each retention rule the z-shift dispersion grows with √M while the occupancy weights stay concentrated and saturates at a level set by the limiting support (not necessarily zero); **H-width**, for projectively consistent raw tuple laws C(p_{h+1}) ≤ C(p_h) (a finer partition), while the normalised occupancy weights of different widths need not form such a family and the level also depends on M, σ_k and the rule, so a fall of the across-key SD with h is expected but not implied (Gloaguen et al.'s 1.2% → 0.3% → 0.1% is the one data point; a two-token Markov chain reverses the ordering when V is tiny); **H-domain**, w and hence α_P(k) depend on the text law, so one key has different levels on different subdomains and languages; **H-transfer**, a threshold calibrated on P′ and deployed on P carries a mean-shift difference δ_k(w_P) − δ_k(w_P′) whose exact across-key variance is g(1−g)/(V−1)·[V Σ_u d(u)² − Σ_c d(c)²] with d = w_P − w_P′ (independent-colour value g(1−g)Σ_u d(u)²), **which controls the difference of means only**: two null laws with identical occupancy weights, V and M can give a calibrated false-positive rate of 0 on the calibration law and 1/2 on the deployment law (the reviewer's example: V = 4, m = 2, h = 0, M = 2; P draws (1,2) or (3,4), P′ draws one of (1,3), (1,4), (2,3), (2,4); re-derived by the conductor), so any statement about the transfer of a calibrated level needs the score-tail discrepancy Δ_k = sup_t |P(S_k > t) − P′(S_k > t)| (α_P(k, D_cal) ≤ α_P′(k, D_cal) + Δ_k) or a location-family approximation with controlled tails, with the mean-shift term as one component.
6. **Other statistics.** For a keyed score s_K(u) (Gumbel-max, inverse-transform, a tournament, an activation statistic) the mean and covariance algebra carries over under finite second moments with the score's own retention rule and seed dependence; it gives neither a shared level law nor a common calibration guarantee.
7. **Remedies and what can be claimed.** Per-key calibration on N null documents: the sample size follows tolerance-region and conformal theory (Vovk 2012; Delbianco et al. 2026: the 1/(N+1) floor; the realised rate of a frozen calibration set has SD of order √(α(1−α)/N)); for i.i.d. continuous scores and rejection strictly above the calibration maximum, P_{D_cal}(α_P(k, D_cal) > α) = (1−α)^N (an upper bound for discrete scores; N = 299 gives 0.0495 at α = 1%), a conservative rule that can cost substantial power; "N ≫ 1/C_h" is withdrawn: the distribution-free sample-size bound needs no tuple-collision statistic, while the threshold itself, its power and its conservatism can depend on the null text law even without transfer, and transfer across laws is governed by item 5 with its stated limits. Stratified splits: a defined decomposition of Var_K over tuple-frequency strata with feasible integer quotas (the generalisation of Jovanović et al.'s alive/dead split), with its covariance computed. Context width trades concentration against edit robustness (Kirchenbauer et al. 2024). Key ensembles divide the mean-shift variance by the ensemble size but need their own conditional guarantee and power analysis (which keys generate, which detect). Training-conditional validity (Vovk; Bian & Barber) and the stability route (Liang & Barber) give the frame; Ren & Barber's two sources of variability the vocabulary.

Targets for Study 1 (a **theorem** is claimed only if T1 or T2 is proved; T3 is an empirical adequacy criterion and cannot license a theorem): **T1**, the occupancy-weighted across-key dispersion under a stated retention rule as a function of length, with its limiting level under a stated support condition (non-zero saturation and non-monotone curves allowed); **T2**, a per-key transfer statement for calibrated thresholds across text laws in terms of a score-tail discrepancy (or a location-family approximation with stated tail control), with the mean-shift identity as one component; **T3**, a regime-specific first-order predictor of the across-key SD of α_P(K) from corpus statistics (C(w), M, V, σ), judged by predicted against observed dispersion across cells.

## Is the residual gap meaningful? (who would act differently)
- A deployer holds one key: they would calibrate the threshold on their own null texts per key, per language and domain, with a stated sample size, instead of trusting the marginal level; today's practice (SynthID's empirical quantile; TextSeal's screening) does this ad hoc.
- A scheme designer would choose statistics and key constructions whose key-conditional level concentrates (larger context width, stratified splits, multi-key averaging) and would report per-key dispersion beside the marginal rate, as Gloaguen et al. began to.
- An auditor or standard-setter would ask for key-conditional (and subdomain-conditional) false-positive statements; NIST AI 100-4 already asks which "edge cases that are not captured by average metrics may be important" (p.40), and the subdomain effect is a fairness question (a fixed key accusing one language's writers more often).
- A methodologist gets a clean instance of training-conditional validity outside predictive inference, with exact identities for the across-key mean shift and an empirical test of a first-order approximation to the level.
Whichever way the measurements come out (dispersion negligible at deployed context widths and lengths, or material), the result changes what a deployer should report. Verdict on meaning: yes.
