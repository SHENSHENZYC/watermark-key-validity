## Research question (TOPIC LOCK, 2026-10-08; gate `KEYCOND_GATE_v0.3.md`, PASS re-narrowed after the R03 review on 2026-10-09 (decision D5), v0.1 and v0.2 kept as the record; the revisions below are labelled)
**Question (one sentence; revised at R01 round 2).** For detection tests whose false-positive guarantee holds on average over a secret random key (exactly, conservatively or asymptotically, by method: token-level LLM watermarks such as KGW, Gumbel-max and inverse-transform; activation-level watermarks; model fingerprints and copyright traps as motivation) but which are deployed with one fixed key, how dispersed is the **key-conditional false-positive rate** across keys for a given key-independent null text law, what properties of the statistic and of the text law govern that dispersion (text length under each retention rule, context width, tuple concentration, subdomain and language), and which remedies (per-key calibration with its transfer across text laws, stratified key splits, context width, key ensembles) restore per-key validity at what power cost?

**Decision:** a deployer sets the threshold and states a false-positive rate for the one key they hold. **Information set:** the detector sees the text (or the suspect model's outputs) and the key, never the generating distribution; at design time it may hold null (unwatermarked, key-independent) texts. **Target (revised at R01):** the key-conditional false-positive rate α_P(k) = P_{X∼P, R}(reject(X; k, R)) for a null text law P, the deployed key k and any fresh test randomisation R (for a calibrated detector, α_P(k, D_cal) with the frozen calibration set in the conditioning), and the distribution of α_P(K) over the key law for fixed P (its dispersion and its tail over sampled keys), against the nominal α, which each published method guarantees only on average over the key draw. Per-key statements are made either for most keys with a stated failure probability (PAC over keys) or for the given key through calibration, and the two are not confused. **Comparator:** the marginal tests as published, each with its own guarantee named (the KGW z-test, asymptotic under an independence idealisation; Kuditipudi et al.'s discrete key-resampling p-value, conservative and text-conditional; the pivotal statistics and optimal rules of Li, Ruan, Wang, Long and Su, exact under i.i.d. pseudo-randomness; e-process detection under its filtration), each with its own repeated-tuple repair rule stated. **Failure mechanism:** marginal validity over the key draw is not conditional validity for the deployed key.

**Type and positioning (revised at R01):** an **adequacy check** plus a **transfer** plus a **small method**: is the marginal guarantee adequate per key on real text, measured systematically across statistics, lengths, context widths, corpora and languages; training-conditional validity (Vovk 2012; Bian & Barber 2023; Liang & Barber 2025) and tolerance-region calibration transferred to key-randomised detection tests; per-key calibration with a transfer-error term across text laws, and a first-order predictive formula for the across-key dispersion validated by simulation and data (exact identities for the fixed-size split and the repair's occupancy weights; a theorem is claimed only if the gate's target T1 or T2 is proved; T3 is an empirical adequacy criterion). At least third in line on the phenomenon by the retrieved record (a limited historical characterisation, not an exhaustive priority count), and the paper says so: Jovanović et al. 2025 named it open and gave the finite-vocabulary special case; Gloaguen et al. ICLR 2026 measured it once; Genhatharan 2026 measured it for KGW over 10 + 128 keys with the mean-shift mechanism, its √T growth, the tokenizer dependence and priced per-key calibration; AutoMark 2026 formalised per-key validity as a PAC-over-keys requirement with a verification test and a multiplier remedy; Fernandez et al. 2026, textGrain 2026 and Delbianco et al. 2026 state the limitation and practise screening or per-cell calibration. **What this project adds (D5, 2026-10-09):** the dispersion theory (exact identities, occupancy under the repeated-context rules, dependence on context width and on the text law's collision structure, the transfer term across laws) and its validation (Study 1); the systematic measurement across statistics, widths, rules, tokenizers, registers and languages with hundreds of keys against that theory (Study 2); the comparison of remedies, including AutoMark's multiplier and per-key calibration, on validity and power with the transfer across laws (Studies 3–4); sixteen wording constraints in the gate.

**Study plan (A6, to be pre-registered one study at a time; narrowed at R01 as a proposal for Stage 5, each protocol still Yichen's lock):** (1) theory and simulation for the red–green (KGW) family: the exact across-key variance for fixed-size and independent colourings, the occupancy weights under the repeated-tuple repair, the first-order level approximation and its hypotheses (length, context width, domain, transfer), on synthetic tuple laws (Zipf, Markov) and on the frozen corpora's empirical tuple laws; (2) measurement on real null corpora: KGW widths h = 0…4 under two tokenizers across registers, languages and lengths, with Gumbel-max as the second score family; predicted against observed dispersion; a crossed key-by-document uncertainty analysis; (3) remedies: per-key calibration (tolerance and conformal) with its transfer error across corpora and languages as the main remedy, and one alternative (context width or a stratified split); key ensembles optional; (4) a paired power-side check on one model (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Apache-2.0) so the remedies' costs are measured, not assumed. A SynthID-style tournament, the previous project's activation statistic, fingerprints and copyright traps are motivation or optional extensions, each timed and licensed before any protocol names it. The null-data grid's compute is timed with the actual implementation at Stage 5 validation before the lock (a Codex finding: the model benchmarks below do not cover it).
