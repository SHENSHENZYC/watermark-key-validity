## Ranking and recommendation
**1. C07 — Key-conditional validity of randomised detection tests (N 4, sum 18).** Observed: no retrieved work takes the per-key false-positive rate of a key-randomised detection test as its object. The known neighbours treat different mechanisms: within-text collisions and repeated n-grams (Cai et al. 2510.22007; Fernandez et al. "Three bricks" 2308.00113), many-keys multiplicity (Fu & Russell 2506.15975), marginal-over-key validity and optimal rules (Li, Ruan, Wang, Long, Su, Annals of Statistics; Kuditipudi et al. TMLR 2024), e-process detection (three 2026 papers), and null-text conformal calibration in one applied setting (Xie, Chen, Ren, Su 2507.23113). Inference: the question (how dispersed is the per-key FPR for a fixed human-text distribution, what property of the statistic governs the dispersion, and what do per-key calibration and derandomisation cost in power) is a theory-plus-adequacy contribution with a small method component, and it generalises to fingerprints and copyright traps. Who acts differently: a deployer with one key calibrates on their own null texts instead of trusting the marginal level; detector designers prefer statistics whose per-key FPR concentrates; auditors state guarantees conditionally. Feasibility: the null side is hash-and-count on human corpora (no model); the power side needs a 1.5B model for a few thousand texts (hours). A6 fit: a theory-and-simulation study, a real-null-text study across watermark families and context widths, a remedies study (null-text calibration, derandomised multi-key designs, e-value aggregation), and a second-setting study (fingerprints or the activation-level statistic, labelled as a transfer). **Flag:** this is adjacent to the previous project's lesson of 2026-09-30 (its key-resampling test was exact over keys but not for one key). The candidate is general and its main setting is token-level watermarks, so it is not that project's question, but Yichen asked not to default to that area and should weigh the adjacency. Scoop risk: the Su group is active one step away (2510.22007, 2507.23113, 2602.14286). **Single next check at the gate:** read 2510.22007, 2308.00113, 2512.16904 (appendix), 2507.23113 and 2306.04634 in full and confirm none quantifies per-key dispersion or its determinants.

**2. C05 — Bradley–Terry adequacy on public arena preference data (N 3, sum 16).** Observed: heterogeneity, rank fragility and uncertainty reporting are heavily occupied (2502.14855, 2606.02547, 2605.06656, 2508.11847, 2605.29395), real paired-comparison data is known to depart from BT (Tang, Wang, Jin 2502.10985), and `arenakit` already ships tie modelling, triad counts, intervals and voter concentration. Inference: what remains is a pre-registered specification-test battery (likelihood-ratio tests against a saturated pairwise model; Rao–Kupper against Davidson against half-win ties; triad transitivity against the BT-implied rate; homogeneity across category, language and evaluation session, which the 140k release's `evaluation_session_id` makes testable) propagated into Arena-Rank's closed-form intervals and rank spread, with a labelled winner's-curse arm transferred from Zrnic & Fithian (2411.18569). Who acts differently: arena operators (which model and tie treatment to report) and leaderboard readers (how much rank uncertainty to add). Feasibility: data only (55k Apache-2.0; 100k and 140k prompts CC-BY-4.0, outputs under provider terms, votes usable), minutes of compute. A6 fit: yes. Risk: the novelty falls to 2 if `arenakit`'s case study or 2502.10985 already runs these tests. **Single next check:** read the `arenakit` README and case study, 2502.10985 and 2606.02547 in full, and the lmarena "Statistical extensions" post.

**3. C12 — Group-conditional calibration of zero-shot AI-text detectors (N 3, sum 15).** Observed: conformal FPR bounding for zero-shot detectors exists (Zhu et al. 2505.05084, ACL 2025), group-adaptive thresholds without a guarantee exist (FairOPT 2502.04528), and detector bias by L1 and proficiency is documented on ICNALE (Li & Wan 2502.12611) and in at least five other audits. Inference: the combination (Mondrian conformal per L1 or proficiency group for Binoculars with a 1.5B base–instruct pair and a Fast-DetectGPT substitute; finite-sample per-group FPR; power cost; calibration size at low α; the unknown-group-at-test-time case) was not found, but the method is textbook and the paper would be an applied evaluation at risk of being read as "MCP plus FairOPT on another corpus". Who acts differently: institutions deploying detectors on multilingual writers; detector vendors reporting per-group FPR. Data: ICNALE (research use, no redistribution of texts; CEFR and country labels; 5,600 essays), HC3 (CC BY-SA 4.0), Ghostbuster data (CC BY 3.0), RAID (MIT compilation). **C02 ties C12 at 15 and the script's tie-break puts C02 third; I rank C12 ahead on judgment:** C02's core allocation answer is partly a textbook result (k = 1 samples per prompt is optimal under pure generation cost) and its nearest neighbour (Wang 2512.21326) is closer than C12's.
