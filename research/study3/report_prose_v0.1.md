## 5. Unanticipated observations (labelled; not pre-registered; the conductor's reading of the generated tables)
- **The calibrated-threshold predictor is centred on real text, where Study 2's fixed-threshold predictor was not; the nominal block still shows that bias.** T1 passes with [[t1_consistent]] of [[t1_resolved]] resolved rows consistent, and the eligible ratios have median [[t1_ratio_median_eligible]] (10th–90th percentiles [[t1_ratio_q10]]–[[t1_ratio_q90]]; [[t1_eligible_below1]] point ratios below 1 and [[t1_eligible_above1]] above; Figure 1), against Study 2's resolved median of about 1.2 for the nominal threshold (Study 2 report §2, S1). The nominal rows of this study reproduce that bias on the same texts with fresh keys: the median observed-to-predicted ratio of the nominal SD over the 20 diagonal units is [[rem_h1_raw_nominal_ratio_median]] (raw, h = 1), [[rem_h1_distinct_nominal_ratio_median]] (distinct, h = 1), [[rem_h2_raw_nominal_ratio_median]] (raw, h = 2) and [[rem_h2_distinct_nominal_ratio_median]] (distinct, h = 2; Table 8), while the per-key conformal rows on the same units give [[rem_h1_distinct_conformal1000_ratio_median]] (N_cal = 1,000) and [[rem_h1_distinct_conformal5000_ratio_median]] (5,000) under the distinct rule. A suggestive account, not an attribution: on the diagonal a per-key threshold's level is set by the calibration order statistic, whose law under the model is the Beta term with the finite-text floor, and across laws by the shift difference v, which the exact cross-set covariance identity supplies; the fixed threshold's dispersion depends instead on the tail of the per-text score law at one fixed point, the term Study 2 found under-predicted by the Gaussian residual. The near-identical pair, the positive control, has v ∈ [[ni_v_range]] and ratios [[ni_ratio_range]] ([[ni_consistent]] of [[ni_eligible]] eligible rows consistent; Table 16).
- **Every non-consistent T1 row is under the raw rule, and the raw rule's per-key level is conservative.** The distinct rule has [[t1_distinct_consistent]] of [[t1_distinct_eligible]] eligible rows consistent; the raw rule [[t1_raw_consistent]] of [[t1_raw_eligible]], with its [[t1_fail_n]] exceptions ([[t1_fail_above1_n]] above the band's direction, [[t1_fail_below1_n]] below) concentrated on [[t1_fail_n_tagE]] evaluation cells ([[t1_fail_by_tagE]]; Table 3): the Gutenberg rows ([[t1_fail_h1_n]] of the [[t1_fail_n]] are at h = 1) have the observed SD below the prediction (the ratio ranges by evaluation cell: [[t1_fail_tagE_ranges]]; Table 3), the Arabic Wikipedia rows under Llama-3.2 at h = 2 have it above (the [[t1_inconsistent]] inconsistent rows: [[t1_inconsistent_pairs]]; theory shares [[t1_fail_theory_share_range]], against a minimum of [[t1_consistent_theory_share_min]] among the consistent rows). Neither the lattice account nor the exceptions on those [[t1_fail_n_tagE]] cells are isolated by this study.
- **Every resolved material row agrees with the predicted sign, and the [[t2_unresolved]] unresolved rows are where the nominal baseline's bias meets the material band.** All [[t2_resolved]] resolved material rows agree with the predicted side; the [[t2_unresolved]] unresolved rows ([[t2_fail_pairs]]) are raw-rule h = 2 pairs predicted to hurt with R_pred just above 3/2 and a paired interval that reaches 1; in them both the nominal denominator (observed above its prediction in [[t2_unresolved_nominal_above_n]] of the [[t2_unresolved]]) and the conformal numerator (above its prediction in [[t2_unresolved_conformal_above_n]], below in [[t2_unresolved_conformal_below_n]]) contribute to R_obs/R_pred = (conformal obs/pred)/(nominal obs/pred), so the aggregate medians alone do not isolate the cause. ## 7. Limitations
- Ten corpus cells (four sources, six languages, two lengths cut to L = 256) and two tokenizers from the frozen open partition; one calibration set of 5,000 texts per cell, shared by every key, so the i.i.d. reference law is a model and the per-key statements are conditional on that set; no model-generated text, no watermarked text, no power side; no statement about the held-out partition (`final: false`).
- K = [[K]] evaluation keys per unit, one sample shared across every method, calibration source, rule, family and width of a cell and tokenizer (paired comparisons; a common key-sampling error); the across-key SD carries a relative Monte-Carlo error of 2.2% on the normal benchmark (protocol §8); the crossed intervals are nominal; the rare-key quantiles and the worst sampled key carry their intervals and nothing more (constraint 12).
- The target is conditional on the realised texts, and the finite-evaluation term is part of it: at h = 2 under the distinct rule it is comparable with the shift term, and on the diagonal at N_cal = 5,000 it is [[diag_n5000_floor_share_range]] of the predicted variance (theory shares [[diag_n5000_theory_share_range]]; Beta SD [[diag_conformal5000_h1_distinct_beta_sd]] pp against a floor of [[diag_conformal5000_h1_distinct_floor_range]] pp), enough to fail the pre-stated eligibility floor of 0.75 without being the larger part (Table 6 reports the block descriptively).
- The predictor's structure (one Gaussian shift, one residual variance per set, Gaussian residuals, p read as Φ̄ of the standardised statistic for both families) is an assumption whose adequacy was measured, not derived; the raw rule's conservative level and the exceptions on [[t1_fail_n_tagE]] evaluation cells are described with a suggestive account, not isolated; the h = 0 and α = 0.001 blocks are descriptive and weaker; the nominal baseline's bias (Study 2's S1 median of about 1.2) persists in the nominal rows here (median ratios [[rem_h1_distinct_nominal_ratio_median]] to [[rem_h2_raw_nominal_ratio_median]]; Table 8) and enters T2's error budget.
- "Same law" and "near-identical law" are design labels for cells and pairs, not distributional identities: the calibration sets are uniform permutation scans conditional on the exclusions of protocol §2 (finite pools; the English Wikipedia scans exclude the other English cell's evaluation ids), and the two English Wikipedia cells' evaluation sets share ids.
- The exceedance diagnostic is a reference, not a PAC-over-keys check (protocol §7): the tolerance statement is over calibration draws per key, the measured share carries the finite-evaluation noise, and the screen's B is a dependent count on one shared evaluation set; comparing a measured share with β or δ decides nothing.
- The multiplier arm is numerical under the raw rule (Proposition 1's prerequisite is violated there) and the empirical plug-in is not AutoMark's test; neither is a statement about their guarantee.
- The break-even, the secondary adequacy blocks and the diagonal-against-reference-law table are the builder's computations, labelled descriptive; no rule was decided on them. The results file exceeded the host's file limit and is committed gzipped with its digests in the run manifest (§1); the raw file is regenerable from the record by the locked analyzer.
- Priority and wording: per-key calibration, its pricing and the multiplier are the published record's (AutoMark; Genhatharan 2026; Aremu et al. 2026; Delbianco, Tohmé, Acciarri 2026; Vovk 2012 for training-conditional validity), as are per-language calibration, the failure of nominal thresholds across languages and the pooled-against-per-language comparison (Nemecek et al. 2026) and pooled null recalibration (ChainMark 2026; constraints 13, 14 and 16); this report adds the per-key transfer measurement within and across laws and tokenizers on a grid of frozen open corpora, the out-of-sample adequacy of the key-free predictor, and the paired comparison of the remedies on the same keys and texts; nothing here is a theorem, a transfer guarantee, a power statement or a real-world discrimination claim.
