- **2608.20047, Nemecek, Zafar, Ganguly, Singh, Chaudhary, Ayday, "Auditing Cross-Lingual Fairness in Language Model Watermarking" (v1, 2026-08-20; older work the earlier screens missed).** The study covers six schemes, three open-weight generators, eleven languages in four scripts and eight typological families, and both base and instruction-tuned models. It proposes "detection thresholds calibrated empirically per deployment context" (abstract page), with a threshold-free companion measure that separates calibration failures from detection failures.
  - Section-level reading (§4.1, §5.1, App. C.2): thresholds are calibrated per cell (language × scheme × generator × regime) on matched-prompt unwatermarked outputs. The shipped thresholds assume an IID-token null that cross-lingual text breaks, and in one scheme–language pair the null tail reaches the score cap.
  - No key is varied, and no per-key level is reported. The word "key" appears three times, all in background.
  - No threshold calibrated on one language is carried to another. Searches for English-calibrated, pooled, shared or single thresholds find nothing.
  - **Not an occupant of (a), (c) or (d). It is the closest neighbour of (b)** on the language axis: it shows that the nominal threshold fails across languages and calibrates within each language. It does not measure the transfer of a calibration, and it is marginal over the key.
- **2607.18445, Li-Chen and Kim, "ChainMark: Model-Free LLM Watermarking with Closed-Form Calibration" (v1, 2026-07-20; older, missed).** This is a new keyed scheme with a closed-form false-positive map. On natural text "a one-corpus empirical recalibration restores the 1% target FPR" (abstract page).
  - Section-level reading (§7.2, §7.3, App. D.2): the analytic threshold drifts on real text. The fix re-estimates the null mean and SD on unwatermarked output of the deployed model, or on a domain-matched corpus, pooled across domains.

## Correction (2026-10-15; after R07 round 1, finding F6; the conductor's correction, verified at the primary source)
The section-level reading of **Nemecek et al. 2026 (2608.20047)** above is wrong on one point. §4.1 of the arXiv HTML defines, beside the per-language threshold, "a global threshold τ^global calibrated from the pooled null ⋃_l X⁻_l" that "models cross-lingual deployment with a single detector and is our headline" (the conductor re-read the HTML on 2026-10-15; no PDF downloaded), and Tables 2 and 7 report detection under that global threshold against the per-language recalibration. So the paper does compare a threshold calibrated on a pool of languages with per-language thresholds; the sentence "Searches for English-calibrated, pooled, shared or single thresholds find nothing" is withdrawn. What stands: the study varies no key and reports no per-key level ("key" occurs in background only), so it **does not characterise variation across keys** (the earlier phrase "marginal over the key" overstated what silence shows). It remains the nearest neighbour of the transfer question on the language axis, now as a pooled-versus-per-language calibration comparison; it does not run the ordered per-key transfer design (a threshold set on one law applied to another, per key) or an across-key evaluation. The protocol's §12 and the decision log were corrected on the same date.
